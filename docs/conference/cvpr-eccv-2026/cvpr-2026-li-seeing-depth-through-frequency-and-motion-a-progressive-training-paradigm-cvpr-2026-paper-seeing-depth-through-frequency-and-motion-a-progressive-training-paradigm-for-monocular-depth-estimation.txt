# Abstract 

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation has achieved remarkable progress in recent years, yet frequency alias-ing and the lack of fine-grained cross-frame motion mod-eling still lead to blurred depth boundaries and subop-timal camera motion estimation. To address these chal-lenges, we propose a progressive self-supervised frame-work that integrates a Frequency-Guided Depth Network (FGDepth) and a PoseQuery Network (PQNet). FGDepth incorporates a plug-and-play Frequency-Guided Sampling module that explicitly enhances high-frequency details and suppresses aliasing artifacts, producing depth maps with sharper boundaries. PQNet employs channel-aligned at-tention to model fine-grained cross-frame motion features, enabling more accurate and robust camera motion estima-tion. Furthermore, we design a progressive three-stage de-coupled training strategy that effectively leverages the com-plementarity between depth and pose estimation, further improving overall performance. Extensive experiments on the KITTI benchmark demonstrate state-of-the-art perfor-mance, achieving a 4.1% reduction in Sq Rel over strong baselines, and our method also exhibits excellent cross-dataset generalization on Make3D. Ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of each proposed component. 

# 1. Introduction 

Monocular depth estimation is a core task in 3D scene un-derstanding. Owing to its hardware efficiency, requiring only a single camera to infer spatial geometry, it has be-come a research focus in fields such as autonomous driving and augmented reality. Self-supervised monocular depth es-timation leverages unlabeled monocular videos with image reconstruction as a supervision signal, effectively avoiding the reliance on large-scale annotated datasets and offering great practical value [10, 39, 41]. In this paper, we aim to enhance self-supervised monoc-ular depth estimation by explicitly addressing two criti-cal limitations of existing methods. First, inspired by the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem, we argue that edge blurring and structural degradation in monocular depth es-timation originate from frequency aliasing introduced dur-ing the downsampling operations of encoder-decoder archi-tectures. As illustrated in Fig. 1, high-frequency compo-nents are folded into low-frequency content, they cannot be effectively recovered during subsequent upsampling. Sec-ond, while depth and pose networks are typically trained jointly, their complementary relationship-where accurate pose estimation can reinforce depth prediction and vice versa-remains largely underexploited, leaving the potential of leveraging reliable pose information for improving depth estimation insufficiently explored. These observations mo-tivate us to design a depth network capable of preserving high-frequency structural details and a more robust pose network, together with a tailored training paradigm to fully exploit their complementarity. Consequently, we propose a progressive self-supervised framework for monocular depth estimation. It consists of two core components: (1) FGDepth, a frequency-aware depth network equipped with plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic Frequency-Guided Sampling (FGS) blocks, which explicitly preserve high-frequency details via a high-pass enhancement path and suppress aliasing artifacts through a low-pass filtering path, thereby preserving the semantic integrity of low-frequency structures; and (2) PQNet with Channel-Aligned Attention, which captures fine-grained cross-frame correspondences for robust pose estimation. Furthermore, to fully leverage the complementarity be-tween pose and depth estimation, we introduce a progres-sive three-stage training strategy, theoretically equivalent to Block Coordinate Descent, that decouples and gradually op-timizes the two networks, significantly improving training stability and cross-dataset generalization. Our main contributions can be summarized as: 1. We propose FGDepth with FGS blocks—plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic sampling modules. FGDepth ex-plicitly models dual-path frequency information to re-cover high-frequency details and suppress aliasing arti-facts, thereby improving boundary precision. 2. We design a PQNet with Channel-Aligned Attention and learnable relative positional encoding. This de-sign enables the network to capture fine-grained cross-frame motion cues for more accurate pose estimation, by querying features across adjacent frames to establish geometric correspondences. 3. We introduce a progressive three-stage training strategy that decouples and gradually optimizes depth and pose networks, leveraging their complementary strengths to improve stability and overall performance, achieving a 

3.55% reduction in Sq Rel compared to conventional single-stage joint training. 

# 2. Related Work 

## 2.1. Single-Stage Joint Optimization in MDE 

In self-supervised monocular depth estimation, depth and camera ego-motion are jointly optimized in a single-stage framework from monocular videos, with supervision rooted in image reprojection loss [41]. Building on this method-ology, prior work has advanced the field through key im-provements. Refinements to loss functions have signifi-cantly improved the accuracy of depth estimation and mit-igated challenges like scale ambiguity [10, 24]. Some approaches enhance depth prediction by leveraging auxil-iary modalities, including optical flow and scene seman-tics [14, 20]. Recent efforts have been devoted to develop-ing more elaborate architectural designs for enhanced depth estimation [26, 36, 38, 39]. Despite these improvements, unified joint training frameworks often struggle with depth ambiguity and limited generalization. These limitations can be primarily attributed to two factors: the aliasing of high-frequency information during feature extraction, and the in-herent instability of single-stage joint training strategies. 

## 2.2. Frequency-Aware and Multi-Stage Training in MDE 

Early methods relied on Fourier domain analysis to explic-itly fuse multi-scale predictions [13]. Subsequently, spatial domain constraints, such as edge-aware smoothness losses guided by image gradients, were widely adopted to preserve structural boundaries [10]. More recently, researchers have sought to optimize the photometric loss in high-frequency regions from the perspective of spatial frequency [4]. Un-like computationally heavy global transforms, our method resolves aliasing via efficient spatial filtering, minimizing latency. Although single-stage joint optimization of depth and pose is a common strategy in MDE, its inherent complex-ity and instability have motivated multi-stage training ap-proaches. These include two-stage schemes based on un-certainty modeling [19] or inter-frame consistency [18], and teacher-student architectures [21]. To further improve convergence, recent works also explore finer-grained, self-evolving progressive refinements [5, 15]. However, these approaches often overlook the integration of reliable motion cues, which is crucial for further improving depth accuracy.

Therefore, we propose a progressive three-stage training strategy that decouples the two networks during training and optimizes them under task-specific constraints at different stages, thereby fully exploiting their mutual in-teractions. Stage 3: Fine-tuning the Final FGDepth Network 

We initialize the FGDepth Network by adapting the prior Depth Network-replacing its upsampling blocks with FGS blocks while retaining other pre-trained parameters. Only modules involving FGS blocks are trained here, with the PoseQuery Network (from Stage 2) frozen. This targeted strategy avoids redundant retraining and lever-ages pre-trained weights to focus on recovering struc-tural information-such as edges and textures-from high-frequency cues. This three-stage training strategy, by de-coupling the optimization of depth and pose networks, aligns with the framework of Non-convex Inexact Block Coordinate Descent (BCD) [32]. Under this framework, al-ternating approximate updates ensure stable convergence to stationary points, thus facilitating effective training 1.

## 4.2. Implementation Details 

The experiments were conducted on a 24-core Intel work-station equipped with 64GB of RAM and two NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs, each with 24GB of VRAM. The entire framework was implemented in PyTorch v1.9.0 , and all in-put images were resized to 640 × 192 before being fed into the network. To ensure fair comparison, the learning rate, number of epochs, batch size, and other training hyperpa-rameters were kept consistent with the baseline [39]. 

## 4.3. Evaluation Details 

Since our method performs self-supervised depth estima-tion (predicting relative depth), we adopt the depth estima-tion metrics defined in previous work [6], including Ab-solute Relative Error (Abs Rel), Squared Relative Error (Sq Rel), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Root Mean Squared Log Error (RMSElog), and accuracy within thresh-olds ( δ < 1.25 1, 1.25 2, 1.25 3). For these metrics, lower val-ues indicate Abs Rel, Sq Rel, RMSE, and RMSElog indi-cate better performance, while higher values for the δ met-rics are preferred. It is worth noting that predicted depth is scaled to align with ground truth prior to evaluation, follow-ing standard practice [10]. For pose estimation, we use the 5-frame Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) as the evaluation metric, consistent with previous approaches [41].

# 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we proposed a progressive self-supervised framework for monocular depth estimation, integrating FGDepth with Frequency-Guided Sampling (FGS) block to effectively recover high-frequency structural details while suppressing aliasing-induced artifacts, and a PoseQuery Network (PQNet) with channel-aligned attention to fine-grained cross-frame motion cues. A three-stage decoupled training strategy fully leverages the complementarity be-tween depth and pose estimation, further improving over-all model performance. Extensive experiments on KITTI demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, including a 4.1% 

reduction in Sq Rel over strong baselines. Moreover, our findings reveal a mutually reinforcing relationship between depth and pose networks, suggesting that progressive, de-coupled optimization may represent a promising paradigm for future self-supervised learning in this domain. 26852 Acknowledgments
