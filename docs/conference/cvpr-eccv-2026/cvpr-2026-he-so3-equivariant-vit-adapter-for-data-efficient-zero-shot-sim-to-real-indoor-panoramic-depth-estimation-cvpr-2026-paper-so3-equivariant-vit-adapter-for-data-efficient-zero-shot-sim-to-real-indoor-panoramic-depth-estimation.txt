# Abstract 

Panoramic depth estimation enables a complete 360 ◦

understanding of 3D environments but faces significant chal-lenges in generalizing to real-world scenes. While recent zero-shot depth models like Depth Anything achieve re-markable generalization on perspective images, their per-formance sharply degrades on panoramas due to projection distortions and the lack of spherical geometric awareness. Moreover, collecting large-scale panoramic RGB-D data is costly, hindering the large-scale training of panoramic foundation models. To address these issues, we propose an SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter, which transfers the power-ful zero-shot capability of the perspective pre-trained ViT to panoramic depth estimation by explicitly incorporating a rotation-equivariant inductive bias. Our adapter intro-duces an SO(3) deformable cross-attention mechanism to effectively align SO(3)-equivariant features with perspective features, enhancing rotational consistency without modify-ing the ViT backbone. Trained solely on synthetic panoramas, our framework achieves robust zero-shot sim-to-real perfor-mance on real indoor benchmarks, including Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D, demonstrating both data efficiency and strong generalization for panoramic depth estimation. 

# 1. Introduction 

Zero-shot monocular depth estimation aims to predict dense depth maps in unseen scenes from a single RGB image, significantly reducing the cost of collecting depth annota-tions. For narrow-FoV perspective images, recent zero-shot models such as Marigold [24] and DepthAnything [49, 50] have demonstrated remarkable cross-dataset generalization. Compared with their perspective counterparts, panoramic zero-shot depth estimation provides a complete 360° under-standing of the environment and effectively reduces blind spots. This capability greatly enhances the efficiency and stability of applications such as robotics, AR/VR, and au-

However, panoramic RGB-D data are far more expensive to collect in real scenes than their perspec-tive counterparts due to the need for specialized hardware and complex calibration [4, 43], while vanilla convolution and transformer operators lack rotation-equivariance, leading to structural inconsistency when modeling spherical geom-etry [9, 17]. These challenges make panoramic zero-shot depth estimation a uniquely underexplored yet practically important problem. The success of zero-shot perspective models provides rich transferable priors for depth estimation, thanks to large-scale pre-training and powerful vision transformer backbones. However, transferring such capability to panoramic images remains non-trivial, as it requires addressing challenges in data availability, spherical geometric modeling, and infer-ence performance simultaneously. Balancing these aspects defines the core challenge in transferring zero-shot depth esti-mation from perspective to panoramic images, motivating re-search on data-efficient and geometry-consistent panoramic adaptation. DepthAnywhere [43] adopts a semi-supervised scheme combining labeled real panoramas and pseudo-labeled synthetic data, achieving strong results but at high real data cost. PanDA [4] further removes real depth supervision yet still depends on large-scale real im-ages and a perspective backbone lacking rotation-consistent operators. Despite these advances, existing approaches re-main limited in data efficiency, geometric consistency, or inference performance, highlighting the need for a geometry-consistent adaptation framework for zero-shot panoramic depth estimation without real training data. To this end, we propose an SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter that explicitly introduces operator-level SO(3)-equivariance into a frozen perspective ViT. Specifically, we first design an Equivariant Spherical Prior Module (ESPM) based on spherical CNNs [9] to extract multi-scale SO(3)-equivariant features as geometric priors. These fea-tures are aligned and fused with ViT features in a rotation-equivariant manner via our elaborately developed SO(3) de-formable cross-attention mechanism (SO(3)-DCAM), which avoids discretization artifacts while preserving rotation-equivariance. Finally, a pair of S 2-SO(3) feature interaction component is devised to inject equivariant priors into ViT to improve rotational consistency, while feeding back outputs to enrich the SO(3) features. Our rotation-consistent adaptation framework bridges the geometric discrepancy between perspective and panoramic representations, thereby enabling effective exploitation of the rich depth priors encoded in the pre-trained perspective ViT. This allows our method to leverage its strong sim-to-real generalization capability, achieving strong data efficiency and robust zero-shot panoramic depth estimation. As shown in Fig. 1, in a zero-shot sim-to-real setting, our method seam-lessly integrates data efficiency, geometric consistency and inference performance into a unified and transferable frame-work. The main contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce an SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter that brings rotation-equivariant geometric awareness into the frozen perspective ViT, enabling it to extend reason-ing capabilities to panoramic images without altering the original architecture. • We propose an SO(3)-DCAM to achieve feature-aligned attention on spheres and rotation groups. It bridges dis-parate geometric spaces through sparse SO(3) sampling and spherical contextual relative position encoding, en-suring accurate and rotation-consistent feature fusion. • We show that training exclusively on synthetic panora-mas enables robust sim-to-real generalization, leading to data-efficient and high-fidelity zero-shot inference on real-world panoramic benchmarks. 

# 2. Related Work 

Zero-shot Monocular Perspective Depth Estimation. 

Early monocular depth estimation models pursued zero-shot generalization by jointly training on multiple datasets. To address the performance limitations caused by cross-dataset domain gaps, they adopt affine-invariant losses [3, 31, 32, 53] or explicitly model camera intrinsics [20, 52, 53]. For in-stance, DistDepth [44] achieves zero-shot generalization under a sim-to-real setting, leveraging structured distillation to transfer depth knowledge from synthetic to real indoor scenes. More recently, DepthAnything [49, 50] achieves re-markable zero-shot performance through semi-supervised training on large-scale unlabeled images. Diffusion-based generative approaches [18, 24, 35, 47] fine-tune Stable Diffu-sion for depth estimation, exploiting its rich visual priors to enhance generalization. 

Monocular Panoramic Depth Estimation. Monocu-lar panoramic depth estimation methods achieve strong in-domain performance. Most existing works focus on miti-gating the distortion inherent in equirectangular projection, such as by using geometry-aware convolutions [28, 39], spherical positional encoding [26, 55], projections with less distortion [1, 23, 26, 33, 41, 42], or slice-based pro-jections [30, 37, 54]. With the advances of perspective foundation models, several recent approaches explore zero-shot panoramic depth estimation. 360MonoDepth [33] pre-dicts patchwise tangent depths with MiDaS and fuses them via multi-scale alignment. DepthAnywhere [43] leverages large-scale unlabeled panoramas for semi-supervised dis-tillation, and PanDA [4] further enhances generalization through pseudo-label generation for real-world panoramas and M¨ obius augmentation. Although these approaches trans-fer perspective features to panoramic images, they lack ex-plicit modeling of spherical geometry, limiting the effective use of perspective priors during transfer. 

Group-equivariant Neural Networks. Group-equivariant neural networks leverage equivariant operations to encode symmetries in data, improving robustness under group transformations while reducing the complexity of both models and samples. Representative approaches to group-equivariant modeling include group convolutions on discrete and continuous transformation groups [8–11, 13–17, 38, 40], which inspire later extensions of equivariance within self-attention mechanisms [19, 21]. Our method builds upon Spherical CNNs [9] to extract SO(3)-equivariant spherical features as inputs to the ViT adapter, and is further inspired by LieTransformer [21] in constructing rotation-consistent local neighborhoods within the attention mechanism. 

Structural and Geometric Enhancements of ViT. Struc-tural enhancements in ViTs improve adaptability and spatial reasoning, such as adapter-based frameworks for efficient transfer [6, 7, 29] and deformable attention for flexible key-point sampling [27, 46, 57]. # 3. The Proposed Methodology 

We propose the SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter, an ex-tension of ViT-Adapter [7] that transfers pre-trained per-spective depth knowledge to zero-shot panoramic depth es-timation. By introducing a rotation-equivariant inductive bias, our framework empowers the pre-trained ViT to effec-tively process spherical features for panoramic depth esti-mation. Specifically, we leverage SO(3)-equivariant features extracted from our ESPM to refine the non-equivariant S 2

features produced by the pre-trained ViT on equirectangular panoramas. This rotation-consistent feature injection allows the model to fully exploit the depth priors learned from per-spective pre-training, leading to accurate and data-efficient sim-to-real generalization. The overall architecture of our framework is outlined in Sec. 3.1. Sec. 3.2 describes the ESPM for extracting rotation-equivariant SO(3) features. Sec. 3.3 details the SO(3)-DCAM, and Sec. 3.4 presents the S2-SO(3) feature interaction for fusing non-equivariant and equivariant representations.

3.3. SO(3) Deformable Cross-Attention Module 

Most attention mechanisms in computer vision are de-signed for narrow-FoV perspective images. When applied to spherical representations, they are adversely affected by non-uniform sampling, which distorts global feature match-ing and leads to suboptimal attention weights. Moreover, they generally rely only on feature similarity, without mod-eling the relative positional relationships inherent to the spherical manifold. To address these limitations, we propose an SO(3) deformable cross-attention mechanism (SO(3)-DCAM), which generalizes the deformable attention mod-ule [57] to the spherical representation by introducing two key innovations: (1) an SO(3) Sparse Sampling for key-value features, which fundamentally replaces the 2D sampling points, and (2) a Spherical Contextual Relative Positional Encoding (SCRPE) that incorporates geometric relationships on the sphere during calculating the attention weights.

3.4. S2-SO(3) Feature Interaction 

When directly applied to ERP images, a ViT pre-trained on narrow-FoV perspective images extracts features that can be regarded as non-equivariant on the sphere S 2. While the rich visual priors encoded in the pre-trained model enable it to capture high-quality and generalizable representations, the absence of equivariance renders these features vulnerable to geometric distortions, ultimately limiting the accuracy of depth estimation. To alleviate this limitation, we design two feature interaction modules that bridge the features extracted by ESPM and ViT. Specifically, an SO(3) Feature Injector module injects the equivariant features Fesp on SO(3) into the non-equivariant features Fvit on S 2, thereby improving their rotational symmetry. In parallel, a Multi-Scale Feature Extractor module feeds the integrated features back from the ViT to enrich the equivariant features Fesp with depth-aware priors learned from large-scale perspective data.

3.5. Loss Function 

We employ the BerHu loss [25] to supervise depth regres-sion, which combines the advantages of ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms for stable and robust optimization. It behaves as an ℓ1 loss for small residuals and transitions to an ℓ2 loss for larger errors, effectively balancing accuracy and smoothness. This loss is widely used in depth estimation and provides stable convergence in training our panoramic depth network.

4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods 

Under the strict zero-shot sim-to-real protocol described above, we compare our proposed SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter with both perspective and panoramic zero-shot depth estimation methods. This setting prohibits the use of any real panoramic data during training, whether labeled or unlabeled, ensuring that all approaches are evaluated un-der the same data constraints. To ensure a fair compari-son, we reproduce or modify existing methods according to their original training schemes, while strictly maintain-ing the no-real-data constraint. # 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we introduce an SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter that enhances the rotation consistency in pre-trained perspective ViTs for panoramic depth estimation. Especially, by incorpating our elaborately designed SO(3)-DCAM, our framework aligns features on spheres and rotation groups in a rotation-equivariant manner, enabling effective adap-tation using only synthetic panoramic data. Experiments under the zero-shot sim-to-real settings demonstrate that this operator-level innovation provides robust zero-shot gener-alization to real-world scenes and significantly improves rotation-consistent geometric reasoning for panoramas. We believe this study offers a principled step toward more gen-eralizable and geometry-consistent transformer architectures for spherical vision, thereby advancing the generalization capability of panoramic depth estimation tasks. 5747 6.
