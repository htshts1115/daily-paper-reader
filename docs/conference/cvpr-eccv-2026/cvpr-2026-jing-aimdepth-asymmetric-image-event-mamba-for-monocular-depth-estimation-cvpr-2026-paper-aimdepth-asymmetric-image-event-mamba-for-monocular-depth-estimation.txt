## Abstract 

Monocular depth estimation is essential for applications such as robotics. The complementary characteristics of event and image modalities have inspired fusion-based methods for robust depth estimation. However, existing methods rely on convolutional or attention-based architec-tures, which either have limited capacity for long-range modeling or incur high computational cost, making them less suitable for depth estimation over long sequences. Moreover, effective image-event fusion remains challeng-ing, since most methods directly fuse features without ad-dressing the domain gap and representational differences between raw events and images, resulting in semantic bias and degraded performance. In this work, we propose AIMDepth, an Asymmetric Image-Event Mamba framework for monocular depth estimation, built on state space models for linear complexity and accurate prediction. To alleviate input-domain misalignment, we introduce a Spectral Cross-modal Prior Guidance (SCPG) for bidirectional prior in-jection at the input level. To reduce the imbalance between sparse events and dense images, we design an asymmetric modal-aware Encoder (AME) with separate encoding paths and feature-level alignment. We further develop a Modality-interactive Local Refinement (ModiLocal) to enable hierar-chical interaction and fine-grained alignment. Experiments on public datasets show that AIMDepth achieves state-of-the-art performance in complex environments.

We then introduce an Asymmetric Modal-aware Encoder (AME), which enables modality-specific representation learning by selectively utilizing encoder features from different levels for events and images. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • We propose Spectral Cross-modal Prior Guidance module for input-level alignment and Asymmetric Modal-aware Encoder for feature-level alignment, effectively reducing modality gaps between events and images. • We propose Modality-interactive Local Refinement mod-ule for progressive cross-modal fusion through hierarchi-cal interaction and fine-grained spatial refinement, en-hancing both global consistency and local detail modeling within the network. • We evaluate our method on public datasets that include both simulated and real-world scenarios, achieving state-of-the-art performance and demonstrating strong robust-ness in complex environments. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each proposed module. 

## 2. Related Work 

Event-image fusion for monocular depth estimation has emerged as a promising direction, leveraging the dense spa-tial cues of image data and the high temporal resolution of event data. Existing fusion architectures mainly rely on CNNs or Transformers. CNN-based models have been widely adopted for this task due to their efficiency in cap-turing local spatial features. RAMNet [6] employs a recur-rent asynchronous multimodal network with ConvGRU lay-ers to extract and fuse temporal-aware features from events and images, while SRFNet [19] introduces an interactive attention-based fusion module that merges event and image features while learning spatial reliability priors. However, their limited receptive fields hinder global context model-ing, making them less effective in complex scenes with oc-clusions or large depth variations. Transformer-based methods capture long-range depen-dencies through self-attention. ER-F2D [3] uses unified at-tention blocks as encoder to model cross-modal relations. Nevertheless, the quadratic computational complexity of at-tention limits their scalability. Recent hybrid architectures have been proposed to balance local and global feature ex-traction. UniCT Depth [12] integrates CNNs and trans-formers with a dual self-attention block to enhance cross-modal spatial and channel interactions. HMNet [9] com-bines multi-level latent memories with event-specific atten-tion mechanisms for adaptive scene processing. Despite their effectiveness, these methods fuse modalities without accounting for the inherent domain gap between images and events. This can compromise semantic consistency during feature extraction and hinder effective cross-modal fusion. Recently, Mamba has demonstrated strong potential in event-based vision due to its ability to model global context with linear computational complexity [5, 25, 35]. However, 8034 its application to event-image fusion for depth estimation remains underexplored. In this work, we propose a Mamba-based framework for event-image fusion depth estimation. Unlike previous approaches that perform feature fusion without explicitly addressing modality alignment, we de-sign an input-level alignment SCPG module and feature-level alignment Asymmetric Modal-aware Encoder. In ad-dition, we design a tailored Modality-interactive Local Re-finement module to facilitate modality fusion, thereby fur-ther enhancing the overall performance of the network. 

## 3. Preliminaries 

3.1. State Space Models (SSMs) 

State Space Models (SSMs) are a class of sequence-to-sequence models inspired by linear time-invariant (LTI) systems. These models map an input sequence x(t) ∈ R

to an output sequence y(t) ∈ R through an intermediate la-tent state h(t) ∈ RN . The dynamics of SSMs are governed by the following continuous-time equations: 

˙h(t) = Ah (t) + Bx (t), y(t) = Ch (t) + Dx (t), (1) where A ∈ RN ×N , B ∈ RN ×1, C ∈ R1×N , and D ∈ R are the system matrices. The term Dx (t) represents a residual connection from input to output. To handle discrete sequences, SSMs employ zero-order hold discretization [8], converting continuous-time param-eters (A, B ) into discrete counterparts (A, B ) using a timescale parameter ∆ ∈ R > 0 : A = e∆A, B =(∆ A)−1(e∆A − I) · ∆B, C = C, D = D. The discretized model is then expressed as follows: 

ht = Ah t−1 + Bx t, yt = Ch t + Dx t. (2) 

3.2. Selective Scan Mechanism 

Traditional SSMs face limitations due to their static parame-terization, which restricts their ability to adapt to varying in-put contexts. Selective State Space Model (S6), or Mamba [7], introduces input-dependent dynamics. In Mamba, the matrices B ∈ RL×N , C ∈ RL×N , and ∆ ∈ RL×D are dynamically generated from the input sequence x ∈ RL×D .This allows the model to selectively focus on relevant parts of the sequence, enhancing its capacity to capture long-range dependencies while maintaining computational effi-ciency. Recently, VMamba [17] and Vim [34] addressed directional bias through bidirectional and cross-scan strate-gies, achieving strong results in vision tasks. 

## 4. Method 

Our proposed AIMDepth adopts a U-Net-like architec-ture, consisting of four key components: Spectral Cross-Modal Prior Guidance, Asymmetric Modal-aware Encoder, Modality-Interactive Local Refinement, and a Mamba-based Decoder. As shown in Fig. 2, for the input event stream, AIMDepth first applies bilinear interpolation to convert the raw event voxel grid Eraw into an image-like representation E ∈ RK×H×W [32], where K denotes the number of temporal bins used to discretize the event stream. This representation is synchronized with the input image 

I ∈ RC×H×W . The paired inputs E and I are processed by the SCPG module to obtain the input-aligned modali-ties ˜E and ˜I. These aligned modalities are subsequently processed by the Asymmetric Modal-aware Encoder, which comprises four shared encoding stages and generates multi-level features FE and FI through modality-specific selec-tion strategies. The extracted features are then fused via the Modality-Interactive Local Refinement module, producing an integrated representation Ffused . This fused feature is fed into a lightweight Mamba-based decoder, which progres-sively upsamples Ffused to reconstruct the spatial resolution. Finally, a convolutional output layer is applied to produce the final dense depth map prediction. 

4.1. Spectral Cross-Modal Prior Guidance 

To address the inherent representation gap between static images and event streams, we introduce the Spectral Cross-modal Prior Guidance module, which aligns the two modal-ities at the input level by leveraging their complementary frequency-domain characteristics. SCPG consists of two complementary modules, Event-targeted Amplitude Trans-fer (EAT) and Image-targeted Phase Enhancement (IPE). EAT transfers structural priors from images to events, while IPE injects dynamic cues from events into images. SCPG establishes a bidirectional alignment that facilitates consis-tent cross-modal representation learning. Notably, by op-erating directly in the frequency domain, SCPG provides a natural and interpretable form of input-level alignment that aligns well with the Mamba architecture.

4.2. Asymmetric Modal-Aware Encoder 

We introduce an asymmetric modal-aware encoder to align the feature representations of image and event modalities. This asymmetric strategy is grounded in the inherent differ-ences between the two modalities in terms of information density and semantic structure: the image modality is dense and rich in texture, making it suitable for capturing local edges and spatial details through shallow network layers; in 8036 contrast, the event modality is sparse and encodes dynamic changes, which are better modeled by deeper layers that ex-tract abstract spatiotemporal semantics. Based on this ob-servation, the asymmetric design enables modality-specific feature enhancement within a shared architecture, thereby promoting alignment in the feature space. As shown in Figure 2, we construct the weight-shared encoder using four stages of Visual State Space (VSS) Blocks [17], where each block incorporates a Selective Scan 2D (SS2D) module to capture global spatial depen-dencies. Downsampling operations are inserted between stages, which halves the spatial resolution while doubling the channel dimension. This forms a hierarchical multi-scale representation from low to high semantic levels. Local Spatial Selective Scan (LSS) To better capture fine-grained spatial details essential for dense depth esti-mation, we introduce Local Spatial Selective Scan. Unlike SS2D which performs global scanning in four directions (see in Fig. 2(b)), this module constructs overlapping local windows and applies directional state propagation within each window. The scanned features are then processed by a selective scan module and merged back into the main representation. This design captures both local variations and broader spatial context, refining geometric precision and enhancing seman-tic consistency across fusion stages. 

4.4. Decoder 

We adopt the Channel-Aware Visual State Space (CVSS) Block [27] as our decoder to reconstruct the final depth map. CVSS Block enhances spatial modeling with channel-wise attention for robust and accurate decoding. Specif-ically, given the input feature Ff use ∈ RB×C×H×W ,the CVSS Block first applies a VSS Block as Fs =

VSS (Ff use ). The output is normalized and convolved to obtain: Fc = Conv (LN (Fs)) . Channel descriptors are com-puted via global pooling as favg = AvgPool (Fc), fmax =

MaxPool (Fc) These are combined to produce attention weights α = σ(favg + fmax ). Then, the attended feature is Fattn = α · Fc Finally, two residual connections are added to form the output: Fout = Fs + Fattn This design combines global context modeling and channel selection, improving reconstruction quality with minimal overhead.

## 5. Experiments 

5.1. Datasets and Evaluation Protocols 

Datasets and Metrics. Following previous work, we evaluated our method on public event-based datasets: the real-world MVSEC and the synthetic DENSE. MVSEC [31] provides event streams and grayscale images from out-door driving scenes. We train on the Outdoor day2 sequence and test on Outdoor day1 and Outdoor night1 to assess ro-bustness under challenging conditions. DENSE [11] of-fers high-quality depth maps, event data, and RGB images. We use Towns 01-05 for training and Town 10 for test-ing, leveraging its clean and well-aligned annotations. We adopt three commonly used evaluation metrics in monoc-ular depth estimation: absolute relative error (A), logarith-mic root mean squared error (R), and accuracy thresholds 

δn < 1.25 n, where n = 1 , 2, 3 (δ1, δ 2, δ 3). 

Implement Details. Our model is implemented using the PyTorch framework. We employ the AdamW optimizer with a weight decay of 0.8 and a learning rate of 2 × 10 −4.The model is trained for 30 epochs with a batch size of 16. Two key hyperparameters are set as follows: the voxel grid channel B = 5 and the low-frequency ratio β = 0 .01 . All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090.

## 6. Conclusion 

In this paper, we proposed AIMDepth, a novel Mamba-based framework for monocular depth estimation that lever-ages the complementary strengths of event and image modalities. To address the challenges of cross-modal fu-sion, we introduce Spectral Cross-modal Prior Guidance (SCGP) for input-level alignment, an Asymmetric Modal-aware Encoder (AME) for feature-level alignment, and a Modality-interactive Local Refinement module (ModiLo-cal) to perform hierarchical fusion and fine-grained align-ment. Built on state space models, our proposed AIMDepth enables efficient sequence modeling with linear complex-ity. Experiments on public datasets, covering both simu-lated and real-world scenarios, demonstrate that AIMDepth achieves state-of-the-art performance and exhibits strong robustness under challenge. These results highlight the po-tential of state space modeling in event-image tasks. 8040 Acknowledgments
