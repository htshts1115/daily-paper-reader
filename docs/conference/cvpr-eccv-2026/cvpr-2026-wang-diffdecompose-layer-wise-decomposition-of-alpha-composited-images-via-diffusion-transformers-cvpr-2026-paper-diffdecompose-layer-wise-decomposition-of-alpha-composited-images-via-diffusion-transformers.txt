Abstract
Existing image decomposition methods struggle to disen-
tangle semi-transparent or transparent layer occlusions
due to mask prior dependencies, static object assump-
tions, and the lack of datasets.
In this paper, we intro-
duce a novel task: Layer-Wise Decomposition of Alpha-
Composited Images, aiming to recover constituent layers
from single overlapped images under the condition of semi-
transparent/transparent alpha layer non-linear occlusion.
To address challenges in layer ambiguity and data scarcity,
we first introduce AlphaBlend, the first large-scale and
high-quality dataset for transparent and semi-transparent
layer decomposition, supporting six real-world subtasks
(e.g., translucent flare removal, semi-transparent cell de-
composition, glassware decomposition, X-ray contraband
decomposition).
Building on this dataset, we present
DiffDecompose, a diffusion Transformer-based framework

that learns the posterior over possible layer decomposi-
tions conditioned on the input image, semantic prompts,
and blending type. Rather than regressing alpha mattes di-

1. Introduction
With the development of diffusion models, image-to-image
[13, 19, 22, 36, 55, 73] and text-to-image [4, 6, 15, 30] gen-
eration methods have been extensively studied and leads to
a wide applications [3, 8, 17, 38, 44, 47, 48, 51, 54, 57, 64].
Among these approaches, layer-wise decomposition is par-
ticularly advantageous for structured image factorization:

the objective is to decompose the image into its constituent
layers, each containing the foreground object, its associ-
ated alpha matte, and potential depth ordering. This fac-
torization affords fine-grained control over individual layers
and, in turn, facilitates creative tasks such as video genera-
tion [1, 3, 9, 16, 26] and scene understanding [58, 59].
While this method offers controllable layer capabilities,
these methods still face two major challenges in semi-
transparent/transparent scenes, as shown in Figure 1 (a):
(1) Limitation of layer-level image inpainting.
While
mask-based methods [14, 24, 33, 62] can accurately handle
object-level information, they completely fail when faces
with scenes requiring a whole image-level mask like the in-
put in subtasks I, II, and III. (2) Limitation of tackling non-
linear layer with diverse contents and complex struc-
tures. Prior works [57, 64] largely target opaque/solid ob-
jects, reducing composition to pixel overlap and overlook-
ing nonlinear mixing (e.g., transparent glassware or semi-
transparent cells). These two drawbacks seriously hinder
the development of image editing.
To address these novel challenges, we present the first
task for layer-wise decomposition of Alpha-Composited
Images (LDAC). As shown in the Figure 1, LDAC aims to
recover a background layer and an alpha-foreground layer
directly from a single composite, without any mask pri-
ors. Unlike the image inpainting task, LDAC is intrinsi-
cally ill-posed: a single alpha composite typically admits
multiple plausible factorizations due to the entanglement of
color and transparency across layers, which substantially
complicates faithful scene reconstruction. Therefore, this
proposed task faced two main challenges not addressed in
prior works: (1) Layer ambiguity and coupling of color
and transparency: Foreground and background occupy the
same visual plane and lack the separability via depth or
edge contrast, thereby introducing significant layer ambigu-
ity. (2) Lack of large-scale dataset: This field lacks relevant
datasets. Current datasets [48, 57] are often generated from
AI, and the final overlapped images tend to have a pixel-
level difference from the initial foreground and background,
resulting in the failure of layer-wise decomposition.
Motivated by the above facts, we propose the first large-
scale, high-quality dataset, named AlphaBlend, to fill the
gaps in the understanding of this task. As shown in Figure
1 (b), AlphaBlend has two characteristics: Firstly, it is the
first dataset to cover six semi-transparent/transparent sce-
narios, with careful construction to capture nonlinear com-
positional behavior, foreground–background entanglement,
and alpha-induced visual ambiguity. Secondly, it spans di-
verse styles and content (e.g., security, glass replacement,
and cell separation, etc.), and each domain contains around
5,000-10,000 training images and 300-500 test images.
Based on the novel task and the AlphaBlend dataset, we
propose a diffusion Transformer-based framework DiffDe-
compose to probabilistically infer multiple plausible layer
decompositions conditioned on context, which has two
novel technical designs:
(1) Rather than explicitly re-
gressing alpha mattes, we reformulate the task as learning
the posterior distribution over compositional layers, condi-
tioned on the observed image, semantic prompts, and blend-
ing type. (2) We design In-Context Decomposition (ICD) to
enable the model to effectively predict single-layer or multi-
layer results under given alpha-composited image condi-
tions, which across various alpha compositional contexts
without explicit supervision for each individual layer, and
also devise Layer Position Encoding Cloning (LPEC) to
preserve the pixel-level correspondence, therefore mitigat-
ing inter-layer entanglement during decomposition.
Our main contributions are four-fold:
• We present a novel generative task, namely LDAC, for
the semi-transparent/transparent scenarios, and reformu-
late this task from a novel perspective as a probabilistic
problem of generating plausible layer decompositions.
• We introduce the AlphaBlend dataset, the first large-scale,
high-quality dataset that supports six LDAC subtasks,
i.e., translucent flare removal, translucent occlusion re-
moval, semi-translucent watermark removal, transparent
glassware decomposition, semi-transparent cell decom-
position, X-ray contraband decomposition.
• We propose a DiT-based framework, namely DiffDecom-
pose, for the LDAC task. We present ICD to realize layer-
to-layer decomposition and LPEC strategy to avoid ambi-
guity and coupling of decomposed layers.
• Extensive experiments on the proposed AlphaBlend
dataset and public LOGO Benchmarks verify the supe-
riority and effectiveness of DiffDecompose compared to
state-of-the-art (SOTA) approaches.
2. Related Work
Diffusion Model (DMs) [37, 39] have significantly ad-
vanced image generation and editing, surpassing GANs
[10, 28, 28, 29, 32] in tasks such as customized genera-
tion [67, 68], video generation [41, 49, 53] and control-
lable editing [21, 61, 69, 70].
Recent extensions have
explored applications in 3D modeling [27] and scene-
consistent generation [31], showcasing the broad gener-
ative capacity of diffusion models.
Despite these ad-
vances, diffusion models remain an open and ill-posed
challenge, particularly in scenarios involving transparency,
semi-transparency, and nonlinear blending. In this work,
we present a novel generative task, namely Layer-Wise De-
composition of Alpha-Composited Images, and propose a
diffusion-based framework DiffDecompose, to decompose
complex semi-transparent/transparent layer-level compos-
ited images into plausible foreground and background lay-
ers. To support this novel task, we construct a first, large-
scale, and high-quality dataset AlphaBlend, which mainly
4625


contains layer-level semi-transparent or transparent images.
Controllable Generation.
Recent advances in control-
lable diffusion models [33, 34, 50, 60, 65] have signifi-
cantly improved image editing, particularly in inpainting.
Existing methods fall into text-based and mask-based cat-
egories.
Text-based approaches [12, 54, 63, 74] depend
heavily on precise prompts and degrade when instructions
are ambiguous [56]. Mask-based approaches [14, 33, 62]
maintain background structure but require accurate masks
and iterative denoising, which weakens conditioning in
complex transparency scenarios. Motivated by noise-free
conditioning in OminiControl [45] and subsequent meth-
ods [18, 20, 42, 43, 71], we reformulate object occlusion
[14, 44, 63] and layer decomposition [48, 57] as a prob-
abilistic generation problem, modeling the posterior over
decomposed layers given a composite image. This allows
our framework to handle not only object-level and channel-
level occlusions but also the more challenging layer-level
semi-transparent cases.
In-Context Learning is initially developed in large lan-
guage models [5, 72], and recently has been extended to
vision tasks such as object editing and region manipulation
[2, 7, 23, 40, 43, 66, 72]. While prior works leverage con-
textual cues for object insertion or style transfer, none have
addressed layer-wise decomposition, particularly in semi-
transparent or transparent settings. In this paper, we intro-
duce ICD, which enables the model to infer multiple com-
posited layers based on contextual prompts without explicit
supervision. Furthermore, we propose LPEC to preserve
spatial alignment and reinforce information consistency be-
tween the decomposed layers and the original image.
3. AlphaBlend Dataset Construction

natural Red-Green-Blue (RGB) background, which can be

a task-specific blending operator (e.g., alpha blending or
screen mode). It consists of six subtasks (Note that we com-
bine Subtasks V and VI into one description):

the physical property of X-ray in synthetic data, and formu-

Framework Overview. As shown in Figure 3, the overview
architecture of DiffDecompose can be divided into two
steps: Firstly, finetuning the AlphaVAE [52] to obtain the
ability to extract RGBA features with our proposed Alph-
aBlend dataset. Then, utilizing the pretrained AlphaVAE to
respectively encode a semi-transparent/transparent RGBA
foreground x, a common RGB background y, and a com-
posited alpha-blend RGBA image z, so as to obtain their
latent spatial-level features. In detail, the prompts are fed
into the frozen T5XXL [35] to extract the text-level fea-
tures. The process of decomposition is realized by ICD
by concatenating the clean condition token of z and noise
tokens of x and y, and utilizing the bidirectional attention
to achieve conditional generation. During the process of
ICD, the proposed LPEC strategy achieves consistency be-
tween the specific layer and the original layer information,
and then utilizes the MMA for the interaction between dif-
ferent modalities. During the inference stage, an observed
image z is directly passed into the DiffDecompose model to
obtain its corresponding decomposed images x and y.
4.2. In-Context Decomposition
Since the disentanglement of alpha-composited images is
non-linear, directly learning the alpha maps is too difficult
for decomposition. Thus, we develop an ICD to process
layer decomposition as a context-aware spatial separation
problem, where the model leverages both visual latent rep-
resentations fx, fy, and fz, and the textual latent repre-
sentations ft to infer the spatial organization and semantic
consistency of foreground and background components. To
successfully realize this process, we develop the LPEC and
introduce Multi-modality Attention (MMA) [15] to achieve
the process of in-context decomposition.
Layer Position Encoding Cloning. To enforce spatial co-
herence across composited layers and enhance the disentan-
glement of foreground x and background y during genera-
tion, we propose the LPEC mechanism. Specifically, we
generate two positional encodings PE and PE′ from the
composited image z, and inject them into the token repre-
sentations cx, cy and cz of x, y and z:

5. Experiments
5.1. Experimental Settings
Implementation Details.
During finetuning the Al-
phaVAE, we take the parameters of the AlphaVAE model
as the initialization, and train it with the foreground of the
AlphaBlend dataset for 30,000 steps. After that, we intro-
duce LoRA [15] to finetune the Flux [25] architecture with
whole AlphaBlend, under the rank of 128, batch size of 1,
and a learning rate of 10−4 on an H20 GPU for 30,000 steps.
Benchmark.
To provide a rigorous and comprehensive
evaluation, we evaluate our DiffDecompose and baselines
on two datasets: our proposed AlphaBlend dataset and the
public LOGO dataset [11].
For the AlphaBlend dataset
(described in Sec . 3), each LDAC subtask provides 500
test images for evaluation. For LOGO evaluation, we uti-
lize its three test benchmarks (i.e., LOGO-H, LOGO-L, and
LOGO-G). To ensure fair comparison and reduce inconsis-
tencies or boundary artifacts, we apply mask dilation for
five iterations with a 5×5 kernel before inpainting.

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a novel task, Layer-Wise
Decomposition of Alpha-Composited Images (LDAC)
We construct a large-scale and high-quality AlphaBlend
dataset that contains multiple layer-level transparent/semi-
transparent foreground alpha-blend images, supporting six
real-world subtasks. To cope with this complex task, we
adopt a novel point that redefines the problem as learning
a posterior over potential layer configurations conditioned
on contextual cues. In the process of training, we propose a
transformer diffusion-based framework termed DiffDecom-
pose, to effectively achieve layer-by-layer decomposition
under given alpha-composited image as condition through
ICD. Furthermore, we also devise the LPEC for the pro-
cess of ICD to effectively align the information between a
certain layer and the original layer. Extensive experiments
verify the effectiveness of LDAC task.
