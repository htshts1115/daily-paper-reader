# Yusheng Li Lizhi Lou Yan Tang Zekai Miao Shaoming Zhang † Jianmei Wang College of Surveying and Geo-Infomatics, Tongji University 

# Abstract 

Metric depth estimation aims to recover depth maps with absolute scale, high resolution, and cross-scene consis-tency from visual observations. Existing approaches ei-ther rely on large-scale models or costly sensors to pre-serve metric accuracy and generalization, both ill-suited to resource-constrained deployment. In this paper, we propose 

LiteSense , a lightweight RGB-ToF fusion framework that leverages compact normalized histogram (CNH) signals to-gether with RGB cues to achieve efficient and reliable met-ric depth estimation. Specifically, LiteSense leverages a U-Net-style encoder-decoder that forms an RGB-D input by concatenating RGB with upsampled ToF depth, providing explicit metric priors. To address resolution disparity and recover fine details, we introduce the Patch-wise CNH Spa-tial Injection (PCSI) module, which leverages zone-wise histogram measurements via cross-attention to guide high-level feature fusion. Extensively evaluated on NYUv2 and SUN RGB-D, LiteSense consistently outperforms monocu-lar baselines and DELTAR with substantially lower compu-tational cost, and demonstrates promising zero-shot gener-alization. We further introduce THDR3K , the first indoor RGB-ToF-CNH dataset, where LiteSense achieves real-world accuracy comparable to—and in challenging cases surpassing—Intel RealSense. All the relevant source codes and the collected dataset are available at GitHub.

Its goal is to reconstruct depth maps from visual observa-tions that preserve absolute scale, achieve high spatial reso-lution, and remain consistent across diverse scenes. Concurrently, monocular depth estimation methods based on deep learning, such as MiDaS [35], ZoeDepth [5], Metric3D [16, 54], and Depth Anything [52, 53], leverage large-scale, multi-domain train-ing data to achieve high accuracy and robust cross-scene generalization. However, both approaches exhibit inherent limitations: (1) lightweight ToF sensors typically provide only low-resolution measurements ( e.g ., 8 × 8), which are insufficient to reconstruct dense scene geometry on their own; (2) monocular models, in the absence of explicit met-ric supervision, struggle to reliably recover a stable and ab-solute scale. These observations naturally motivate a hy-brid paradigm that integrates the absolute depth priors from lightweight ToF sensors with the high-resolution texture in-formation from RGB images, thereby enabling efficient and stable metric depth estimation. Representative efforts that incorporate sensor priors for metric depth estimation include: (1) constraining the scale of monocular networks using sparse LiDAR point clouds as prompts or prior-feature embeddings [29, 48], and (2) guiding fusion with lightweight ToF measurements by inte-grating depth and variance maps with RGB features via at-tention [22]. While demonstrating improved scale discrim-inability and cross-scene robustness, these methods suffer from high computational overhead and detail loss due to resolution mismatch. To address these issues, we propose 

LiteSense , an RGB-ToF fusion framework guided by CNH data, as illustrated in Fig. 1. LiteSense adopts a U-Net-style [38] encoder–decoder ar-chitecture for dense depth prediction. In contrast to Li-DAR, which provides sparse discrete points, a lightweight ToF sensor outputs a low-resolution but spatially continu-ous depth map. This map is upsampled and concatenated with the RGB image to form an RGB-D input, providing an explicit metric prior. However, the significant resolution disparity between ToF and RGB modalities makes the up-sampled depth map alone insufficient to recover fine struc-tural details. To mitigate this issue, CNH measurements from the ToF sensor are incorporated. For each zone, the CNH yields a normalized return histogram, depicting sig-nal counts versus range, which summarizes the within-zone depth distribution. Exploiting the zone-wise nature of CNH and its block-wise alignment with RGB-D data, we introduce the patch-wise CNH spatial injection (PCSI) module, inspired by tile-based super-resolution [25, 26, 28, 30]. Within each local patch, PCSI projects the aligned CNH features and in-tegrates them into the RGB-D features via cross-attention to obtain locally fused representations. Subsequently, the per-patch outputs are stitched together and aggregated on a tile-wise basis to form comprehensive fusion features. No-tably, the PCSI module operates exclusively at high-level features, thereby preserving the integrity of early-stage rep-resentations and mitigating potential tiling artifacts. We extensively evaluate LiteSense on NYUv2 and SUN RGB-D. Our method consistently outperforms monocular baselines and DELTAR [22] while incurring substantially lower computational cost, and it demonstrates promising zero-shot generalization capabilities. To further assess its real-world applicability, we construct THDR3K , an indoor RGB-ToF dataset with CNH measurements. Experimen-tal results indicate that LiteSense produces depth estimates comparable to, and in challenging cases even surpassing, those obtained from Intel RealSense. The main contribu-tions of our work are as follows: • We propose LiteSense, a lightweight RGB-ToF fusion framework that leverages CNH signals together with RGB cues to deliver high-resolution and accurate depth. • We are the first to introduce CNH into depth estimation and design the PCSI module to exploit their zone-wise statistics for effective region-wise RGB-D fusion. • We construct THDR3K, the first indoor dataset that jointly provides RGB images, lightweight ToF depth, and CNH signals to support research on RGB-ToF fusion for metric depth estimation. 

# 2. Related Work 

Metric Monocular Depth Estimation (MMDE) MMDE aims to recover pixel-level metric depth from a single RGB image. Early supervised methods generally adopt an encoder–decoder framework to directly regress depth [9, 11, 18, 20, 21, 24, 36, 51, 55]. Later works further reformulate depth regression as adaptive depth distribution estimation to enhance performance [1, 3, 4, 10, 27], yet accuracy still tends to degrade on unseen data. To address this limitation, recent research increasingly shifts toward self-supervised relative depth estimation, us-ing large-scale datasets to improve robustness across di-verse scenes [6, 23, 35, 52, 53]. These models demon-strate strong zero-shot performance, but still require either fine-tuning [5, 13] or the incorporation of camera intrin-sics [7, 12, 16, 32, 54] to achieve high accuracy in metric depth. Moreover, such models usually involve high com-plexity, making real-time inference difficult. Even some lightweight networks have been explored [49, 56], they are generally less effective in preserving metric scale. 

Auxiliary-Guided Depth Estimation In many 3D appli-cations requiring metric scale, active depth sensors remain the default for reliable measurements [17], but obtaining dense depth is costly. Recent work therefore augments monocular depth estimation with sparse sensor cues. A stream of research utilizes sparse LiDAR depth, where LiDAR measurements are treated as prompts [29, 31] or embedded as features [48] into depth foundation mod-els [52, 53]. # 3. Method 

We propose LiteSense, a lightweight framework as de-picted in Fig. 2. It integrates high-resolution RGB im-ages with low-resolution ToF data to enable end-to-end, high-resolution metric depth estimation. The framework begins with specialized feature extractors for RGB-D cues and CNH distribution features, followed by the proposed PCSI module, which performs localized cross-modal fusion within their corresponding spatial regions. Finally, a multi-stage decoder progressively reconstructs the fused repre-sentation into a dense depth map within the spatial region jointly covered by the camera and ToF sensor. 

3.1. Leveraging ToF Auxiliary 

A key challenge in this task is how to fully exploit the information contained in ToF measurements. In the DELTAR [22], the mean and variance of the depth are used to approximate a single Gaussian distribution. Sub-sequently, a fixed sampling strategy is applied to obtain a set of discrete depth values around the distribution peak, thereby utilizing the auxiliary information. Although this design enriches the representation beyond a single depth value, it inevitably discards much of the distributional de-tail present in the raw sensor output. This motivates us to instead leverage the CNH data, a highly structured repre-sentation produced directly by the sensor firmware, which compactly preserves the underlying depth distribution and allows the model to better exploit the full auxiliary infor-mation provided by the ToF sensor. However, designing a unified representation that jointly encodes both depth values and histogram distributions in a form suitable for network learning is non-trivial. Several intuitive approaches, such as depth-weighted histogram ag-gregation or Gaussian mixture fitting, fail to preserve the physical interpretability of the raw signals. Therefore, we choose to represent the depth value and the CNH distribu-tion separately. Considering the inherent spatial correlation in depth data, the ToF depth map is first upsampled and con-catenated with the RGB image to form an RGB-D input, where the depth channel provides an initial scale prior. And for the CNH, its information is extracted independently and injected into higher-dimensional feature spaces to provide complementary guidance during subsequent processing. 5785 3.2. Hybrid Feature Extraction 

Spatial Block From the perspective of lightweight de-sign, we adopt and modify the MobileNetV4 [33] architec-ture, which offers strong efficiency performance trade-offs, to extract features from the RGB-D input. Specifically, we utilize four downsampling blocks from the Small variant and refine their channel widths and stride configurations to progressively capture multi-scale features at 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, and 1/16 resolutions. This design en-ables the encoder to retain fine-grained texture cues from the RGB image while simultaneously integrating the abso-lute scale priors introduced by the ToF depth measurements. 

Histogram Block Since the ToF regions operate indepen-dently, we first normalize the CNH of each region sepa-rately to ensure that each histogram consistently reflects the internal depth distribution within its own local range. Each region yields a one-dimensional distribution with 

18 bins, leading to a total of 64 independent distributions. To encode these signals, we adopt an MLP-based encoder without any downsampling, thereby preserving the original structure of the distributions. This design allows the model to extract subtle variations in the depth distribution across different spatial regions. 

3.3. Patch-wise CNH Spatial Injection (PCSI) 

During feature encoding, the low-resolution ToF depth map is already fused with the RGB input to provide a coarse global scale prior. The remaining challenge is to effectively integrate the CNH distribution features with the RGB-D spatial representations. As attention mechanisms are widely employed in multimodal fusion, we adopt a cross-attention strategy to capture the complementary relationship between RGB-D and CNH features. In particular, the RGB-D fea-tures serve as the Query, while the CNH features are used as the Key and Value, allowing the RGB-D branch to selec-tively attend to structural cues embedded in the CNH. However, applying attention globally introduces interfer-ence between spatially unrelated areas, because the CNH distributions exhibit no correlation across different regions. To mitigate this issue, we propose the Patch-wise CNH Spa-tial Injection (PCSI) module, as illustrated in Fig. 3, which enforces localized cross-modal guidance. 3.4. Depth Decoder 

The decoder follows a U-Net-style progressive upsampling structure, in which semantic cues and spatial details are gradually integrated to reconstruct a full-resolution depth map. During the decoding process, CNH-enhanced repre-sentations are fused only at the 1/2 and 1/4 scales, whereas the deeper and lower-resolution stages rely solely on the original RGB-D features without CNH injection. There are two key motivations behind this design. First, as discussed in Sec. 3.3, PCSI module requires partition-ing the RGB-D feature map into 64 patches, each aligned with a ToF measurement region. To ensure that each patch contains sufficient spatial samples to interact meaningfully with its corresponding CNH distribution, the patches can-not be too small. At the 1/2 and 1/4 resolutions, the re-sulting patch sizes correspond to 1/16 or 1/32 of the full-5786 resolution grid, representing the smallest spatial granular-ity that still preserves informative local structure. Second, restricting fusion to these intermediate resolutions helps maintain global spatial continuity throughout the recon-struction process. If fusion were applied at all stages, the re-peated patch partitioning could introduce noticeable block-boundary artifacts in the final depth map.

Benchmarks Since training requires a substantial amount of data, we adopt widely used indoor depth benchmarks to simulate ToF measurements. 4.3. Ablation Study 

To investigate the impact of the introduced auxiliary priors and the fusion strategy on the final performance, we conduct comprehensive ablation experiments. The baseline used in this study is a lightweight network that takes only RGB im-ages as input. Quantitative results are reported in Tab. 4, providing a detailed comparison of each component. 

Effect of Different ToF Auxiliary First, we use the same linear feature extractor to encode both the ToF depth and the CNH distribution, and fuse each with the RGB encoder features via cross-attention. We also evaluate an alternative auxiliary input by replacing CNH with the sampled depth distribution used in DELTAR. The results show that the native CNH distribution significantly outperforms both the depth-only input and the sampled distribution. This demon-strates that the CNH measurements is essential and effective for achieving accurate depth estimation. 

Contribution of RGB-D Input We train a model that uses only the low-resolution ToF depth, which is upsampled to form an RGB-D representation. Impact of PCSI Module We further evaluate the role of the proposed PCSI module by removing it and replac-ing localized fusion with global cross-attention between the RGB-D and CNH features. This variant exhibits a clear per-formance degradation compared with the full model. This demonstrates that the proposed PCSI module plays a cru-cial role in ensuring reliable cross-modal guidance, as it en-ables each spatial region to be modulated solely by its cor-responding CNH distribution. Such localized fusion allows the model to accurately localize structural depth priors and effectively learn high-resolution features under lightweight model settings without cross-region interference. 

4.4. Application 

Deployment Performance We test the deployment per-formance of LiteSense across multiple hardware platforms. With a 480 ×480 input, the model achieves inference speeds of approximately 15 ms on a GPU (NVIDIA RTX-3060), 

160 ms on a CPU (Intel i5-12400F), and 570 ms on a NPU (Rockchip RK3576) without quantization, demonstrating real-time capability across heterogeneous devices. 

Quality of the Predicted Depth We adopt two com-monly used depth evaluation metrics, bias and jitter, for quantitative assessment [57]. The device is placed at a 60 ◦

angle toward a planar wall, and measurements are collected from 0.5 m to 4.0 m. For each frame, a plane is fitted as the reference surface. The mean L1 error between the measured depth and the fitted plane is reported as bias, and the standard deviation of the error as jitter. As presented in Fig. 7a, LiteSense achieves overall distance accuracy comparable to RealSense in the mid-range, demonstrating the benefit of fusing RGB information with ToF auxiliary. Meanwhile, accuracy decreases beyond 3.5 m, as this dis-tance approaches the operational limit of this ToF sensor. 

Advantage of RGB-ToF Fusion Method The qualitative comparisons in Fig. 7b show that LiteSense produces more complete and stable depth maps in low-texture regions and occluded areas, whereas stereo-based depth cameras ( e.g ., RealSense) frequently produce invalid measurements. This advantage arises from the ToF sensor providing spatially continuous depth within its effective range. Furthermore, even when the scene extends beyond the ToF sensing dis-tance, LiteSense can still rely on RGB structural cues to preserve object geometry and maintain depth continuity. 

# 5. Conclusions 

In this work, we present LiteSense , a depth estimation net-work that fuses low-resolution depth and CNH measure-ments from a lightweight ToF sensor with RGB imagery, enabling high-quality dense depth reconstruction within the overlapping region of the two sensors. Through a U-Net-style encoder–decoder architecture and the proposed PCSI module, the model effectively enhances both structural de-tail and metric consistency in the predicted depth maps.
