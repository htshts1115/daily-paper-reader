## Abstract 

Depth priors provide salient geometric structure that benefits camouflaged object detection (COD), but directly using Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) causes a task misalignment that still fails to identify camouflaged objects. To address this issue, we propose the Depth Segment Anything Model (DepthSAM), a MDE-adapted method specifically designed to mitigate this misalignment. DepthSAM incorporates two core innovations: (1) a Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Adapter (SMEA) that enables MDE to learn semantic infor-mation unique to camouflaged scenes, and (2) a Ge-ometric–Semantic Fusion Module (GSFM) that effi-ciently integrates geometric cues with high-level se-mantics. With these components, DepthSAM achieves both robust semantic understanding in camouflaged en-vironments and accurate segmentation of camouflaged objects. Extensive experiments show that DepthSAM achieves new SOTA performance on three major bench-marks. For example, on COD10K, its Sα and F ωβ

metrics surpass the best competing methods by 3.0% and 4.3%, respectively. 

## 1. Introduction 

Camouflaged Object Detection (COD), a significant challenge in computer vision, aims to identify objects that blend seamlessly into their surroundings. This capability is crucial for diverse visual tasks, including medical image analysis [39] [70], military reconnais-sance [4], and disaster damage assessment [13]. Meth-ods for COD evolve from custom Convolutional Neu-ral Networks (CNNs) to Transformer-based architec-tures [15, 44]. These earlier approaches, often trained exclusively on specialized datasets, suffer from limited generalizability and performance bottlenecks. There-fore, recent research methods shift towards leveraging more powerful Vision Transformers [62] or fine-tuning large foundation models [5], such as the Segment Any-thing Model (SAM) [23]. As shown in Figure 1, the core conflict is that MDE aims to reconstruct all geometric shapes in a scene, whereas COD’s goal is to isolate specific objects. This misalignment occurs when an object’s geometry is intertwined with its background. The MDE models indiscriminately reconstruct both, causing the object’s structure to become confounded with the background. And geometry alone is often insufficient in these com-plex cases to separate the object from the background. Therefore, how to resolve this misalignment, i.e., how to highlight the object’s geometry and effectively fuse these depth priors with semantic information, remains a key challenge for COD. To overcome this core challenge of task misalign-ment, we propose the Depth Segment Anything Model (DepthSAM). This method explicitly adapts the MDE foundation models for COD. In the encoding stage, we introduce Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Adapters (SMEA) [38], injecting them into the frozen MDE encoder. Their responsibility is to adaptively steer internal features. This steering action compels the de-coder’s multi-scale outputs to suppress background ge-ometry and focus on the camouflaged object’s structure. In the fusion stage, our Geometric-Semantic Fusion Module (GSFM) efficiently integrates these geomet-ric cues with high-level semantic features. Through the synergistic action of these two components, Depth-SAM successfully resolves the task misalignment be-tween MDE’s scene-level reconstruction and COD’s object-level segmentation, accurately segmenting cam-ouflaged objects. Our main contributions can be summarized as fol-lows: 1) We propose DepthSAM. It is the first to adapt the Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) for COD, set-ting a new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on three major benchmarks. 2) We introduce a Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Adapter (SMEA), which is injected into the MDE foundation model encoder to modulate generic, depth-centric features, steering them to be-come object-centric and task-aware. 3) We design a Geometric-Semantic Fusion Module (GSFM) that ef-ficiently integrates these modulated geometric cues with high-level semantic features to generate the final, precise prediction mask. 

## 2. Related Work Camouflaged Object Detection. Early Camouflaged Object Detection (COD) research and many recent foundation model-based techniques [5, 16, 44] are de-signed to discern objects by learning subtle differences in color and texture from RGB images. Their perfor-mance hinges on the quality of these appearance cues. While effective in many scenarios, this dependency creates a significant performance limitation when ob-jects are under extreme camouflage, as these cues be-come ambiguous or deceptive [15]. Our work, Depth-SAM, aims to transcend this limitation by leveraging the MDE model. However, as established in our in-troduction, directly using MDE introduces the task misalignment problem. In this paper, our approach in-troduces two novel components, the Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Adapter (SMEA) and the Geometric-Semantic Fusion Module (GSFM), to effectively adapt MDE for the COD task. 

Monocular Depth Estimation. The field of Monoc-ular Depth Estimation (MDE) has recently shifted to-ward zero-shot foundation models, led by MiDaS [41] and the Depth Anything (DA) series [59] [60]. Con-temporary state-of-the-art methods have significantly mitigated reconstruction artifacts [18] [53] and enabled metrically accurate, prompt-based inference [27]. Such advancements provide robust geometric priors for vari-ous downstream applications, including 3D reconstruc-tion [56], video object segmentation [29], and image restoration [49]. For Camouflaged Object Detection (COD) in particular, depth cues are essential for break-ing the visual mimicry of foreground targets. While previous approaches like RISNet [51] use depth out-puts for boundary refinement, and DSAM [63] adopts two-stream architectures to fuse RGB and depth fea-tures, they often struggle with the inherent cumulative error problem in depth estimation. In contrast, our method involves direct fine-tuning of the MDE model. By modulating the model’s internal features, we mini-mize accumulated errors and more effectively exploit the potential of geometric information. 

## 3. Proposed Method 3.1. Overall Architecture 

As depicted in Figure 2, our proposed DepthSAM method adapts the pre-trained MDE foundation model Depth Anything v2 (DAv2) [60] for the COD task. DAv2 consists of a DINOv2 [36] encoder for visual feature extraction and a DPT [40] decoder for feature upsampling. We introduce two core components. First, we inject Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Adapters (SMEA) into the DINOv2 encoder. These adapters modulate its features, causing the frozen DPT decoder’s multi-scale outputs {F1, F 2, F 3, F 4} to become biased to-wards the camouflaged object. Second, our Geometric-Semantic Fusion Module (GSFM) efficiently fuses these object-biased features into a single, high-quality representation. Finally, a convolutional prediction head maps this representation to the final segmentation mask. The entire model is supervised by a hybrid loss func-tion L = LBCE + LIOU , combining Binary Cross-Entropy [7] and Intersection over Union [42] losses. In addition, we introduce an Auxiliary Loss [11] to ensure the sparsity of SMEA.

3.3. Geometric-Semantic Fusion Module 

Following the SMEA’s modulation of the encoder, the operation of the DPT decoder is influenced. The SMEA’s guidance causes the (frozen) DPT decoder’s output features {F1, F 2, F 3, F 4} to become biased to-wards highlighting the camouflaged object’s geometric priors. However, since the DPT decoder is frozen, its outputs are still fundamentally representations for geometric understanding, not direct segmentation rep-resentations. Furthermore, these multi-level feature maps {F1, F 2, F 3, F 4} contain complementary infor-mation. The shallower features ( e.g ., F1) are rich in modulated geometric cues, while the deeper features (e.g ., F4) hold strong semantic information. To efficiently fuse this complementary information, we design the GSFM. As shown in Figure 2(b), the GSFM employs a Progressive Fusion Architecture. It systematically integrates these features in a shallow-to-deep manner by cascading our core component: the Spatial-Frequency Refinement Module (SFRM). This design allows for a multi-stage process where fine-grained geometric details from shallower layers can progressively guide and refine the semantic representa-tions of deeper layers. This progressive fusion process culminates in a single, high-quality feature output, Fo.Finally, this Fo is passed to a lightweight prediction head. This head, composed of two convolutional layers, maps the feature channels down to 1 to generate the final prediction mask P . This process can be described by the following formula: 

Fo = SFRM (SFRM (SFRM (F4, F 3), F 2), F 1). (2) At each stage of the GSFM, the SFRM is designed to fuse two input feature maps ( e.g ., assuming the two input features are x1 and x2 respectively). As shown in Figure 2(c), its core is a dual-domain design. Fol-lowing an initial interaction ( e.g ., concatenation and convolution) to merge the inputs, the module processes the features in two parallel streams, the Spatial ( S)Stream, which is rich in local geometric details. The Frequency ( F) Stream (via FFT), which efficiently captures global semantic context. Comprehensive in-formation exchange is then enabled by four parallel 25832 Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) mechanisms: (i) Spatial Self-Attention, which refines local structural de-tails within the S stream.(ii) Frequency Self-Attention, captures global contextual relationships within the F

stream.(iii) Geometry-guided Semantics, leverages the spatial stream’s structural map to guide feature aggrega-tion in the frequency domain. This uses local geometry to anchor and calibrate the global semantic represen-tation. (iv) Semantics-guided Geometry, Leverages the attention map from the frequency stream to guide feature aggregation in the spatial domain. This allows high-level semantics to suppress background noise and enhance true object boundaries. By aggregating the results of these four MHSA operations, cross-domain reinforcement is achieved, yielding a fused feature map that is both semantically robust and structurally pre-cise. ## 4. Experiments 4.1. Experimental Setup Datasets. To evaluate the performance of the proposed DepthSAM, we conducted extensive experiments on three public datasets: CAMO [24], COD10K [10], and NC4K [34]. CAMO is the first dataset for COD, con-taining 1,250 camouflaged images covering various categories, with 1,000 for training and 250 for test-ing. COD10K is a larger and more challenging dataset, comprising 3,040 images for training and 2,026 for testing. NC4K is another large-scale test dataset with 4,121 images. 

Evaluation Metrics. We adopt four widely-used met-rics to evaluate model performance comprehensively: the S-measure ( Sα), the weighted F-measure ( F ωβ ), the E-measure ( Eξ), and the Mean Absolute Error (MAE, 

M). Furthermore, to assess the parameter efficiency of our proposed DepthSAM, we report the number of its trainable parameters (Trainable Params). 

Implementation Details. We implement our proposed method in PyTorch. During both training and test-ing, we resize all input images and their corresponding ground truths (GT) to 512×512. We employ standard data augmentation techniques, including random hor-izontal flipping and random cropping. We build the model on top of the pretrained full DAv2 [60] architec-ture. For our SMEA modules, we set the total number of experts to 8, with an activation of Top-K=2. We use the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 5e-5, which we multiply by 0.1 every 150 epochs following a step decay schedule. We train the model for 300 epochs on four NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs with a total batch size of 8 (2 per GPU). 

4.2. Comparison with SOTA Methods 

To demonstrate its effectiveness, the proposed Depth-SAM is compared with 18 SOTA COD meth-ods, including traditional methods BGNet [46], FEDER [15], FSPNet [20], HITNet [19], Pop-Net [57], CamoFormer [62], DaCOD [52], RIS-Net [51], ZoomNeXt [37], FSEL [47], FocusDif-fuser [68], EUFNet [32], VSCode [33], RUN [16], CamoDiffusion [45], and large model-based methods DSAM [63], SAM-Adapter [5], CG-COD [64]. To ensure a fair comparison, we obtain the evaluation met-rics by using the authors’ prediction maps, running the codes provided by the corresponding authors with de-fault parameters, or using the reported results directly from the literature. 

Quantitative Evaluation. The quantitative results are presented in Table 1. We introduce a family of models (DepthSAM-B/L) to show scalability, with our largest model, DepthSAM-L, establishing a new SOTA by outperforming all competitors across three benchmark datasets (improvement over the best competitor CG-COD is highlighted in green). For instance, on NC4K, our model achieves an Sα of 0.929 and an F ωβ of 0.909. As highlighted in green, this surpasses the best compet-ing method, CG-COD, by 2.5% and 4.0%, respectively. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different input resolutions (Table 1). The results show that while re-ducing the resolution leads to a moderate performance decrease, our model still maintains a significant advan-tage over competing methods at the same resolution. This SOTA performance provides compelling evi-dence for our central thesis. Previous SOTA methods, such as FEDER and RISNet, primarily focus on de-signing complex modules to amplify faint appearance cues. Their performance is thus limited when these cues become ambiguous. In contrast, DepthSAM’s SOTA performance stems from leveraging robust geo-metric priors from MDE. Our results demonstrate that DepthSAM successfully resolves the task misalign-ment problem that arises from applying MDE founda-tion models to the COD task. This, in turn, confirms that our method can effectively harness MDE’s pow-erful geometric priors for this task. This advantage is fully reflected in all key metrics. For example, we achieved significant results in two key structural sim-ilarity metrics. Our leading Sα confirms its superior perception of the overall structure of objects ( e.g ., re-gion integrity). Meanwhile, our leading Eξ further demonstrates the model’s ability to accurately capture local structural information. Our leading F ωβ is a com-prehensive metric evaluating overall performance via a weighted average of precision and recall. 4.3. Zero-shot generalization in VCOD 

To subject our approach to the most stringent test of generalization, we evaluated its zero-shot performance on the task of Video Camouflaged Object Detection (VCOD). VCOD introduces significant temporal arti-facts, such as motion blur, rapid lighting shifts, and video artifacts. These factors render appearance-based cues, which are already ambiguous in static images, even more deceptive. We directly applied our DepthSAM model, which was trained exclusively on static COD image datasets, to the challenging MoCA-Mask benchmark without any fine-tuning. As demonstrated in Table 2, Depth-SAM achieves remarkable new SOTA results. It out-performs SOTA methods specifically designed for the video domain, such as TSP-SAM and SAM-PM. No-tably, our Sα score reaches 78.4, surpassing the next best competitor, ZoomNeXt (73.4), by a significant margin of 5.0%. Figure 4 provides a visual explanation for this robust, zero-shot performance. The sequence is challenging, featuring significant motion blur and a tiny object, which corrupts appearance cues. Methods like SLT-Net-LT are severely affected by these artifacts, producing large, erroneous detections. Other strong competitors, such as ZoomNeXt and TSP-SAM, ex-hibit temporal instability and frequently miss the object (false negatives) in several frames. In contrast, Depth-SAM consistently produces clean and accurate masks in every frame. This outstanding zero-shot generalization SOTA re-sult provides the most compelling evidence for the gen-eralization capability of our DepthSAM. Our model re-lies on an MDE foundation model, whose core strength is precisely inferring underlying geometry from appear-ance. Our DepthSAM successfully modulates these geometric priors, making them highly discriminative for the camouflaged object. This ultimately confirms that by resolving the task misalignment, our model has learns a focus camouflaged object geometry capability. It is this capability that enables it to generalize to the VCOD domain.

4.5. Cross-Domain Adaptability 

To validate its generalizability beyond COD, we re-trained DepthSAM on three diverse domains. Ad-ditionally, we introduce the mDice and mIoU 

metrics to evaluate performance in medical scenar-ios. Specifically, these include natural scenes (RGB SOD, using DUTS [50]), optical remote sensing scenes (ORSI SOD, using EORSSD [65]), and med-ical polyp segmentation (using five datasets: CVC-300 [1], CVC-ClinicDB [2], CVC-ColonDB [48], ETIS-LaribPolypDB [43], and Kvasir [22]). In the natural scene SOD (Table 6), DepthSAM achieves SOTA performance, reaching an Sα of 0.947 and an F ωβ of 0.921 on the DUTS dataset, significantly surpassing existing models. This demonstrates that geometric cues provide a significant advantage even when appearance is abundant. For instance, Figure 8(a) visually shows that our model leverages geometry to achieve precise boundary localization for challenging objects like starfish, where traditional methods ( e.g ., VSCode) often fail. In remote sensing (ORSI SOD, Table 7), the model exhibits remarkable robustness, scoring 0.9503 in Sα on EORSSD. As Figure 8(b) con-firms, it accurately detects tiny objects (row 1) while effectively suppressing artifacts from the RGB image. For example, in row 2, the RGB image contains a clear airplane artifact or shadow that is absent in the depth map; our model (Ours) consequently remains unaf-fected by it, achieving a clean detection. Finally, in medical imaging (Table 8), the model yields highly competitive results, achieving leading average scores of 0.8156 mDice and 0.8654 mIoU across the five datasets. This is visually validated in Figure 8(c), which indicates that the model successfully exploits geometric protrusions to achieve precise localization, producing segmentations visibly closer to the ground truth (GT) than methods like Spider or WeakPolyp. In summary, these cross-domain experiments strongly attest to DepthSAM’s adaptability. 

## 5. Conclusion 

Our proposed DepthSAM method solves the task mis-alignment problem between scene-level Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) and object-level Camou-flaged Object Detection (COD). Our method comprises two components: (1) a Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Adapter (SMEA), which guides the frozen MDE model to suppress background geometric information and fo-cus on the camouflaged object’s structure; and (2) a Geometric-Semantic Fusion Module (GSFM), which fuses the features output by the MDE decoder into a high-quality representation with rich semantics and clear boundaries for precise segmentation. Extensive experimental results validate the effectiveness of these two components, confirming the feasibility of leverag-ing and adapting MDE models for the segmentation task. 25837 Acknowledgments
