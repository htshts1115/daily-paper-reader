Abstract. Scaling has driven major advances in vision foundation mod-
els, yet extending it to metric depth estimation remains challenging due
to heterogeneous sensor noise, camera-dependent biases, and metric am-
biguity in noisy cross-source 3D data. We introduce MetricAnything,
a simple and scalable pretraining framework that learns metric depth
from diverse, noisy 3D sources without manual prompts, camera-specific
modeling, or task-specific architectures. At its core is the Sparse Met-
ric Prompt, formed by randomly masking depth maps, which acts as
a universal interface to decouple spatial reasoning from sensor and cam-
era biases. Pretrained on ∼20M image–depth pairs from reconstructed,
captured, and rendered 3D data across 10,000+ camera models, Met-
ricAnything demonstrates—for the first time—a clear scaling trend in
metric depth. The model excels at prompt-driven tasks such as depth
completion, super-resolution, and Radar-camera fusion, while its distilled
prompt-free student achieves state-of-the-art results in monocular depth
estimation, camera intrinsics recovery, single/multi-view metric 3D re-
construction, and VLA planning. Using its pretrained ViT as a visual
encoder also significantly enhances Multimodal Large Language Models
in spatial intelligence. These results show that metric depth estimation
can follow the same scaling laws as modern foundation models, paving a
scalable and efficient path toward real-world metric perception. Project

Introduction
Vision foundation models have achieved remarkable progress by scaling model ca-
pacity and leveraging increasingly large and diverse datasets, enabling emergent
capabilities and robust generalization [22,47,69,77,79]. As AI systems increas-
ingly interact with the physical world through robotics, AR, and autonomous
driving, foundation models must go beyond 2D perception to perceive the 3D
world. Monocular depth estimation (MDE) serves as a fundamental bridge, pro-
viding depth cues essential for physical interaction.
While relative depth estimation has demonstrated successful scaling through
synthetic data and large-scale pseudo-label distillation [122,123], metric depth

as illustrated in Fig. 1a. Unlike relative depth’s ordinal relationships, metric
depth requires learning absolute, physically meaningful distances—a fundamen-
tally harder problem compounded by data-related challenges.
The core bottleneck lies in the heterogeneity of metric depth data sources.
Real-world 3D annotations come from diverse sensors (LiDAR, RGB-D, stereo
cameras) or reconstruction algorithms (SfM, MVS, SLAM), each introducing dis-
tinct noise patterns, hardware-specific artifacts, and camera-dependent biases.
This heterogeneity creates three critical issues: (i) significant domain gaps be-
tween sources, (ii) noisy supervision from sensor misalignment and algorithmic
matching failures, and (iii) metric ambiguity due to varying camera intrinsics.
Together, these factors prevent the “data soup” scaling strategy that has proven
successful in other domains, limiting current metric depth methods to careful
curation of small, clean datasets.
Recent approaches address these challenges with prompt-based methods [60,
64, 104, 112, 137], using sparse depth points or simulated LiDAR cues to guide
prediction. However, they remain limited in scope, typically focusing on specific
prompt-driven tasks with small datasets and relying on complex, hand-crafted
prompt pipelines that introduce strong human priors. This task-specific engi-
neering limits scalability and generalization.
In this work, we present a fundamentally different perspective:
rather than engineering better prompts for specific tasks, we demonstrate that
a simple, scalable prompt-based pretraining paradigm can unlock the metric
depth perception capabilities with large-scale heterogeneous 3D data. In other
words, we are not aiming to construct a stronger model for a particular prompt-
driven task (e.g., depth completion or super-resolution). We posit a simple yet
effective pre-training paradigm to demonstrate a new direction, which proves
that with effective mitigation of data scarcity issues, scaling trends can similarly

occur in the metric estimation track, just as in NLP and 2D vision. Our key
insight is that sparse prompts can serve as a universal interface to decouple
spatial understanding from sensor-specific biases, enabling effective learning from
diverse, noisy sources without complex engineering.
To realize this vision, we introduce MetricAnything, a minimalist pre-
training paradigm tailored for metric depth estimation, which generates Sparse
Metric Prompts by randomly masking portions of depth maps. This design
intentionally avoids task-specific architectures, complex prompt construction
rules, or strong 3D inductive biases, making specialized network design unnec-
essary. Instead, it enables the model to naturally learn structural and metric
understanding directly from data. To support scaling, we assemble approxi-
mately 20M image–depth pairs across three categories: reconstructed 3D data
(SfM/SLAM/MVS), captured 3D data (LiDAR/ToF/RGB-D), and rendered 3D
data. All samples are aligned with metric annotations; for raw point clouds, we
obtain per-pixel depths by projection using known calibration. The collection
spans over 10,000 camera models and diverse environments, enabling unified
pretraining across heterogeneous 3D sources.
The pretrained model, with its simple architecture and pretraining objective,
excels in prompt-based tasks such as depth completion, super-resolution, and
radar-camera depth estimation. Leveraging a dedicated distillation process, we
further create a prompt-free student model that achieves state-of-the-art perfor-
mance across diverse downstream tasks, including monocular depth estimation,
camera calibration, multi-view metric 3D reconstruction, and Vision-Language-
Action (VLA) planning. We further demonstrate that leveraging the pretrained
ViT from our MetricAnything as a visual encoder substantially enhances the
spatial reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models. Remark-
ably, without any task-specific design, both the pretrained and distilled models
consistently achieve state-of-the-art performance across these tasks.
Our findings align with “The Bitter Lesson” [96]: general-purpose, data-driven
methods outperform hand-crafted designs. By showing consistent gains with
scale (Fig. 1a) and robust generalization (Fig. 1b) across ten downstream tasks,
we establish prompt-based pretraining as a scalable path toward general metric
depth perception. Our contributions are:
• MetricAnything: A minimalist prompt-based pretraining paradigm that
employs Sparse Metric Prompt to decouple spatial understanding from sen-
sor biases, enabling scalable learning from heterogeneous 3D sources.
• Demonstrated Scaling: Aggregating 20M diverse image–depth pairs re-
veals stable scaling trends in metric depth, previously unseen in this domain.
• Universal Generalization: Pretrained and distilled models achieve state-
of-the-art results across ten downstream tasks without adaptation.

Related Work
2.1
Monocular Depth Estimation
Relative depth estimation. Relative depth has become the dominant for-
mulation due to its ability to learn from heterogeneous supervision, including


4
J. Yang et al.
pairwise ordering [15,16], stereo/MVS cues [31,57], and pseudo-depth [82,122].
Early CNN-based methods framed depth as regression [23, 24, 50], followed by
discretization and classification strategies to address ambiguity and non-uniform
distributions [6,7,26], though often tied to dataset-specific depth ranges. Large-
scale multi-dataset training with scale-and-shift-invariant objectives proved crit-
ical: MegaDepth [57] and MiDaS [82] demonstrated the benefits of heterogeneous
data mixing, while transformer architectures introduced global context beyond
CNN locality [22,81]. Depth Anything further validated scaling for relative depth
through synthetic supervision and massive pseudo-label distillation (62M im-
ages), achieving strong generalization [122,123]. Extending this success to met-
ric depth remains challenging, as prior works [8,10,38,74,75,108,125] highlight
the greater data demands and complexity of metric-aware perception. However,
heterogeneous and noisy metric depth data limit straightforward scaling. Un-
like prior approaches, our prompt-based pretraining mitigates these issues and
enables scalable metric depth learning.
Metric Depth Estimation. Metric depth estimation has evolved from fixed-
intrinsics RGB-D/LiDAR settings [24, 34, 124] to open-domain, camera-aware
formulations that couple depth with intrinsics or canonical camera transforms
[8,10,35,36,38,73–75,108,125]. Representative strategies include domain-specific
metric heads (ZoeDepth) [8], canonical camera normalization for zero-shot trans-
fer (Metric3D) [38,125], high-resolution ViTs predicting canonical inverse depth
(DepthPro) [10], and direct regression of metric 3D points with camera con-
ditioning (UniDepth) [75]. MoGe-2 decouples metric scale from affine-invariant
point maps [107,108] but relies on complex non-learnable refinement pipelines. In
contrast, we avoid hand-crafted processing and propose a scalable, data-driven
pretraining paradigm that learns robust metric representations from noisy, het-
erogeneous 3D sources.
2.2
Metric Depth Estimation with Sparse Priors
This line of work predicts dense metric depth from RGB with sparse cues such
as LiDAR, RGB-D, or radar [18,19,70,98,130,136]. Representative methods in-
clude Marigold-DC [104], OMNI-DC [137], PriorDA [112], PromptDA [60], and
TacoDepth [111], which leverage diffusion guidance, gradient integration, metric-
prior alignment, prompt injection, or sensor-specific fusion. Despite progress,
most approaches remain limited to specific prompt types or sensor modalities, of-
ten relying on heavy hand-designed prompt simulation and small-scale datasets.
In contrast, we minimize human prior design and instead scale simple pretext
tasks over naturally heterogeneous data. This enables robust spatial understand-
ing and state-of-the-art performance across multi-sensor fusion, monocular depth
estimation, camera calibration, multi-view 3D reconstruction, and VLA planning
without task-specific specialization.

3.1
Multi-Source Data Collection
To enable scalable pretraining, we aggregate open-source 3D datasets with metric
annotations and standardize all inputs as metric depth maps with validity masks:
  \ {\m
a t hbf { G } , \ mathb f {M}\}, \quad \mathbf {G} \in \mathbb {R}^{H \times W}, \;\; \mathbf {M} \in \{0,1\}^{H \times W}, \vspace {-1mm} 
(1)
where G denotes the metric depth along the camera z-axis, and M indicates
valid measurements (M(p) = 1 for valid pixels and M(p) = 0 otherwise).
Reconstructed 3D Data. We aggregate depth maps from open-source datasets [4,
5,20,40,101,105,113,119] derived from diverse reconstruction techniques, includ-
ing SfM, SLAM, MVS, and binocular/multi-view stereo (e.g., plane-sweeping,
PatchMatch). While these maps provide per-pixel metric depth G with validity
masks M, artifacts remain prevalent in challenging regions—such as textureless
areas, specular surfaces, thin structures, occlusions, and dynamic scenes—primarily
due to correspondence matching failures.
Captured 3D Data. We also incorporate point clouds captured by LiDAR,
ToF, and RGB-D sensors [1,14,21,29,33,37,78,93,95,114,117,127]. Given the
camera intrinsics K and sensor-to-camera pose Tc←s = [R | t], depth maps
{G, M} are obtained by projecting each sensor-frame point Xs
j onto the cor-
responding camera plane. Sensor-derived depth is often noisy and sparse due


6
J. Yang et al.
to limitations in sensor resolution and the influence of environmental factors
such as severe weather. Additional sources of error include beam divergence and
sampling gaps (affecting thin structures), weak or anomalous returns on specu-
lar, metallic, or transparent/semi-transparent surfaces, multi-path interference,
mixed pixels at sharp boundaries, out-of-range distances, occlusions, and motion-
induced time offsets. These factors result in invalid measurements, indicated by
M(p) = 0 for affected pixels.
Rendered 3D Data. To complement real-world data, we incorporate synthetic
depth maps from virtual engines [2, 25, 27, 32, 32, 56, 83, 85, 106, 110, 115, 133].
Despite limited realism, these noise-free maps preserve fine structural details
and sharp geometry, providing valuable geometric supervision.
In total, this purposefully aggregated dataset comprises approximately 20
million image–depth pairs, captured by over 10,000 distinct camera models and
spanning a wide variety of real and synthetic scenes.
3.2
Pretraining via Sparse Metric Prompt
Problem Formulation. Given a monocular image I ∈RH×W ×3 and a corre-
sponding sparse metric prompt:
  P = \ {(x _i, y
_i, d_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}, \vspace {-1mm} 
(2)
where each triplet (xi, yi, di) represents a pixel coordinate (xi, yi) and its associ-
ated metric depth value di. The objective of pretraining is to learn a function fθ,
parameterized by θ, that predicts a dense metric depth map D from the input
image I, conditioned on the prompt P:
  D = f _{\
t h eta }(I, P), \quad D \in \mathbb {R}^{H \times W}. \vspace {-1mm} 
(3)
Here, prompt P provides metric depth for a small subset of pixels, offering limited
geometric cues. The model fθ learns to propagate these sparse metric constraints
across the image, yielding a complete, geometrically consistent, and metrically
accurate depth map. Since our data sources inevitably contain inherent noise
and varying patterns of incompleteness, fθ is trained to identify and correct
potential errors within the sparse prompt P.
Prompt Preparation. From each source in {G, M}, we randomly sample
N \in [2{ ,}000, 40{,}00 0] valid pixels (around 1% of all image pixels) from its depth
map to construct a sparse metric prompt P and its corresponding mask M '.
Due to diverse patterns, sparsity, and incompleteness across {G}, the sparsely
sampled prompt P inevitably inherits heterogeneous distributions and irregular
structures, which challenge models to effectively handle diverse distributions.
To mitigate this, we first apply a prompt preparation step that maps all
prompts into a shared intermediate domain. Following PriorDA [112], we apply
a pre-trained depth prediction model [10] to obtain a prior depth map P_
d. Then,
inspired by See3D [67], we perform Pixel-wise Depth Scale Alignment (PDSA)
and Global Metric Depth Recovery (GMDR) to fill the missing regions in P under
the guidance of P_
d. The final input prompt is constructed by concatenating the
PDSA-refined map, the GMDR-corrected map, and the prompt mask M ' along

the channel dimension. This yields a unified and regularized representation of
shape H × W × 3, avoiding issues with irregular prompt network design and
training efficiency, while also easing the handling of diverse prompts.
Prompt Injection. Although this preparation process can maintain metric
consistency between Pd and Prompt P through pixel-wise and global alignment,
its parameter-free (non-learning) nature remains sensitive to sampling patterns
and noise propagated from prompt P. To further address this, we employ a
prompt-injection mechanism that allows the model to correct noisy prompts
and generate accurate, dense depth predictions. Following prior works, several
strategies can be used for prompt injection, including adaptive layer normaliza-
tion (AdaLN) [71], cross-attention [84,103], ControlNet-style conditioning [128],
conditioned DPT heads [60], and conditioning layers parallel to the RGB in-
put [112]. Among these, we opt for a conditioned DPT head, as it introduces
only about 5% extra parameters while maintaining efficiency through lightweight
interpolation and shallow convolutional layers.
This design allocates most parameters to the foundational depth backbone,
allowing the main network pathway to correct noise and structural inconsis-
tencies, rather than relying on the injected condition layers. Importantly, we
make no bespoke modifications to the backbone; instead, we reuse a general,
efficient, and widely validated architecture [10]. The only difference is that we
merge the patch encoder and image encoder into a single shared ViT to achieve
a balance between accuracy and efficiency, as detailed in our ablation studies.
This emphasizes our deliberate shift away from hand-crafted priors and man-
ually designed prompts, relying entirely on data-driven learning. By isolating
conditioning from architectural changes, we can cleanly assess the effectiveness
of the learned prompts and rigorously study the scaling behavior.
Pretraining Objective. Following DepthPro [10], we use mean absolute error
(MAE) and scale-and-shift invariant mean absolute gradient error (SSI-MAGE)
losses for synthetic data. For real-world data, we adopt a robust MAE loss that
drops top-n-largest-loss regions per image during training to mitigate noise sen-
sitivity, where n is set as 20%. The overall objective is:

3.3
Prompt-Free Model Distillation
Distillation Process. To validate the benefits of large-scale pretraining and
transfer them to scenarios where prompts are unavailable, we distill the pre-
trained (teacher) model into a prompt-free student that performs dense depth
prediction solely from RGB inputs. Specifically, we leverage the trained teacher
model to generate high-fidelity pseudo depth maps conditioned on sparse prompt
P for all real-world images in the collected dataset via a simple feed-forward pro-
cess, effectively transforming sparse and noisy prompts into dense, high-quality
supervision signals (i.e., pseudo labels). Unlike conventional RGB-D or LiDAR
sensors—whose effective sensing range is constrained by hardware to only a few
to tens of meters—and many depth-estimation algorithms that struggle to es-
timate distant backgrounds, our teacher-generated pseudo labels span diverse

a prompt-free monocular depth estimation model, preserving the teacher’s met-

main factors. First, unlike most real-world datasets biased toward short-range
depths, our teacher model generates reliable pseudo labels across both near and
far regions (Fig. 3). This wider depth distribution exposes the limitations of
conventional depth losses: direct depth supervision (L1/L2) blurs fine geomet-
ric details, whereas inverse-depth loss decays too rapidly with distance, losing
effective supervision for distant regions. Consequently, these standard losses are
suboptimal for supervising our high-fidelity, large-range pseudo labels. Second,
typical ViT encoders with DPT decoders employ U-Net-style skip connections to
propagate shallow features upward. This design stabilizes training under noisy
supervision because low-level cues (e.g., textures and colors) are inherently more
consistent and easier to learn. By routing these low-level features directly to the
output head, the network mitigates conflicts with noisy targets, thereby smooth-
ing gradient fluctuations. However, this heavily relies on low-level details, un-
derutilizing the high-level semantic cues essential for precise depth reasoning. In
our setting, the pseudo labels are generated by a unified model, exhibit minimal
domain gaps, and have been refined to eliminate most noise. Thus, we can reduce
the reliance on shallow-to-deep feature injection and explore more advanced net-
work designs that fully exploit the rich semantic representations from the deep
blocks of the ViT encoder.
Improved Student. Building upon the above observations, we retain the multi-
scale fusion mechanism of [10] while introducing two modifications. First, we
adopt a distance-balanced log-depth loss to improve supervision across varying
depth ranges. Specifically, instead of directly regressing metric depth, we trans-
form the predicted and ground-truth depths into log space to balance gradients
between near and far regions. This preserves fine-grained sensitivity in close-
range areas while preventing long-range depths from being under-supervised.

Experiment
4.1
Implementation Details
We train the pre-trained model on 144 H200 GPUs for 100k steps with a 10k-
step warm-up, using peak learning rates of 1×10−6 for the ViT backbone and
1×10−5 for the DPT head and prompt layer. The additional convolutional ker-
nels and biases required for prompt injection are zero-initialized. For the distilled
prompt-free student, pseudo-labels are generated by the pre-trained model. We
evaluate two training paradigms. (1) From-scratch training: the ViT backbone is
initialized from DINOv3 ViT-H+/16 [90], with a randomly initialized DPT head.
Training runs on 144 H200 GPUs for 200k steps with a 5k-step warm-up and
peak learning rates of 2×10−6 (ViT) and 2×10−5 (DPT). (2) Fine-tuning: all
parameters are initialized from MoGe-2 [108] and trained on 80 H200 GPUs for
10k steps using a 1k-step warm-up, with peak learning rates of 2.5×10−6 (ViT)
and 2.5×10−5 (DPT). Additional training details are provided in the Supp.

is trained only once with simple, sparsely sampled prompts and operates fully
zero-shot manner, without any task-specific design or prompt alignment.

4.4
Ablation Study
Scaling up Data. We pretrain our model on data subsets ranging from 5% to
100%. On KITTI with 8× super-resolution, AbsRel steadily decreases from 5.22
to 2.34, indicating improved generalization as the data scale increases (Fig. 1a).
To further validate this scaling behavior, we build two controlled baselines. On
the teacher side, we follow the prompt processing and loss design of PriorDA
and train a PriorDA-style baseline under the same data scales, 100k training
steps, and evaluation protocol. From 8M to 20M samples, full MetricAnything
(MA) reduces AbsRel by 22.5%, whereas the PriorDA-style baseline degrades
by 4.4%. On the student side, we distill the same student architecture using
MoGe-2-generated pseudo labels to isolate the effect of the teacher-label source
(Fig. 11). From 16M to 20M, the MoGe-2 baseline slightly drops by 0.8%, while
full MA further improves by 2.1% and maintains a clear accuracy advantage.
Scaling Attribution. We perform component-wise attribution under the same
student data, training schedule, and evaluation setup. The green numbers in
Tab. 12 indicate the relative AbsRel reduction of Full MetricAnything over
each ablated variant. We ablate four components: replacing random sparse-mask
prompts with PriorDA-style hand-designed prompts, using only single-source
synthetic/rendered data, replacing the inverse-skip student with U-Net-style fu-
sion, and removing the distance-balanced distillation loss. All ablations degrade
performance, showing that the student scaling gain relies on the joint design of
Sparse Metric Prompt pretraining, heterogeneous data, inverse-skip distillation,
and distance-balanced supervision, rather than data scale alone.

excessive weights (e.g., 600) bias the model towards distant regions at the cost
of near-field details, while insufficient weights compromise distant supervision.
Consequently, we select C = 400 for the optimal trade-off.
Test-Time Resolution Scaling. As shown in Fig. 9, our model naturally
supports test-time resolution scaling. When increasing the input resolution from
1× to 3× and 9×, the predicted depth maps recover finer structures and sharper
object boundaries, without any additional fine-tuning.

and extreme conditions; (b) Extended discussions on additional experiments and
ablation studies; (c) More implementation details.
5
Conclusion
We present MetricAnything, a scalable framework learning from heteroge-
neous sources without manual prompts. Utilizing Sparse Metric Prompts to de-
couple geometry from sensor priors, we reveal the first clear scaling law in metric
depth. Our models achieve SOTA results across downstream tasks, offering a ro-
bust solution for real-world depth perception.
