## Abstract 

Monocular depth estimation serves as a core technique in endoscopic applications such as 3D reconstruction and localization. However, most existing methods focus primar-ily on in-domain depth estimation, which limits their robust-ness and prevents them from delivering impressive cross-domain performance, due to variations in depth distribu-tions, illumination conditions, and texture patterns. In this work, we propose Depth Any Endoscopy (DAE) , a novel self-supervised framework for generalizable depth estima-tion in monocular endoscopy. To specify, we develop a dual-level Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) adaptation paradigm that effectively tailors Vision Foundation Models to diverse en-doscopic procedures, such as laparoscopy and colonoscopy, accounting for the challenges posed by varying environ-ments. Internally, we integrate LoRA and Adapter modules within the MoE architecture, allowing the model to flexi-bly adapt to the characteristics of input data. Externally, a mixture of domain-specific experts provides customized guidance to enhance the training stability. In addition, we introduce a learnable gradient harmonization mecha-nism to dynamically balance the optimization between the depth and pose networks, along with a semantic distribution calibration module that strengthens the semantic consis-tency of depth predictions. Extensive experiments demon-strate that the proposed DAE achieves state-of-the-art per-formance in both zero-shot and in-domain depth estima-tion scenarios. The source code is publicly available at 

https://github.com/ShuweiShao/DAE .

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Self-supervised Depth Estimation 

In recent years, self-supervised depth estimation methods have attracted widespread attention and made remarkable progress, primarily because they avoid the need for hard-ware sensors to acquire costly ground-truth depth labels. One of the early works by Zhou et al. [69] formulated depth estimation as a novel view synthesis task, laying the groundwork for this research direction. Following this, numerous methods have been developed [3, 15, 30, 49, 54, 59, 65, 67, 68]. For example, Godard et al. [15] proposed a minimum reprojection loss to improve robust-ness against occlusions, and an auto-masking loss to ex-clude pixels that do not conform to the camera motion model during training. Concurrently, a growing body of research [28, 34, 45, 47, 53, 57, 62] has extended self-supervised depth estimation to endoscopic scenarios. Tu-ran et al. [53] were among the notable pioneers to explore self-supervised endoscopic depth and ego-motion estima-tion, following a paradigm similar to that of [69]. Liu et al. [28] later leveraged Structure-from-Motion to provide sparse geometric supervision. To alleviate the photometric ambiguities induced by inter-frame brightness fluctuations, Ozyoruk et al. [34] and Shao et al. [47] introduced an affine brightness Transformer and an appearance flow network, re-spectively. Yang et al. [62] focused instead on designing an efficient network architecture that integrates convolutional operations with Transformer modules. 2.2. Generalizable Depth Estimation 

The recent evolution of generalizable depth estimation [5, 14, 18, 23, 37, 38, 40, 60, 61, 63] has brought considerable progress to monocular approaches, driven by the scaling of training data and the refinement of optimization techniques. As one of the seminal works, Ranftl et al. [40] proposed to train a model in the disparity space and developed a scale-and shift-invariant loss function to mitigate discrepancies arising from varied dataset characteristics. This formulation has since been widely used in subsequent research. On the basis of [40], Yang et al. [60, 61] further leveraged extensive unlabeled data, combining data augmentation with auxiliary teacher supervision to acquire a robust and general-purpose depth estimation model with improved generalization. Al-ternatively, Ke et al. [23] harnessed the rich visual knowl-edge embedded in Stable Diffusion [44], repurposing it for depth estimation task to deliver fine-grained depth predic-tions. Given the strong geometric priors inherent in these Vision Foundation Models, recent studies [10, 50, 51, 70] have increasingly explored adopting them for endoscopic scenarios. For example, Cui et al. [10] showcased the po-tential of adapting Depth Anything [60] using the dynamic vector-based LoRA and convolutional neck modules. Af-terwards, Sheikh et al. [22] integrated weight-decomposed LoRA [27] with high-rank updating [22] to achieve more effective adaptation. In this work, we continue prior efforts while concurrently facilitating adaptation across diverse en-doscopic procedures, paving the way towards a more uni-versal depth estimation framework for surgical endoscopy. 

2.3. Mixture-of-Experts 

Mixture-of-Experts [20] is a scalable technique for increas-ing the model capacity while maintaining minimal compu-tational overhead, with Feed-Forward Networks [2, 13, 43] typically serving as the standard choice for expert modules within MoE architectures. Building upon MoE, contempo-rary studies [24, 29, 32] have incorporated multiple uniform LoRA modules into Large Language and Vision Models to facilitate multi-task learning. Beyond this, MoE has been applied to downstream dense prediction tasks [35, 36, 55], enhancing robustness and modeling prediction uncertainty. In this work, we delve into endoscopic depth estimation and devise a dual-level MoE architecture, featuring intra-model and extra-model components, crafted to effectively navigate the variability and complexity of endoscopic environments.

In this section, we provide a detailed description to the core components of the proposed DAE framework (see Fig. 3), including the principles of self-supervised depth estimation (Sec. 3.1), the dual-level Mixture-of-Experts (Sec. 3.2), the learnable gradient harmonization (Sec. 3.3), the semantic distribution calibration (Sec. 3.4), and the overall architec-ture and loss formulation (Sec. 3.5).

3.2. Dual-Level Mixture-of-Experts 

The proposed MoE design involves dual levels: intra-model and extra-model. The intra-model MoE, integrated into the VFMs such as Depth Anythingv2 [61], dynamically routes through LoRA experts of varying ranks and adapter experts with different kernel sizes based on the input characteristics. The extra-model MoE offers tailored depth guidance, with each expert following a static routing strategy determined by a domain indicator. Further details are provided below. 

Intra-model MoE. Data from different surgical proce-dures tend to exhibit considerable variations, making it hard to adapt VFMs using the vanilla LoRA [19]. For instance, prior methods [10, 51] rely on a fixed and pre-defined rank, which limits LoRA’s flexibility and hampers its ability to effectively handle such domain variations. Drawing on the success of MoE in prior tasks [24, 29, 32, 36, 55], we inte-grate LoRA into a Mixture-of-Experts architecture, where multiple LoRA modules with distinct ranks r act as experts. A lightweight selector SL (·) dynamically routes the input query Iin to the Top-κ optimal experts in light of its char-acteristics. 3.3. Learnable Gradient Harmonization 

In self-supervised depth estimation, both the depth and pose networks are jointly optimized using a shared photometric loss. As a result, ensuring balanced gradients between these two networks is essential for stable and efficient training dy-namics. For instance, Godard et al. [15] introduces a scal-ing factor of 0.01 to adjust the gradient magnitudes of the pose network. In experiments, we find that this harmoniza-tion factor is typically dataset-dependent. For the SCARED dataset [1], a factor of 0.001 enables proper convergence, while a value of 0.01 causes the model to fail. In contrast, the model trained on the SimCol3D [42] converges properly with a factor of 0.01. This discrepancy may arise from the varying difficulties in learning depth and pose across dif-ferent datasets, and without suitable mitigation, such gradi-ent imbalances could lead to slow convergence and unstable training dynamics. In this regard, we develop a learnable gradient harmo-nization mechanism that adaptively regulates the optimiza-tion dynamics between depth and pose networks across dif-ferent domains. Given a domain indicator ζ ∈ D associated with the input data, we first map ζ to a learnable domain embedding using an embedding layer, which is then fed into an MLP layer to generate a domain-specific harmonization factor α. ## 4. Experiment 

4.1. Implementation Details 

The proposed framework is implemented in PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPU utilizing the AdamW optimizer [31]. The learning rate is initialized at 

1 × 10 −4 and reduced by a factor of 0.1 after 10 epochs. We adopt the data augmentation strategies from [15, 69], train-ing the model for 20 epochs using a batch size of 8. In our MoE-LoRA and MoE-Adapter layers, we set the number of experts to 4, with Top-1 expert being selected as optimal for each input. The pre-defined ranks for MoE-LoRA are 4, 8, 16, and 32, while the kernel sizes for MoE-Adapter are 3, 5, 7, and 9. The tunable layers in VFM Depth Anythingv2 are [2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11], with all other layers frozen. We adopt the standard evaluation metrics as used in [10, 47]. 

4.2. Dataset 

The training data has four mixed datasets: SCARED [1], Hamlyn [33], SimCol3D [42], and Colondepth [41]. For the SCARED dataset, we follow the split from [47], which includes 15351 frames for training and 551 frames for eval-uation. For the Hamlyn dataset, we manually curate 10392 frames with higher-quality. Moreover, we randomly sample 15351 frames from the SimCol3D, and employ all 15840 frames from Colondepth for training. In total, our training data consists of 56934 frames across all datasets. Regard-ing the zero-shot evaluation, we employ the unseen datasets C3VD [4], C3VDv2 [16], and SERV-CT [12], along with clinically collected arthroscopy data exclusively for quali-tative comparison. The C3VD and C3VDv2 datasets each consist of 551 randomly sampled test frames, whereas the SERV-CT has 48 test frames. For the in-domain evaluation, we adopt the SCARED and SimCol3D datasets. The Sim-Col3D test set comprises 551 randomly sampled frames. 

4.3. Quantitative Zero-shot Evaluation 

We begin by evaluating the proposed DAE through an ex-amination of its zero-shot generalization ability across dif-ferent endoscopic datasets. As shown in Table 1, DAE con-sistently outperforms the compared approaches on all met-rics. C3VD and C3VDv2 datasets are derived from colono-scopic recordings, whereas SERV-CT is collected from la-paroscopic procedures. ## 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we introduce DAE, a unified self-supervised depth estimation framework that delivers consistent perfor-mance across diverse endoscopic procedures. The proposed DAE leverages a dual-level Mixture-of-Experts paradigm to adapt Vision Foundation Models to heterogeneous surgi-cal domains, along with a learnable gradient harmonization mechanism and a semantic distribution calibration module to strengthen coordinated optimization and enforce seman-tic consistency of depth predictions.
