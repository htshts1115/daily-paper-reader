Abstract. Monocular depth estimation foundation models, such as the
Depth Anything series, have achieved remarkable performance across
diverse domains. However, they still suffer from critical failures under
adverse weather conditions, such as fog, rain, snow, or at night. To ad-
dress this, we present Weather-Conditioned Depth Anything (DA-W),
a framework that explicitly disentangles style from content for weather-
robust depth estimation. Specifically, we introduce a Style Filter trained
on a curated mix of real and synthetic degradation datasets to extract
content-independent, degradation-aware weather embeddings. This style

Introduction

3D perception [15, 33, 57] for cost-effective applications, such as autonomous
driving, robotics, and AR/VR, where it can replace relying on LiDAR or stereo
cameras. Driven by large-scale datasets and model training, recent depth founda-
tion models [2,54] have demonstrated strong generalization in depth estimation
across diverse environments. However, their performance degrades dramatically
under adverse weather conditions—fog, snow, rain, and nighttime—due to at-
mospheric scattering, occlusion, specular artifacts, and low-light noise, which
fundamentally alter the appearance (See Figure. 1).

Consequently, the key question is whether features learned from synthetic
data generalize reliably to real-world scenarios. To probe this generalization
gap, we analyze how a foundation depth model represents appearance varia-
tions across domains. We extract the [CLS] tokens in vision transformer (ViT)

feature embeddings via t–SNE [35] in Figure. 3 (left).
As may be seen, the features are fundamentally entangled. Representations
from different weather types (e.g., fog, rain, snow) are heavily mixed, rather than
forming separable clusters. The model conflates weather-specific style with scene
geometry into a single, undifferentiated representation, instead of providing a
pure weather code. This entanglement is the root cause of the model’s brittleness,
as it cannot distinguish appearance variations caused by weather from those of
the scene itself, preventing the learning of generalizable weather patterns and
fatally hindering synthetic-to-real adaptation.
Motivated by these insights, we propose Weather-Conditioned Depth Any-
thing (DepthAnything-W or DA-W), a simple yet robust adaptation strategy
to robustify depth estimation under adverse weather conditions. We first design
a Style Filter network using contrastive learning on a curated collection of 7 real-
world and 4 synthetic weather datasets. This module learns to align the style
distributions of real and synthetic degradations, as shown in Figure 3(right),
thereby producing a discriminative weather embedding. The embedding then
conditions the Depth Anything foundation model using lightweight modulation
techniques AdaLN-Zero. This lightweight conditioning allows the model to adjust
its predictions based on the estimated weather while keeping the encoder frozen,
preventing catastrophic forgetting and preserving clean-scene performance. Ex-
tensive experiments demonstrate that DA-W achieves state-of-the-art perfor-
mance across both general and adverse-weather benchmarks Figure 2.

Related Work
2.1
Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE).
Monocular depth estimation (MDE) predicts scene geometry from a single RGB
image and serves as a foundational component for various 3D perception tasks
in robotics [22,46], augmented and virtual reality [2], and autonomous driving.
The Initial deep learning approaches were either supervised using datasets such
as KITTI [17] and NYUv2 [45], or self-supervised through stereo [18,65] or video
photometric consistency [55,61,64]. However, both strategies exhibited domain
bias and limited generalization capabilities.
Recent research has addressed domain bias in early supervised and self-
supervised pipelines by using more diverse datasets and larger models. These
strategies help improve cross-domain robustness and facilitate foundational depth
estimation. MiDaS [40] was one of the early methods to improve generalization
by training on multiple datasets. Subsequent research advances these efforts by
pretraining on larger and more diverse labeled and unlabeled datasets, as demon-
strated by the Depth Anything series [31,59,60]. Additional methods, including
ZoeDepth [1], UniDepth [38], and Metric3D(v2) [20], target generalization across
various camera systems and domains. These strategies demonstrate strong per-
formance on previously unseen tasks.
In addition to scaling discriminative models, another research direction in-
vestigates generative depth estimation, which leverages strong priors from large
generative models. These methods formulate depth prediction as sampling from
a conditional distribution. The framework inherently supports uncertainty-aware
inference and enables tasks such as depth inpainting. For example, Marigold [24]
employs diffusion-based image generators for monocular depth estimation. Flow
matching models like DepthFM [19] formulates monocular depth estimation as
distribution transport and enable rapid, high-quality sampling.
2.2
Robust Depth Estimation.
Although large-scale training improves average cross-domain performance, depth
models remain brittle when input images violate standard assumptions of image

formation, such as rain streaks, fog scattering, or nighttime noise. These con-
ditions corrupt or remove geometric cues [16,42]. Among these factors, adverse
weather is particularly challenging because of scattering, occlusions, and low
illumination [16].
To address this, prior work largely falls into two categories: (1) Two-stage
pipelines, which restore degraded images using diffusion or unified restoration
models like UniRestore [7] and MWFormer [66] before depth estimation, but
often alter geometric cues and degrade accuracy [42]; and (2) Weather-aware
training, which incorporates robustness directly into the depth estimation model.
WeatherDepth [53] employs curriculum contrastive learning from clear to severe
weather, and md4all [16] enforces consistency between clean and degraded sam-
ples. Foundation model extensions like DepthAnything-AC [48] and ACDepth [21],
incorporate physics-based degradations and knowledge distillation to achieve
strong zero-shot robustness. However, although these methods enhance resilience,
they often entangle weather appearance with scene geometry. Our approach
explicitly disentangles them using a Style Filter that learns domain-invariant
weather embeddings, thereby enabling robust depth estimation without sacrific-
ing clean-image generalization.
3
Proposed Method
3.1
Preliminary: Depth Anything Model
We provide a concise overview of the Depth Anything [60] framework. As shown
in Figure 4, it comprises two main components: the encoder employs a vision
transformer (DINOv2) to extract hierarchical features, augmented with a multi-
scale readout mechanism that produces pyramid feature representations, and
the decoder adopts the DPT design [39], progressively fusing multi-scale fea-
tures through refinement blocks with skip connections, followed by a lightweight
prediction head for dense depth regression. The decoder performs progressive
upsampling with skip-wise fusion to recover fine structure. Depth Anything is
trained on large mixed-supervision data with an affine-invariant objective, yield-
ing zero-shot generalization across diverse scenes. In our method, we keep this
backbone intact and inject weather conditioning only in the decoder to preserve
clean-scene behavior while improving robustness.

4.2
Training Details
We adopt a parameter-efficient two-stage training protocol. Stage I: We train
the Style Filter on the combined real and synthetic weather data to learn a
compact 64-D weather embedding. The training procedure is described in MW-
Former [66]. Stage II: We optimize only the DPT decoder head and the AdaLN-
Zero modulation layers, which inject the weather embedding at five decoder
stages. We train for 20 epochs using AdamW with a learning rate of 5e-6, weight
decay of 0.01, and a batch size of 16. All experiments are conducted on a single
NVIDIA A100 GPU.
4.3
Evaluation Protocol
We follow the evaluation protocol of DepthAnything-AC [48], using standard rel-
ative depth metrics (AbsRel and δ1) across two benchmark categories. Adverse-
weather benchmarks: We evaluate on real world adverse weather datasets
NuScenes-night [6], RobotCar-night [36], DrivingStereo-rain/cloud/fog [58], and
the synthetic weather corruption datasets KITTI-C (Dark, Snow, Fog, Mo-
tion) [26]. Mix-weather benchmarks: To address the practical setting where
multiple weather conditions may coexist (e.g., rainy nights or snowy nights), we
introduce a mix-weather benchmark composed of both synthetic and real-world
subsets. Clean-domain benchmarks: To verify that weather adaptation does
not degrade general performance, we report results on KITTI [17], NYU-Depth
v2 [45], Sintel [5], ETH3D [43], and DIODE [52].

General benchmarks (clean scenes). To assess catastrophic forgetting, we
evaluate on standard clean datasets (Table 3). DepthAnything-W maintains
the backbone’s performance, with small but consistent gains on KITTI and
Sintel, parity on ETH3D. This indicates that decoder-side AdaLN with zero-
initialized heads preserves clean-scene behavior and improves robustness under
adverse weather conditions.
Mix-weather benchmark (coexisting conditions). Real driving often ex-
hibits co-occurring degradations, such as rainy nights or snowy nights, which
are not covered by single-factor benchmarks. To evaluate this setting, we con-
struct a mix-weather benchmark with both synthetic and real-world subsets. For
the synthetic subset, we follow RoboDepth [26] to compose weather corruptions
on the KITTI Eigen split, testing 11 mixed corruption combinations across 5
severity levels. As summarized in Table 4, DepthAnything-W consistently im-
proves both δ1 and AbsRel over DepthAnything v1/v2/v3 and DepthAnything-
AC across most mixed-condition tracks, indicating that explicit weather embed-
dings and decoder-only AdaLN conditioning remain effective even when multiple
condition factors overlap. For the real-world subset, we qualitatively evaluate on
Boreas [4] night+snow sequences, where our predictions exhibit fewer structural
artifacts and more stable geometry under simultaneous low-illumination and
snowfall (see Figure. 6). These results indicate that adaptation to individual
weather conditions can transfer to coexisting degradations: our conditioning de-
sign remains effective on mixed corruptions without requiring restoration-stage
preprocessing.
5.1
Ablation Study
Table 5 shows three design choices: fine-tuning strategy (full or decoder-only),
data composition (with or without real-world degradations), and AdaLN-based

weather conditioning. Although no single configuration dominates all metrics,
the complete decoder-only variant with real-world degradations and weather
injection offers the best overall trade-off.
Fine-tuning strategy. Transitioning from full fine-tuning to decoder-only tuning
gives a better overall trade-off across adverse and clean benchmarks. Specifically,
decoder-only tuning results in higher δ1 on NuScenes-night (0.741 versus 0.727)
and enhances clean-scene generalization on NYU-D and KITTI. These findings
indicate that the pretrained encoder is sufficiently general and that freezing it
helps prevent representation drift.
Data composition. Incorporating real-world degradations during training pri-
marily benefits real adverse-weather domains, as demonstrated by clear gains on
RobotCar-night and a modest improvement on DS-rain, while clean benchmarks
remain largely unaffected. A minor trade-off is observed on NuScenes-night, in-
dicating a residual domain mismatch between the real-night data characteristics
and this benchmark.
Weather conditioning. Enabling AdaLN-based weather injection further enhances
nighttime robustness, particularly on RobotCar-night and to a lesser extent on
NuScenes-night , while maintaining strong performance on clean benchmarks
(KITTI : 0.080/0.937). For DrivingStereo corruptions, the effect is mixed, as
performance is slightly reduced on DS-fog, suggesting that not all synthetic cor-
ruptions benefit equally from explicit weather conditioning.
Loss and restoration ablations. Table 6 isolates the objective terms requested by
the reviewers. Removing Ldist causes the largest degradation, increasing Global
AbsRel from 0.117 to 0.352, which confirms that teacher distillation anchors the
adapted model to geometry. Lpair provides a small but consistent gain, while Laug
shows no measurable independent gain under this protocol and is best interpreted
as a real-weather consistency regularizer. The RGB restoration baseline disables
Style Filter/AdaLN conditioning and adds a lightweight degraded-to-clean RGB
head trained with masked L1 loss on synthetic pairs, weighted by λrgb = 0.1.
It is competitive but does not improve the Global score over DA-W, suggesting
that weather-conditioned modulation is not merely image restoration. Full fine-
tuning improves the real-night average but is not better globally, supporting
decoder-only adaptation as the better robustness-generalization trade-off.

adverse weather conditions. The model’s effectiveness stems from its key com-

dings, and the AdaLN-Zero-based decoder, which injects these embeddings into
a frozen Depth Anything backbone. To evaluate the proposed architecture, a
