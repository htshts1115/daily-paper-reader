## Abstract 

Existing dynamic scene rendering methods often adopt rigid-body or direction-limited assumptions, yet real-world motion and contact routinely violate these, producing arti-facts near occlusion boundaries. To address this, we intro-duce a unified, source-aware framework for dynamic ren-dering that enforces the consistency of Gaussian primitives under explicit manifold constraints. We project predicted velocities onto physically grounded priors via efficient, par-allel inner solves: (i) a Helmholtz parameterization that separates divergence-free and potential-flow motion com-ponents; (ii) an anisotropic, compressible directional prior; and (iii) an affine family that disentangles rotation from isotropic scaling. Experiments on extensive benchmarks show consistent improvements over state-of-the-art meth-ods in reconstruction fidelity and temporal coherence. Our approach ensures physically realistic rendering, especially near contacts, and substantially reduces motion-boundary artifacts. 

## 1. Introduction 

Dynamic scene reconstruction and view synthesis from time-varying, multi-view imagery require a temporally con-sistent scene representation and an accompanying veloc-ity field, while enforcing manifold and contact constraints at interfaces. This problem is central to computer graph-ics because real scenes commonly exhibit non–volume pre-serving effects, e.g., compression/expansion, exposure drift, and growth/decay, as well as contact, which violate the source-free or strictly divergence-free assumptions often used in dynamic view synthesis. An approach that jointly handles compressibility and contact with an identifiable, well-conditioned estimator yields more stable training and higher-fidelity results, benefiting AR/VR telepresence, vi-sual effects, and robotics/digital-twin applications where

accuracy, physical plausibility, and turnaround time are crit-ical. Recent progress in dynamic view synthesis and re-construction has been driven by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) [3, 13, 32, 39–41, 45, 50, 55] and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [26], which encode time via learned de-formation fields or by estimating per-frame scene flow; later variants introduce kinematic priors (e.g., piecewise-rigid or affine parts) to stabilize optimization and improve extrap-olation. Continuity-based formulations further reduce re-liance on explicit flow supervision by matching residuals of the advection/continuity equation, yielding linear inner solves that scale to large datasets. However, prevailing methods typically assume source-free, volume-preserving motion and thus struggle with compressibility, appear-ance/exposure drift, and material growth or disappearance. Contact and friction are rarely modeled—non-penetration and tangential slip at interfaces are often ignored or approx-imated with soft penalties, leading to boundary artifacts. In addition, low-texture regions and spectrally overlapping bases can produce ill-conditioned normal equations, while richer or piecewise basis families impose substantial mem-ory overhead. Addressing this problem poses intertwined challenges in modeling, algorithms, and systems. Separating compress-ible flow from a source field is only weakly identifiable from image evidence. Reliable separation, especially under par-tial observability or low signal, requires carefully designed priors and temporal regularization. Manifold constraints add further difficulty: contact sets change discretely over time, making the objective nonsmooth and inducing combi-natorial interactions among motion priors. Moreover, mov-ing boundaries should satisfy mass conservation to prevent spurious flux. Contemporary rendering pipelines are pre-dominantly data-driven and thus noise-sensitive, weakening convergence guarantees. Robustly extracting approximate signed-distance isosurfaces, normals, and surface-normal velocities from monocular, noisy sequences is nontrivial. Each iteration need also solve inner problems at scale under tight memory budgets, motivating locality-aware methods. Finally, runtime and stability hinge on care-ful loss scheduling and hyperparameter tuning: overly hard priors introduce bias, overly soft priors reintroduce ambi-guity, and although stop-gradient projection improves sta-bility, unreliable contact detection can impede learning. Continuity-driven projection with compressibility, ex-plicit sources, and contact constraints introduces coupled challenges in identifiability, numerical conditioning, and systems design. Once a source term is allowed, divergence and sources become partially interchangeable; identifiabil-ity therefore depends on boundary conditions, sampling density, and image-gradient structure, and benefits from ap-propriate gauges and a global mass budget to preclude de-generacy. Geometric constraints and temporal regulariza-tion can be ill-conditioned in low-texture regions, under repetitive viewpoints, or with spectrally overlapping bases. Stable optimization requires normalization, damping, and structure-preserving preconditioning. Contact and friction further increase nonsmoothness; robustness improves with hysteresis in contact detection and a fallback from sticking to non-penetration to avoid solver chattering. Estimation remains fragile under illumination changes, occlusions, and sensor noise. Simple finite differences amplify artifacts and bias the projection, motivating multi-scale smoothing and robust loss functions, at the inevitable cost of attenuating high-frequency motion. When density changes induced by velocity divergence are confounded with genuine appearance or disappearance, and objects undergo contact that violates standard direc-tional priors, dynamic scene reconstruction from images alone becomes ill-posed. We address this by formulating a source-augmented continuity objective and jointly estimat-ing the velocity field and source–sink field so that they ex-plain observations in the 2D image plane or in 3D while remaining feasible on time-evolving constraint manifolds. Constraint surfaces are represented implicitly. Their ma-terial derivatives yield the surface-normal speed, and on the active set we impose non-penetration and Coulomb friction, restricting admissible motion to the tangent cone. Our motion priors instantiate convex, efficiently solvable subproblems, including directional, divergence-free, and compressible variants, together with manifold/contact pri-ors. The overall solver follows a projection-based strat-egy: each iteration computes closed-form solutions for the compressibility-aware priors and solves small second-order cone programs for manifold/contact constraints, producing a projected field via a local linear model. Compared with existing velocity-prior methods, this formulation disentan-gles divergence-driven density change from true appear-ance/disappearance, enforces physically plausible contact, and preserves analytic structure in all inner problems, yield-ing a stable, batched solver that integrates seamlessly with standard rendering pipelines. We present a continuity-driven, projection-based frame-work for dynamic reconstruction that accommodates com-pressible motion and appearance change while retaining closed-form, linear inner solves and avoiding explicit phys-ical simulation. At each time step, we augment the con-tinuity equation with an explicit source term and solve a unified convex least-squares projection onto velocity and source priors, yielding surrogate fields with analytic or ef-ficiently solvable structure. These projected fields super-vise learning in an alternating scheme: we compute the in-ner projection with stop-gradient, then update model pa-rameters in an outer loop using a projection loss and a continuity-consistency term. To enhance physical plausi-bility and control, we replace directional priors with man-ifold constraints, enforcing non-penetration and Coulomb friction via per-sample closed-form projections, with both pointwise and local-linear variants. To ensure identi-fiability between divergence and sources, we impose a global mass budget and a zero-mean gauge, complemented by boundary-aware bases and a curriculum that progres-sively enables compressibility and sources. Compared with existing velocity-prior approaches, our method yields greater stability, contact-aware behavior, and higher accu-racy across diverse dynamic scenes. Overall, our contribu-tions are as follows: • The introduction of a source term explicitly separates motion-driven density variation from genuine creation or annihilation to keep continuity, a capability not afforded by existing physics-based modelling methods. • The implicit-surface representation of obstacles with ex-plicitly constructed normal and tangential physical priors for computing the moving boundaries, thereby enforc-ing geometric constraints and enabling the handling of curved-surface obstacles. • The model of a non-penetration constraint and a Coulomb friction cone, thereby unifying sticking and sliding con-tact and incorporating physically accurate contact me-chanics into the priors. • Controllable priors, which integrate with the potential flow channels and temporal smoothing on density, effec-tively mitigating ambiguities among physical quantities and improving the decomposability and interpretability. 

## 2. Related Work 

Point-Based Rendering Conventional image-based re-construction underpins 3D scene synthesis [5, 9, 14, 20, 21, 33, 34, 52] but degrades as geometry and appearance be-come more complex [6, 10, 14, 19, 28, 29, 37, 44, 46, 48, 51, 53, 57, 61, 68]. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) learn an im-plicit, continuous volumetric density and radiance field pa-rameterized by multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) [2, 4, 23, 27, 8309 38–41, 45, 56, 64, 67], often surpassing geometry-driven methods in visual fidelity, but come with substantial train-ing and inference compute [2, 4, 27, 39–41, 45, 56, 64, 67]. In contrast, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [26] represents scenes explicitly with highly parallelizable Gaussian primi-tives, delivering real-time rendering while preserving strong visual quality. 

Dynamic Scene Rendering Unlike traditional meth-ods [9, 25, 30, 63, 69], neural rendering techniques improve image quality using neural networks but remain computa-tionally expensive [1, 2, 8, 11, 17, 22, 32, 38, 50, 54]. While some approaches accelerate rendering with efficient net-work structures [7, 13, 15, 16, 18, 31, 36, 40, 41, 43, 45, 49, 55, 59, 60], they still fall short of real-time performance in dynamic scenes. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [26] offers a promising alternative, using Gaussian functions to repre-sent scenes in a lightweight, point-based rendering frame-work. Furthermore, 3DGS introduces a GPU-accelerated rendering pipeline for real-time rendering. Recent work has extended 3DGS to dynamic scenes, but maintaining consis-tent point cloud associations over time remains a challenge, especially under fast motion or complex object changes, of-ten leading to blur and degraded quality. Although recent efforts have extended 3DGS to dynamic scenes by intro-ducing temporal models [12, 24, 35, 62, 65, 66], existing methods still fall short of capturing the underlying physics of motion, resulting in renderings that lack convincingly re-alistic dynamics.

4.3. Ablation Studies 

Source-Aware Compressible Flow. As shown in Table 3, enabling only the source-aware compressible flow (Table 3 (b)) delivers a substantial accuracy gain over the baseline at essentially no extra cost. PSNR improves by 3.53 dB, while LPIPS decreases by 74%. This improvement stems from attributing intensity changes to divergence instead of forcing them to be explained by flow alone, the model sup-presses stretching and smearing artifacts, preserves high-frequency detail, and yields more temporally coherent re-constructions. Because the inner projection remains a lin-ear least-squares solve, the configuration maintains training stability and wall-clock efficiency. 

Manifold and Contact Constrains. Enabling only mani-fold and contact constraints (Table 3 ( c)) improves recon-struction quality over the baseline at unchanged wall-clock cost. Our approach yields a +1.33 dB PSNR gain and 63% lower LPIPS. These gains stem from projecting predicted velocities onto the Gaussian primitive feasible set induced by non-penetration and frictional kinematics at implicit sur-faces, which curbs occlusions, sharpens boundaries, and promotes temporally consistent motion without introducing additional degrees of freedom.

## 5. Conclusion 

We have presented a source-aware, compressible dynamic scene rendering framework, which addresses real-world motion rendering where incompressible, source-free as-sumptions fail. Our approach combines three complemen-tary parameterizations with a lightweight projection solver. We first decompose the velocity field into divergence-free and potential-flow components and represent the source term in a dedicated basis, capturing compressible behav-ior while separating true growth from divergence-induced concentration changes. Furthermore, we confine the com-pressible component to a prescribed subspace, ensuring volumetric change occurs only along controlled directions and improving identifiability. Third, we decouple rota-tion, trace-free strain, and isotropic scaling, yielding a low-parameter, robust approximation of local motion. Our ap-proach achieves high rendering fidelity and temporal co-herence with physically realistic motion, while maintaining competitive training and inference cost. 8315 References
