# Seeing Both Sides: Towards Bidirectional Semantic Alignment for Open-Vocabulary Camouflaged Object Segmentation 

## Guohui Zhang Fuming Sun * Yu Zhao Yuqiu Kong Jing Sun Fasheng Wang Dalian Minzu University 

## Abstract 

Open-Vocabulary Camouflaged Object Segmentation (OV-COS) aims to segment camouflaged objects from unseen categories under textual guidance precisely. However, ex-isting methods often employ a unidirectional interaction strategy, where textual prompts guide the matching of vi-sual features. Such a design neglects the bidirectional in-teraction between visual and language modalities, making the model vulnerable to the semantic gap between image-level textual semantics and pixel-level segmentation cues, which in turn leads to severe semantic confusion in com-plex camouflaged scenarios. To address this challenge, we propose BaCLIP, a novel bidirectional semantic align-ment framework for OVCOS. At its core lies the Mutual Refinement and Enhancement Module (MREM), which es-tablishes bidirectional cross-attention between visual and textual features, enabling mutual semantic calibration to resolve ambiguity and strengthen cross-modal alignment. Moreover, we introduce an Adaptive Prompt that transforms refined textual embeddings into semantic-aware prompts for Segment Anything Model (SAM), enabling direct tex-tual guidance and improving mask precision. Experimen-tal results on the OVCamo benchmark demonstrate that BaCLIP consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance with a compact architecture, effectively mitigating seman-tic confusion and advancing the understanding of cross-modal camouflage perception. Our code is released at https://github.com/okmaybach/BaCLIP-CVPR2026.

It effectively mitigates semantic confu-sion between visual features and textual descriptions. • We design the Mutual Refinement and Enhancement Module, which employs bidirectional cross-attention to suppress semantic confusion and enhance cross-modal consistency. • We introduce adaptive textual prompts, tailored for SAM’s prompt encoder, to bridge the gap between image-level semantics and pixel-level segmentation precision. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. OVCOS 

COS [3, 7, 30] aims to detect and segment objects that are visually embedded in their surroundings. Early ad-vances were driven by convolutional networks (CNNs) [9, 15] and, more recently, by Transformer-based models [6, 8, 25], which progressively improved feature discrimina-tion, boundary refinement, and context modeling. How-ever, these methods operate at a fundamental level under a closed-set assumption—they are both trained and evalu-ated on a fixed set of object categories. This severely lim-its their applicability in real-world scenarios, where cam-ouflaged objects can belong to a virtually unlimited num-ber of unseen categories. Indeed, recent work has begun to push COS towards open-vocabulary / VLM-guided dense prediction (e.g., OVCOS) to handle unseen categories and language supervision [16, 29]. To address this limitation, the field has begun to embrace Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation (OVSS) [4, 12], which leverages natural language supervision instead of a fixed label set to recognize and segment objects from arbi-trary categories. Inspired by vision–language pre-training models such as CLIP [17], methods like DenseCLIP and OVSeg have demonstrated promising transfer capabilities by aligning visual and textual embeddings in a shared se-mantic space [11, 18]. However, a significant gap remains between general OVSS techniques and the specific chal-lenges of camouflage [16]. Most existing OVSS approaches 27656 follow a unidirectional text-to-vision paradigm [12], where language guides visual decoding without feedback. This often leads to semantic confusion in camouflage scenarios, where visual boundaries are subtle and categories are easily confused. The recent establishment of the OVCOS task [16] for-malizes this emerging direction. Pioneering methods such as OVCoser [16] adapt CLIP for camouflaged object seg-mentation by incorporating handcrafted visual cues. Su-CLIP [19] proposes Context-Aware Prompt and a seman-tic consistency loss to mitigate semantic confusion. How-ever, they still rely on unidirectional semantic propaga-tion [11, 23], which fails to fully resolve the inherent am-biguity among visually similar concepts [1]. In response, we propose a bidirectional guidance paradigm that not only uses language to direct visual segmentation [12] but also employs visual features to refine and disambiguate semantic representations. This dual interaction enables a more robust and precise understanding in open-vocabulary camouflage scenarios. 

2.2. Prompt-driven Segmentation Frameworks 

Since the advent of the SAM [10], prompt-driven segmen-tation frameworks have attracted significant research in-terest. SAM demonstrates strong generalization through its prompt-based interface, where segmentation masks are predicted from diverse inputs, such as points, boxes, or coarse masks. However, SAM remains inherently category-agnostic and lacks high-level semantic awareness. To en-hance its understanding of open-world semantics, several studies integrate SAM with vision–language models. For instance, SEEM [32] augments SAM with unified visual and textual prompts, enabling interactive segmentation and referring expression understanding. OpenSeg-SAM [4] and OVSAM [28] further incorporate CLIP-derived embed-dings to bridge category-level semantics with SAM’s mask generation pipeline. Despite these advancements, existing prompt-guided methods still follow a unidirectional design in which textual features influence visual decoding, but not vice versa, lead-ing to inconsistent feature alignment and degraded localiza-tion under complex conditions. In contrast, our method in-troduces a bidirectional semantic alignment mechanism ,enabling visual context to dynamically refine textual se-mantics and textual cues to reinforce visual discriminabil-ity. Consequently, this leads to a significant reduction in semantic confusion.

3.3. CCE 

While MREM focuses on semantic alignment, CCE en-hances fine-grained visual perception. As illustrated in Fig. 5, CCE refines the visual features Ev using a Multi-Frequency Multi-Scale Attention (MFMSA) module. This module is composed of the following stages: 

Multi-Scale Feature Extraction. The input visual fea-tures Ev are processed by three parallel branches to cap-ture multi-scale information. Features are downsampled at different rates ( Down 2, Down 4) and respectively passed through a convolution layer to produce multi-scale feature maps E1, E 2, and E3.

Multi-Frequency Channel Attention (MFCA). As 27658 shown in Fig. 5 (bottom left), each multi-scale feature Ei

(where i ∈ { 1, 2, 3}) is fed into the MFCA module. This module uses a DCT Bias to decompose Ei into multiple frequency components. These components are individually processed by parallel Fully-Connected (FC) layers. The re-sults are aggregated and used to create a channel attention map, which is multiplied by the original feature Ei to pro-duce the frequency-refined feature χi (i ∈ { 1, 2, 3}). 

Multi-Scale Differential Attention (MSDA). The re-fined features {χ1, χ 2, χ 3} are fed into the MSDA module (Fig. 5, bottom right) to enhance inter-scale boundary cues. The channel-recalibrated feature map χi is used to extract discriminative boundary cues at multiple scales in the spa-tial domain. Here, we introduce two learnable parameters (αi and βi) to control the information flow between the fore-ground and background, respectively, as follows: 

λi = Conv 3 (αi(χi ⊗ Fi) ⊕ βi(χi ⊗ Bi)) , (7) where Fi = Sigmoid (Conv 1(χi)) is a foreground atten-tion map, ⊗ denotes element-wise multiplication, ⊕ means element-wise addition. Consequently, the background at-tention map Bi can be derived by element-wise subtraction of Fi from a matrix of ones, that is, Bi = 1 − Fi. Fi-nally, to aggregate each refined feature from different scale branches, we apply upsampling to match the resolutions. For more stable training, we apply a residual connection af-ter the spatially refined feature map as follows:

3.4. Text Prompts for SAM 

To integrate the cross-modal semantic alignment into spa-tial segmentation, we extend the prompt mechanism of the SAM [10] to support textual prompts . Unlike the original SAM, which relies on spatial cues such as points, boxes, or masks, our framework further enhances the textual outputs of MREM, enabling them to function as semantic-aware prompts for the prompt encoder. 

Adaptive Prompt Generation. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the refined textual embeddings Et ∈ RN ×C (output from MREM) are transformed into adaptive prompts. First, Et

is processed by a SelfAttention layer to capture inter-token dependencies: 

Pt = SelfAttention (Et). (9) Then, a Projection layer and SAM’s Prompt Encoder (whose parameters are frozen) map these features into the final semantic-aware prompt embeddings E∗ 

> t

Prompt-Visual Fusion. During the decoding stage, the enhanced visual features E∗ 

> v

from the CCE and the gen-erated textual prompt embeddings E∗ 

> t

This process grounds the semantic meaning of the text in spatial context, allowing the model to delineate object re-gions that match the described category or attributes. By replacing manual spatial prompts with adaptive tex-tual prompts, SAM becomes open-vocabulary and semanti-cally guided. This design not only enhances its generaliza-tion to unseen categories but also improves segmentation consistency when handling camouflaged objects that lack distinct spatial priors.

Lseg = LBCE + LDice , (11) where LBCE is the BCE loss and LDice is the Dice loss. 

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Implementation Details 

The proposed model is implemented using the PyTorch framework and is trained on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB memory). During training, each input image is resized to 384 × 384 , with the CLIP parameters remaining frozen. We employ the AdamW optimizer with hyperparameters: batch size = 4 , learning rate = 3 × 10 −5,epochs = 30 . A cosine annealing scheduler adjusts the learning rate. Basic data augmentations, including random flipping, rotating, and color jittering, are introduced to pre-process training data.

## 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose the BaCLIP framework to tackle the semantic confusion in Open-Vocabulary Camouflaged Object Segmentation. The framework employs a bidirec-tional feature interaction mechanism, with the Mutual Re-finement and Enhancement Module at its core, using bidi-rectional cross-modal attention to alleviate semantic confu-sion and improve the model’s ability to differentiate camou-flaged categories. Additionally, the Camo Context Extrac-tor module, utilizing multi-frequency and multi-scale atten-tion, enhances feature extraction in complex backgrounds. To improve flexibility and prevent overfitting, we integrate SAM’s prompt encoder and mask decoder, keeping their parameters frozen, and adapt MREM-enhanced textual fea-tures to SAM’s prompt input dimensions.
