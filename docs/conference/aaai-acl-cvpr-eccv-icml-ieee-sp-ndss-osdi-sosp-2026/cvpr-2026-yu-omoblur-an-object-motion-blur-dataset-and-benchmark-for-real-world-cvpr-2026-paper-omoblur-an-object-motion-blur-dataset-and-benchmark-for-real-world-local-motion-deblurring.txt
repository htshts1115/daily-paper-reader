OMoBlur: An Object Motion Blur Dataset and Benchmark for Real-World
Local Motion Deblurring
Dingchuan Yu, Jiatong Li, Jingwen Zhou, Zhengyue Zhuge, Yueting Chen, Qi Li

Abstract
Object motion blur in static scenes is spatially heteroge-
neous, differing from conventional deblurring problems yet
frequently occurring in real handheld capture scenarios.
Existing datasets either rely on costly beam-splitting cap-
ture with residual misalignment or employ synthetic blur
that fails to model the continuous photon-integration pro-
cess during exposure. To overcome these limitations, we
introduce OMoBlur, a physically grounded dataset that em-
ulates realistic exposure integration via programmable sen-
sor control, ensuring close alignment between synthetic
and real blur distributions.
OMoBlur provides 20,000
blur–sharp–mask pairs covering diverse object motion
types. Leveraging this dataset, we further propose OMD-
Net, an object-motion-aware deblurring network that inte-
grates a Motion–Appearance Extract Block, a Flow-Guided
Gate Predictor, and an Adaptive Gated Fusion mecha-
nism. This design enables the network to selectively restore
blurred regions while preserving static backgrounds, with-
out requiring pixel-accurate mask annotations. Extensive
experiments demonstrate that OMoBlur’s physically faith-
ful data collection and large-scale diversity significantly
enhance the network’s generalization to real-world mo-
tion blur, establishing OMoBlur and OMDNet as a robust
benchmark and practical solution for local motion deblur-
ring. The dataset and code is publicly released at https:
//yudingchuan.github.io/OMoBlur_homepage/.
1. Introduction
Motion blur generally falls into two categories: camera
shake blur and object motion blur. Camera shake blur is
spatially continuous across the image and has seen remark-
able progress in restoration [3, 5, 6, 9, 17, 18, 24, 30, 32].
In contrast, object motion blur arises when independently
moving objects appear in a static scene with a static cam-
era. This blur is typically localized and discontinuous: mul-
tiple objects may move with different velocities and di-

ReLoBlur [19], the first dataset for locally motion-

inconsistencies, leading to residual misalignments that de-

Unlike fully real-captured datasets,
GoPro [24] synthesizes blur by accumulating consecutive
frames. Although it includes object-motion scenes and is
widely used, the accumulation relies on a simplified model
and creates a domain gap to real blur, limiting generaliza-
tion. Another common approach generates blur by convolv-
ing a sharp image with blur kernel(s) [6, 7, 31]. However,
this method faces inherent limitations in modeling object
motion. First, while spatially varying kernels can theoret-
ically represent non-uniform blur, the arbitrary kernels do
not adhere to the kinematic constraints of physical motion
(e.g. the cycloidal path of a rolling wheel). Second, com-
plex motions like a spinning ball cause pixels to appear or

Building on OMoBlur, we present OMDNet, a single-
image deblurring framework designed for local motion blur
removal and static background preservation without manual
blur region annotation. The architecture comprises two core
components: a Motion–Appearance Extract Block (MAEB)
for rich feature representation and flexible propagation, and
a Flow-guided Gate Predictor (FGP). The FGP guides the
motion branch within the MAEB to supply higher-quality
motion information, which in turn supports more reliable
blur region gating. Additionally, we introduce an Adaptive
Gated Fusion (AGF) mechanism, which leverages coarse
blur masks as soft supervision to further enhance the pre-
cision of gate prediction.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We establish OMoBlur, the first physically faithful,
accumulation-based object motion blur dataset that sim-

with almost full exposure. It substantially narrows the
synthetic-to-real gap and offers 20K+ pairs covering di-
verse motion types with static backgrounds.
• We design an object-motion deblurring network OMD-
Net, which elegantly integrates motion-aware represen-
tation learning with adaptive gating to restore blurred re-
gions while preserving sharp backgrounds.
• Extensive experiments demonstrate that our OMoBlur
dataset enables strong generalization to real-world local
motion blur, establishing it as an effective resource for
both training and evaluation.

2. Related Work
2.1. Motion Blur Datasets
Kernel-based methods initially modeled motion blur us-
ing a single global blur kernel [2, 29], and were later ex-
tended to patch or pixel-wise kernels [1, 8, 13, 31, 40, 44].
Their reliance on the spatially smooth kernel assumption re-
stricts the effectiveness on real-world object motion. Multi-
frame accumulation methods generate blur by averaging
consecutive sharp frames [24, 30], with frame interpola-
tion often used beforehand to produce visually continuous
blur trajectories [25, 28, 33, 42]. While multi-frame meth-
ods excel at temporal modeling and are the mainstream for
dataset generation, the potential for high-fidelity blur sim-
ulation remains unrealized in existing datasets, hindering
real-world generalization. Fully real-captured datasets (e.g.
RealBlur [27], ReLoBlur [19], RBI [43]) use coaxial beam-
splitters to simultaneously capture blur-sharp pairs. How-
ever, significant challenges in achieving pixel-wise align-
ment, both geometrically and photometrically, limit their
performance for supervised deblurring.
2.2. Object Motion Deblurring Methods
Restoring motion blur caused by independently moving ob-
jects requires recovering blurred regions while preserving
sharp backgrounds. LBAG [19] introduces a simple gate
block to localize blurred areas using ground-truth masks
from their ReLoBlur dataset. LMD-ViT [20] improves effi-
ciency via adaptive pruning, retaining transformer windows
likely to contain blur and avoiding computation elsewhere.
MUGNet [34] uses a probabilistic model to represent mo-
tion uncertainty, improving the reliability of blur map es-
timation. PGDN [12] trains an additional self-supervised
network to predict a 3-parameter blur kernel, which serves
as an explicit prior to guide deblurring.
3. OMoBlur Dataset
In this section, we first examine the physical exposure pro-
cess to develop a model that more accurately reflects real
motion blur formation. We then discuss the characteristics
of object motion blur. Finally, we describe the image acqui-
sition procedure and the construction of the dataset.

3.2. Object Motion Discussion
We adopt a rigid-body kinematic perspective to catego-
rize object motion according to its projection characteristics
onto the image plane.
• In-plane motion: Translation perpendicular to the opti-
cal axis and rotation about it. The resulting blur stems
from a sequence of well-defined 2D image transforma-
tions, which preserve the scene’s relative structure and
thus present a more constrained deblurring problem.
• Out-of-plane motion: Motion involving a translational
component along the optical axis or rotational compo-
nents of yaw/pitch. Such motion introduces inherent 3D
geometric effects that cannot be captured by 2D motion
models, such as scaling from depth variation and self-
occlusions from rotation, leading to a severely ill-posed
inverse problem.
This classification guides our data collection in Sec. 3.3,
ensuring our dataset comprehensively covers object motion
patterns across the full spectrum of deblurring difficulty.

Guided by the discussion in Sec. 3.2 , we capture pedes-

Chinese calligraphy/paintings and record them with hand-
held motion to enrich complex motion and scene textures.
Our dataset construction pipeline (Fig. 3) is as follows.
The blurry image is produced by averaging consecutive
RAW frames and converting the result to RGB via the ISP.
Each single RAW frame is then processed through the ISP
to RGB: the middle frame serves as sharp ground-truth,
while consecutive frames are used for optical flow estima-
tion via MeFlow [35] to derive blur masks. Despite care-
ful processing, optical flow errors in areas with repetitive
textures or dark backgrounds can still cause mask inaccura-
cies. Therefore, we introduce AGF mechanism to mitigate
unreliable mask supervision during training and manually
correct inaccurate masks in the test set to ensure fair evalu-
ation. After carefully filtering invalid scenes, we release a
total of over 20,000 blur-sharp-mask image pairs.
In summary, through systematic blur modeling and pre-
cise acquisition engineering, we introduce, to the best of
our knowledge, the first large-scale, high-quality dataset of
physically faithful, accumulation-based object motion blur.

Motion-Appearance Extract Block.
We employ a NAF-
Block (from NAFNet [4]) as a shared stem in the MAEB
(Fig. 5) for efficient feature extraction, then split the path-
way into two branches. The appearance branch uses an-
other NAFBlock, while the motion branch is processed by a
Differential Transposed Attention Module (Diff-TAM). The
design of Diff-TAM is inspired by differential amplifiers in
electrical engineering and differential attention[36] in lan-
guage modeling, a mechanism that suppresses background
responses by comparing paired attention paths. This prop-
erty makes it highly effective for isolating motion patterns,
thereby providing high-quality input for subsequent optical
flow prediction. For efficiency, we implement a Restormer-
style transposed attention that operates along channel di-
mensions and restrict Diff-TAM to the MAEB rather than
throughout the backbone. Architectural details are provided
in Appendix B.1.

4.2. Adaptive Gated Fusion Mechanism
Existing methods for complete motion region prediction[19,
34] rely on dual supervision: (1) the predicted gate matches
ground-truth mask, and (2) the gated restoration aligns with
the ground-truth sharp image. This design has its draw-
backs. First, algorithmic masks contain inaccuracies [12],
which makes mask supervision unreliable unless using
labor-intensive manual annotations[20]. Second, blur-sharp
pair imperfections like misalignments and noise cause the
gate to activate incorrectly in static backgrounds.
Motivated by these observations, we propose an adap-
tive gated fusion (AGF) mechanism that uses masks only as
a soft prior during training. Concretely, we compute two
gated predictions in training, both supervised by the sharp

5. Experiments and Analysis
Models are trained on OMoBlur dataset, which contains
18,910 image pairs for training and 1,354 pairs for test-
ing. We use the Adam optimizer with a batchsize of 8 to
train models for 500K iterations. The initial learning rate
is 1 × 10−4 and is decayed at predefined milestones. Fol-
lowing BAPC [19], we adopt a similar blur-aware cropping
strategy to sample 256×256 patches that are more likely to
cover blurred regions as inputs for all models.
5.1. Benchmarking Deblurring Methods
We evaluate the proposed OMDNet and open-sourced
baseline methods on the OMoBlur test set at the na-
tive resolution of 1920 × 360 using 1 NVIDIA GeForce
RTX 4090 GPU. These deblurring methods include CNN-
based [4, 10, 19], Transformer-based [20, 37], and Mamba-
based method [16].
We measure reconstruction fi-
delity using PSNR/SSIM and weighted PSNR/SSIM [20]
which focus on blurred regions. Additionally, we report
LPIPS(VGG)[39] and DISTS[11] to better assess percep-
tual quality in the context of deblurring.
As summarized in Tab. 1, OMDNet achieves the best
performance across all metrics.
Its most substantial
gains are on weighted PSNR/SSIM and perceptual metrics
LPIPS/DISTS, highlighting exceptional capability in restor-
ing blurred regions and producing visually realistic outputs.

focuses on severely blurred areas and preserves the back-
ground by refraining from forcibly correcting the inherent

5.2. Generalization Analysis
Generalization of Models Trained on OMoBlur.
We re-
construct the middle frame, which is the physically consis-
tent choice for solving motion-direction ambiguity [15, 21].
ReLoBlur instead targets the beginning frame. This mis-
match limits cross-dataset score comparability, so we focus
on qualitative comparisons.
As illustrated in Fig. 6(b), models trained on OMoBlur
consistently remove the cycloidal trails of rapidly rotat-
ing wheels. The reconstructions even appear cleaner than
the ground-truth in ReLoBlur. This indicates that our syn-
thesized blur distribution closely matches real motion blur
and that the sharp references used for training are suffi-
ciently clean to support high fidelity restoration. In harder
cases such as rapidly moving small text, OMDNet shows
stronger dealiasing and detail preservation, benefiting from
the multi-frame flow-guided supervision. Additional visual
results are provided in Appendix C.2.
Superiority of OMoBlur over Other Synthetic Strate-
gies.
To assess the validity and necessity of our time-
integrated signal-accumulation blur model together with
carefully engineered capture-side refinements, we conduct a
cross-dataset training. Two widely used baselines, MIMO-
UNet and Restormer, are trained on three sources: GoPro,
OMoBlur-Frame Interpolated version (OMoBlur-FI), and
OMoBlur, and then evaluated on ReLoBlur to examine gen-
eralization. All ReLoBlur images are downsampled by 2×
to ensure a consistent input scale. Specifically, OMoBlur-
FI follows the REDS protocol [25]: we reduce the original
500 fps sequences in our dataset to 125 fps, apply 16× in-
terpolation with the high-performing EMA-VFI [38], and
synthesize blur. For GoPro, we use the official pre-trained
models released by MIMO-UNet and Restormer.
Figure 7 presents representative scenes from ReLoBlur.
Restormer trained on GoPro shows minimal restoration
in these cases, and MIMO-UNet trained on GoPro pro-
duces noticeable artifacts (particularly evident in Fig. 7(b)),
likely reflecting overfitting to the physically inconsistent
blur distribution. Although FI-based blur appears as nat-
ural as OMoBlur (see Fig. 8), its distribution and physi-
22632


cal formation differ from real blur.
This mismatch lim-
its generalization.
Consequently, for in-plane translation
on low-frequency content (e.g. smooth white-car contours
in Fig. 7(a)), models trained on OMoBlur-FI show some
deblurring.
For in-plane rotation with rich textures (e.g.
wheels in Fig. 7(a)) and for out-of-plane motion (e.g. a
turning black car in Fig. 7(c)), they largely fail to remove
blur. By contrast, models trained on our OMoBlur main-
tain strong performance across these scenes. In the walk-
ing scene with severely blurred pocket zipper (Fig. 7(d)),
prior work on ReLoBlur use complex network designs, yet
the results are still limited [20, 34]. Baselines trained on
our OMoBlur handle it cleanly, showing the strength of our
large-scale, physically-faithful dataset.

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce OMoBlur, the first large-scale,
physically grounded dataset for object motion blur, along
with OMDNet, a novel deblurring framework tailored to
this challenge. By optimizing the acquisition strategy to
closely approximate the photon-integrated exposure pro-
cess, OMoBlur significantly narrows the gap between syn-
thetic and real-world blur and provides a high-fidelity
benchmark for localized motion deblurring. The proposed
OMDNet effectively integrates motion-aware feature ex-
traction with adaptive gated fusion, which achieves accu-
rate blur removal while maintaining background integrity.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that our dataset and
method achieve state-of-the-art performance. We believe
that our physically consistent dataset construction method-
ology can benefit a broader range of vision tasks and inspire
the development of more effective real-world deblurring so-
lutions.
