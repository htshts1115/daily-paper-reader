# Abstract 

High-resolution human video matting aims to predict ac-curate alpha mattes for semi-transparent regions while en-suring temporal consistency across frames. Despite notable progress, current methods still fail to achieve a satisfactory trade-off between quality and efficiency, with limitations in subject stability, temporal modeling, and computational cost. In this paper, we introduce μMatting, an innova-tive resolution-agnostic two-stage framework for video mat-ting: (1) coarse matte localization using a portrait-aware masked autoencoder; (2) refinement of critical regions via sparse 3D convolution, augmented by a temporal modula-tor that injects global spatio-temporal cues for enhanced consistency and contextual awareness. From data perspec-tive, existing research remains limited by the insufficient quality of datasets, including (1) inaccurate alpha frac-tional values resulting from imperfect annotation, and (2) visual inconsistencies arising from arbitrary foreground-background compositions that lack natural coherence. To address this, we introduce αMatte4K, a large-scale 4K-resolution human video matting dataset, which achieves ac-curate annotations and physical consistency through physi-cally based rendering (PBR). Extensive experiments show that μMatting surpasses state-of-the-art methods in ac-curacy and spatio-temporal consistency, while αMatte4K boosts baseline performance, driving applications in real-world scenarios. The project is open-sourced at https: //github.com/kadatec/mu-Matting .

The second group consists of chunk-based meth-ods, like VMFormer[13], which improve temporal reason-ing by jointly processing multiple frames, but multi-frame self-attention becomes extremely expensive in computa-tion and memory at high resolutions. To address these challenges, we propose μMatting , a novel framework tailored for high-resolution video mat-ting, as shown in Fig.2(Top). In contrast to prior works, 

μMatting is designed to operate directly on 4K content while maintaining temporal continuity. To achieve this, we revisit the temporal structure of natural video content. As illustrated in Fig.2(Bottom), our empirical analysis re-veals that temporal variation is sparsely distributed: only 13.7% of pixels exhibit noticeable change over time, pre-dominantly near boundaries and fine-grained regions, while most of the foreground remains static. This observation motivates our staged design for processing high-resolution videos. In the first stage, a coarse matte is generated us-ing a pre-trained masked autoencoder (MAE) backbone [9] that encodes strong human-centric priors, enabling robust foreground localization. In the second stage, alpha val-ues in specific regions, such as hair, clothing edges, and translucent areas, are refined. This is achieved via 3D sparse convolutions that aggregate spatio-temporal features across neighboring frames. To enhance temporal coherence and global awareness, we further introduce a Temporal Sparse Context Modulator (TSCM), which compensates for con-textual gaps caused by sparse sampling. Beyond architectural shortcomings, the absence of high-quality datasets further hinders progress. Although all methods strive to pursue temporal coherence and prediction accuracy, the available data lack these qualities. According to the classical composition formulation I = αF + (1 −

α)B, accurate and temporally coherent alpha supervision are fundamental to effective supervised learning. However, current matting datasets, e.g. VideoMatte240K (VM) [15] and HHM50K[22], construct alpha mattes via manual anno-tation, matting algorithms or chroma-key technique, all of which result in imprecise and noisy annotations, as shown in Fig. 1. Furthermore, due to the complexity of video dataset construction, some datasets, such as VM, provide only human foregrounds and corresponding alpha mattes, requiring compositing with external background datasets. This composition can lead to physically implausible spatial cues, which may limit prediction accuracy and generaliza-tion to real-world scenarios. To address this root cause, we present αMatte4K , a first-of-its-kind 4K-resolution video matting dataset featuring both accurate and temporally coherent α mattes. Unlike prior datasets, αMatte4K is constructed entirely through PBR via a four-stage pipeline, encompassing a wider range of subjects, motion dynamics, scenes, and camera settings. However, when processing data with extremely high reso-lution and dense details, existing models require downsam-pling or per-frame processing, which undermines the spatial precision and temporal consistency. In summary, our contributions are as follows: 1. We construct a large-scale human video matting dataset αMatte4K, which contains 900 videos of 115K high-resolution (2160×3840) frames with pixel-accurate al-pha mattes via PBR. It covers a wide range of foreground subjects and background scenes. To the best of our knowl-edge, this is the largest and most diverse dataset for this task and is expected to facilitate future research. 2. We propose a novel framework μMatting, which en-ables uncompressed 4K video matting for the first time. It combines robust subject prior estimation with selective spatio-temporal refinement in detail-critical regions. # 2. Related Works 

2.1. Matting Datasets 

Existing image/video matting datasets are typically built via manual annotation[17, 23, 24], matting/generation algorithms[2, 22, 26], or chroma-key[2, 15], as illustrated in Fig.3. For static images, representative datasets include AIM[24], D646[17], PhotoMatte85[22], MAGICK[2], and SynthHuman[20]. The development trend ileans toward expanding data scales, with production methods gradually shifting from traditional manual annotation to algorithms or PBR[5, 20] to balance quantity and quality. In video matting, scale is limited by the difficulty of annotating con-secutive frames. Currently, the most widely used dataset is VideoMatte240K(VM)[15], which is divided into VM-SD and VM-HD by resolution. Based on chroma-key technol-ogy, VM scales its data volume through the composition of annotated foregrounds and external backgrounds, yet this stitching approach introduces FG-BG inconsistency. Differ-ent from VM, CRGNN[23] offers carefully annotated real-world data and is served as a standard benchmark for prac-tical evaluation. As 4K+ inference grows, existing training datasets reveal clear limitations in annotation quality (e.g., artifacts, insufficient precision) and temporal consistency, which restricts the development of the matting field. 

2.2. Video Matting and Temporal Consistency 

Video matting[4, 6, 11, 12, 14, 16] requires consistent pre-diction across frames, making it more complex than static image matting. DVM[21] introduces the spatio-temporal feature aggregation module for temporal alignment, but its reliance on extra trimaps limits scalability. Like DVM, certain mask-guided methods require preprocessed guid-ance masks as an additional input to assist inference. For example, MaGGIe[7] and MatAnyone[25] rely heavily on SAM2[19], where imperfect masks can severely degrade matting quality. In terms of maintaining temporal consis-tency, later methods utilize memory modules to fusion in-formation from previous frame to retain continuity, such as RVM[16]. BiMatting[18], built on RVM, lowers computa-tion while preserving visual quality. Recently, transformer-based models advance temporal reasoning: AdaM[14] pro-cesses frames sequentially with memory from past sev-eral frames, while VMFormer[13] jointly models multiple frames using feature- and query-based attention. 

2.3. High-Resolution Matting 

High-resolution matting imposes significant computational demands, often exceeding GPU memory limits and neces-sitating approximations. Some solutions such as RVM[16] downsample and later upsample, sacrificing fine details. Other works adopt local refinement strategies to focus computation on detail-critical regions. For example, BGMv2[15], inspired by PointRend[10], applies patch-wise refinement, though it lacks inter-patch continuity and suf-fers at boundaries. SparseMat[22] improves upon this by performing pixel-level selection for precise refinement. To address spatial and temporal challenges jointly, we propose a principled 3D convolutional framework that mod-els long-range dependencies across time while preserving high-resolution alpha fidelity, bridging the gap in video mat-ting under high-resolution settings. 

# 3. Dataset-αMatte4K 

As discussed earlier, most existing video matting datasets suffer from two critical limitations: (1) imprecise alpha an-notations, resulting from manual labeling or chroma keying; and (2) compositing-based pipelines that neglect the physi-cal consistency between foreground and background, lead-ing to mismatches in lighting, geometry, and motion. To address these issues, we construct a large-scale video mat-ting dataset designed to fulfill the following goals: accurate alpha mattes, physically plausible details, temporal coher-ence, and full 4K resolution. To achieve these objectives, we adopt a structured four-stage pipeline (see Fig. 4(b)). 

3.1. Dataset Construction Pipeline 

1. Digital Character Setup . We begin with 30 high-quality human models sourced from MetaHuman 1, featuring di-verse appearances in skin tone, age, hairstyle, and clothing. These characters are then animated using skeletal motion from Mixamo 2, covering a broad range of natural behav-iors, including walking, dancing, and interacting. 2. 3D Scene Construction . We construct 22 large-scale urban and natural 3D environments in Unreal Engine (UE),

3.2. Accuracy and Fidelity 

We adopt a PBR pipeline to compute per-pixel alpha, yield-ing numerically precise ground truth. Unlike hand-crafted masks, physically rendered mattes preserve fine details and boundaries even at full 4K resolution, especially in chal-lenging areas such as hair and motion blur, which are dif-ficult, almost impossible, to manually annotate (Fig. 4(d)). Beyond accuracy, high-fidelity video matting also requires adherence to physical plausibility across time and space. We explicitly model effects such as hair dynamics, spatial layout, physical perspective, lighting, and shadows. 

3.3. Diversity and Scalability 

In addition to fidelity, diversity is equally crucial for model generalization. Our dataset’s diversity is systematically en-gineered across four core axes: 

Character assets : αMatte4K includes a wide variety of human subjects with different genders, ages, body types, hairstyles, and clothing styles. 

Motion types : αMatte4K incorporates a broad spec-trum of activities, including dancing, standing, walking, and multi-person interactions, with motion clipping, splicing, and speed variation applied to the these motion patterns to further enhance diversity. 

Environmental contexts : Scenes cover indoor settings such as bedrooms and offices, and outdoor scenarios includ-ing streets and open areas. Lighting conditions encompass natural light (e.g., sunset, sunny, and cloudy weather) and artificial illumination such as indoor warm/cool light, fully accounting for the complexity of real-world scenarios. 

Camera settings : In terms of shot sizes, our dataset in-cludes headshots, half-body, and full-body views, covering a wide range of common human subjects; for camera move-ments, it encompasses static shots as well as dynamic move-ments such as pushing in, pulling out, orbiting, and track-ing. This diversity ensures that the dataset can simulate a wide range of real-world shooting scenarios and enhances robustness across heterogeneous filming conditions. Overall, αMatte4K contains: 900 video sequences with over 115K frames; 4K resolution; Diverse humans, mo-tions, backgrounds, and camera movements; Accurately an-notated alpha mattes. # 4. Method-μMatting 

We propose μMatting, a novel high-resolution video mat-ting framework, specifically designed for spatio-temporal reasoning under computational constraints. As illustrated in Fig.5, our architecture consists of two main components: (a) a Coarse Alpha Predictor (CAP) that performs robust subject localization and generates a coarse matte A↓ 

> c

. We also obtain key-region masks K for refinement by erosion and dilation from Ac; and (b) a Fractional Alpha Refiner (FAR) that refines K using sparse 3D convolutions and TSCM, producing a refined matte Ad. The final output matte A is obtained by combining the coarse prediction Ac

and the refined result Ad.

5.4. Ablation Studies 

To rigorously assess the contribution of each component in our framework, we conduct a series of ablation studies by progressively removing or replacing key modules. The ob-jective is to isolate and quantify their respective impact on foreground stability, edge quality, and temporal consistency. 

5.4.1. Effectiveness of CAP 

We adopt the modular design of SparseMat and replace its original Low-resolution Prior Network (LPN) with our CAP, while keeping its Sparse High-resolution Module (SHM) unchanged. This setup allows us to directly eval-uate whether CAP yields more reliable low-resolution pre-dictions than LPN. All models are trained on HHM50K and evaluated on the HHM2K test set under both low-resolution (LR) and high-resolution (HR) settings. As shown in Tab. 4, CAP consistently outperforms LPN across all metrics. Vi-sual comparisons in Fig. 9 further confirm that CAP+SHM produces cleaner and more complete foreground masks, es-pecially in challenging regions such as hair. These results demonstrate that CAP improves the structural integrity and independence of human foregrounds, effectively reducing internal voids and suppressing external artifacts.

# 6. Conclusion 

Current video matting methods heavily rely on the mini-mally enhanced VM dataset. To address this, we intro-duce αMatte4K: a large-scale, diverse dataset with high-precision matte supervision specifically for high-resolution human video matting, overcoming long-standing limita-tions and introducing a novel data paradigm. For 4K pro-cessing, we propose μMatting—a framework delivering ex-ceptional alpha fidelity and temporal consistency without resolution loss. Extensive benchmarks confirm μMatting’s superiority.
