Abstract 

FrontVeg V2 is an open-source, training-free software framework for foreground-aware zero-shot segmentation of plant traits in high-resolution images of trellised crops. The pipeline combines monocular depth estimation, auto-matic foreground extraction using Valley-Aware Depth Thresholding, tiled zero-shot segmentation, Graph-Based Mask Assembly, and geometry-aware fusion. This design enables plant organs and disease symptoms to be seg-mented while reducing detections arising from neighboring vegetation rows. The current implementation integrates Depth Anything V2 (DAV2) and SAM3 and can be used through both command-line batch processing and a Napari graphical interface. FrontVeg V2 provides a reusable framework for multi-crop, multi-trait digital phenotyping without task-specific model retraining. 

Keywords: 

Plant Phenotyping, Zero-Shot Segmentation, Foundation Models, Monocular Depth Estimation, Trellised Crops

1. Motivation and significance 

Accurate plant trait segmentation is an important step in digital pheno-typing, as it provides the basis for measuring traits such as leaf area, fruit number, and disease severity from images. In trellised cropping systems, such as vineyards and modern orchards, this task remains challenging because a single side-view image can contain several vegetation rows with very similar visual appearances. As a result, segmen-tation models can detect organs from neighboring rows together with the target vegetation. This is particularly problematic when the objective is to characterize only the foreground crop row. At the same time, phenotyping images are often acquired at high resolution to preserve small structures such as leaves, fruits, and disease symptoms, making direct full-image inference difficult for many vision models. Monocular depth foundation models offer a complementary source of infor-mation by estimating relative scene depth directly from a single RGB image, 2without requiring additional depth sensors. Recent vision foundation mod-els provide strong zero-shot and open-vocabulary segmentation capabilities without task-specific training. Models such as SAM and SAM2 [1, 9], as well as open-vocabulary approaches such as GroundingDINO+SAM [11, 15, 1] and SAN [16], have reduced the need for task-specific segmentation mod-els. In parallel, monocular depth estimation has advanced through mod-els such as MiDaS, ZoeDepth, Metric3D, Depth Anything, and UniDepth [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]. In agricultural imaging, existing datasets and methods have addressed fruit, leaf, grape cluster, and disease segmentation, including Min-neApple [17], WGISD [20], GrapeSAM [21], ViViD-5K [22], PlantSeg [23], and disease-specific approaches [24, 25, 26, 27]. However, these approaches generally focus on a specific crop, trait, disease, sensor, or segmentation task, and do not explicitly use scene geometry to identify the foreground vegeta-tion row. FrontVeg V2 builds on these complementary research directions by combining monocular depth estimation with tiled zero-shot segmentation in a training-free pipeline. The estimated depth is used as a geometric prior to restrict the segmentation to the foreground crop row, while open-vocabulary prompts allow the same framework to target different plant traits and disease symptoms. FrontVeg V2 is a training-free software pipeline designed for foreground-aware zero-shot segmentation of plant traits in high-resolution images of trellised crops. The software combines monocular depth estimation, auto-matic foreground extraction, tiled zero-shot segmentation, graph-based mask assembly, and geometry-aware mask fusion. Its main objective is not to re-place existing foundation models, but to provide a processing framework that makes their zero-shot predictions more suitable for structured agricul-tural scenes. The current implementation uses Depth Anything V2 [6] for monocular depth estimation and SAM3 [8] for zero-shot instance segmenta-tion, while their model paths are provided externally to the software rather than being fixed in the source code. From a scientific perspective, FrontVeg V2 provides a reusable framework for studying how monocular scene geometry can be combined with vision foundation models for plant phenotyping. It allows researchers to evaluate zero-shot segmentation on different crops and plant traits without retraining the complete pipeline for each new task. The configurable prompts, tiling pa-rameters, depth-processing parameters, and externally provided model paths also make it possible to test different models and processing configurations within the same workflow. In this way, the software can be used not only as a practical phenotyping tool, but also as an experimental framework for investigating geometry-aware zero-shot segmentation in agricultural environ-3ments. The software is also associated with our research presented in the CVPPA 2026 Workshop at ECCV [28], where the proposed framework was evalu-ated for zero-shot plant trait segmentation in trellised crops using side-view monocular RGB images. This provides an initial research application of the software, while the SoftwareX version is intended to make the complete im-plementation more accessible and reusable for future agricultural imaging studies. The software was evaluated on RGB images of grapevine, apricot, and apple trees acquired under heterogeneous field conditions. The evaluation dataset comprises 158 images from six geographic locations in France, Spain, Italy, Belgium, and Switzerland, including grapevine images from Colmar and apricot images from Amarine (France), as well as apple images from the DeepPhenoTree–CVPPAData Challenge dataset (DOI: https://doi.org/ 10.57745/NORPF1 ) [12]. Images were acquired with different camera sys-tems, illumination conditions, and viewpoints. The benchmark covers leaf and fruit segmentation for all three crops, and black rot and downy mildew segmentation for grapevine. Ground-truth masks (Table 2) were manually annotated at pixel level using LabelBox [18], following guidelines established with plant scientists and agronomists. Approximately 200 hours of annota-tion produced around 10,000 manually delineated object instances.

The same software implementation is therefore intended to support both batch processing for experiments and interactive inspection of individ-ual images. 

2. Software description 

FrontVeg V2 is a modular Python software for foreground-aware zero-shot plant trait segmentation in high-resolution agricultural images. Its core li-brary is decoupled from the graphical interface, enabling both batch process-ing on HPC clusters and interactive local visualization. 

2.1. Software architecture 

The pipeline is organized into two parallel processing branches followed by a final fusion step. Figure 1 illustrates the overall architecture. The soft-ware processes a monocular RGB image and a user-defined textual prompt (e.g., leaf , fruit ) to output an instance segmentation map restricted to the foreground vegetation row. The architecture comprises four main modules.   

> Figure 1: Overall architecture of FrontVeg V2. The software processes the input RGB image through a depth-based foreground extraction branch and a tiled zero-shot segmen-tation branch. The two outputs are combined in the final fusion step to retain plant traits belonging to the foreground vegetation row.

Geometry-Aware Foreground Extraction: The first branch extracts a binary foreground mask based on depth information. The software loads a 5monocular depth foundation model (DAV2 [6]) from an externally specified path. This design allows users to swap the depth estimator without modifying the pipeline source code. The model estimates a relative depth map from the RGB image. To automate the selection of the depth threshold, the software applies the Valley-Aware Depth Thresholding (VADT) algorithm. The software con-verts the depth map into a one-dimensional histogram H(x), where x ∈

[0 , 255] . To suppress local fluctuations, it smooths the histogram with a Gaussian filter, yielding Hs(x). The prominent local maxima form the set 

P = {p0, p 1, . . . , p n}. Since the first mode p0 generally corresponds to the distant background, the module discards it. For each consecutive pair of modes (pi, p i+1 ), the algorithm defines the corresponding valley as: 

vi = arg min  

> x∈[pi,p i+1 ]

Hs(x).

To avoid selecting thresholds from distant regions, the search is limited to the first four candidate valleys V = {v1, v 2, v 3, v 4}. The software selects the optimal threshold as: 

Topt = arg min  

> v∈V

Hs(v).

Thresholding the depth map with Topt directly yields the binary foreground mask Mf g .

High-resolution Zero-Shot Segmentation: In parallel, the second branch processes the original RGB image. To preserve small plant structures, the software divides the image into overlapping square tiles. By default, it uses 

640 × 640 tiles with an overlap factor O = 0 .5. This results in a stride S

defined by: 

S = L(1 − O),

where L is the tile size. Both L and O are configurable by the user. Each tile is independently processed by a vision foundation model (SAM3 in our implementation [8]) using the user’s textual prompt. The software discards fragments smaller than 50 pixels or covering more than 55% of a tile. 

Graph-Based Mask Assembly (GMA): Because overlapping tiles gen-erate redundant and fragmented predictions, the software applies a mask assembly module. GMA represents each valid mask fragment as a node in a graph. The module establishes edges between fragments using a two-stage overlap validation. First, it identifies candidate pairs using bounding box Intersection-over-Union ( IoU box > 0.05 ). Second, it evaluates these pairs using the mask IoU: 

IoU mask (mi, m j ) = |mi ∩ mj ||mi| + |mj | − | mi ∩ mj |.

6The module retains edges where IoU mask > 0.10 . The algorithm extracts connected components to form reconstructed object instances. For overlap-ping pixels belonging to different instances, GMA applies a Winner-Takes-All procedure based on confidence scores to output a unified segmentation mask 

Msam .

Geometry-Aware Fusion: Finally, the software aligns Mf g to the spatial resolution of Msam . It applies a pixel-wise logical AND operation between the two masks. This step acts as a geometric filter: it removes predictions located outside the foreground vegetation region without altering the shape of the objects detected by the zero-shot segmentation branch. 

2.2. Software functionalities and interfaces 

FrontVeg V2 provides two complementary interfaces: • Command-Line Interface (CLI): Designed for automated batch processing on HPC clusters. Users define input/output directories, model paths, and parameters (tile size, overlap, prompt) via arguments, allowing large-scale phenotyping without code modification. • Napari Plugin: A graphical interface ( https://napari-hub.org/ plugins/frontvegv2.html ) for interactive analysis. Agronomists can load images, tune pipeline parameters visually, and export both seg-mentation masks and quantitative instance statistics (object counts, areas).

Beyond raw performance, FrontVeg V2 resolves the manual annotation bot-tleneck in digital phenotyping. Creating the 158-image evaluation dataset required approximately 200 hours of manual expert annotation. By offer-ing zero-shot inference without fine-tuning, FrontVeg V2 saves an estimated 1,300 hours of manual labor per 1,000 images, accelerating phenotypic data collection. Earlier versions of FrontVeg have supported agricultural research, including doctoral studies on grapevine leaf-area estimation and published work on tree segmentation [19]. Conclusions 

FrontVeg V2 provides a training-free framework for foreground-aware plant trait segmentation in high-resolution images of trellised crops. By com-bining monocular depth estimation with tiled zero-shot segmentation and graph-based mask assembly, the software reduces interference from neigh-boring vegetation rows while preserving fine plant structures. Its modular implementation and availability through both command-line and Napari in-terfaces make it suitable for interactive analysis and large-scale phenotyping workflows. FrontVeg V2 therefore offers a reusable basis for geometry-aware, multi-crop and multi-trait phenotyping without task-specific model retrain-ing. The pipeline runs on consumer-grade hardware (Intel i7-11850H, 32 GB RAM, RTX 3080 16 GB) without cloud dependency. VADT preprocessing takes 1 s/image, while sliding-window SAM3 inference remains the main bottleneck ( 2 min/class), providing a baseline for future real-time edge de-ployment.
