# Harnessing SPAD Noise: Test-Time Gated Attention for Indoor Depth Estimation 

## Yiquan Gao Heriot-Watt University 

## Abstract 

Monocular depth estimation (MDE) has emerged as a cost-effective alternative to hardware-dependent 3D sens-ing modalities, yet predicting precise metric depth remains a persistent challenge due to intrinsic scale ambiguity, edge bleeding, and limited zero-shot generalization in unseen en-vironments. Recent multi-modal approaches incorporate 1D/3D Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD) data to re-solve these ambiguities, but they mainly treat SPAD noise as a harmful artifact required for denoising or leave the noise ignored. In this paper, we present an essentially dif-ferent insight: native SPAD noise is an informative prior that implicitly encodes deterministic environmental char-acteristics. Driven by this paradigm shift, we introduce a novel, lightweight Test-Time Gated Attention framework by Harnessing SPAD Noise, therefore dubbed HSN. Tailored for indoor scenarios, HSN leverages the synergistic combi-nation of 1D SPAD histograms and Gate Adapters (GAs). Through three complementary optimization phases, it gen-erates channel-wise gated attention vectors to dynamically adjust encoder feature maps at inference time, effectively resolving ambiguous scale and boundary challenges with-out the heavy computational overhead or dense 3D modal-ities that other TTA methods suffer from. Extensive exper-iments on challenging real-world indoor benchmarks show that HSN achieves state-of-the-art metric depth accuracy in in-domain settings, while exhibiting even greater potential under zero-shot generalization to unfamiliar environments. Code will be made publicly available. 

## 1. Introduction 

Although hardware-based 3D sensing modalities like Li-DAR, stereo vision, time-of-flight, and structured light are widely used, monocular depth estimation (MDE) has emerged as a compelling solution requiring only a single camera. By eliminating expensive and bulky hardware de-pendencies, MDE provides a versatile alternative for diverse fields, including indoor navigation [44], robotics [7], aug-mented reality [10], and AI-generated content (AIGC) [25]. While MDE excels at capturing relative depth cues, predict-ing precise metric depth (i.e., absolute distances) remains a persistent bottleneck. This limitation primarily stems from the scale ambiguity intrinsic to monocular vision, alongside severe edge bleeding at boundaries and structural artifacts within object surfaces. Furthermore, these challenges be-come seriously exacerbated during zero-shot generalization in unseen environments. To tackle these issues, recent works have incorporated various modalities, such as 3D SPAD volumes [23, 33], 3D point clouds [26], IMU [37], and 1D SPAD histograms [20, 27], focusing on either multimodal feature fusion during full training or rescaling at test-time adaptation. Compared to other modalities, 1D SPAD histograms are acquired via a single-pixel time-resolved detector with a low-power pulsed light source, which renders them the most cost-effective yet highly noisy data form, offering the least amount of infor-mation. Confronted with SPAD noise, existing 3D SPAD depth networks [23, 28, 33] predominantly focus on denois-ing to eliminate these fluctuations as destructive artifacts. Meanwhile, current 1D SPAD methods either integrate the histograms into deep multimodal feature fusion during full training [20], or restrict their utility to test-time histogram matching [27]. As a result, they completely bypass raw noise processing, leaving the statistical properties of the 1D SPAD noise unexploited. Departing from all these existing tracks, we present a fundamentally different perspective: this native noise is not merely a pure corruption, but rather an informative prior that implicitly encodes deterministic environmental charac-teristics. To realize this, our work introduces a novel Test-Time Gated Attention framework named HSN that explic-itly harnesses the SPAD noise prior inherently embedded within 1D SPAD histograms. Specifically, this framework aims at refining depth perception for indoor scenarios dur-ing test time, since 1D SPAD signals suffer from severe am-bient light interference outdoors but maintain a relatively stable noise structure indoors. In the indoor setting, HSN uses the noise as a guidance signal to yield a channel-wise gated attention vector that dynamically calibrates the cor-responding encoder maps, thereby effectively addressing arXiv:2610.15431v1 [cs.CV] 11 Oct 2026 the ambiguous scale and boundary challenges in monoc-ular metric depth estimation. By building upon the effi-cient combination of 1D SPAD and MLPs, HSN achieves a highly lightweight TTA solution that thrives under ex-tremely sparse data regimes, which avoids heavy TTA de-signs such as the diffusion overhead of MfH [43] and the reliance on dense 3D point clouds in DR-LiDAR [26]. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows: • A Novel Noise-Driven Paradigm: We challenge the con-vention of viewing SPAD noise solely as a destructive artifact destined for denoising. Instead, we introduce a paradigm shift by repurposing the 1D SPAD noise signal as an informative prior that correlates with environmental characteristics to enable geometric and scale calibration for indoor monocular metric depth estimation. • Lightweight Test-Time Adaptation Framework: We propose an efficient framework named Test-Time Gated Attention by Harnessing SPAD Noise (HSN), along with the design of three complementary optimizations: statisti-cal distribution alignment, physically-constrained losses, and a stability audit mechanism. By exploiting the syn-ergy between 1D SPAD histograms and Gate Adapters, HSN yields channel-wise gated attention vectors to adap-tively calibrate encoder feature maps at inference time, eliminating the heavy computational overhead and dense 3D modalities common in competitive TTA methods. • Superior In-Domain and Zero-Shot Generalization: 

As a generic plug-and-play TTA framework for encoder– decoder MDE architectures, HSN is validated via exten-sive experiments on challenging indoor benchmarks in-volving real-world data. While achieving state-of-the-art metric depth accuracy for the in-domain setting, it ex-hibits even more profound potential in zero-shot gener-alization across unseen scenes. 

## 2. Related Work 

Monocular Metric Depth Estimation. Conventional ab-solute depth perception methods heavily rely on stereo vi-sion or expensive active sensors like LiDAR to resolve real-world dimensions. To circumvent these hardware con-straints, recent deep neural networks formulate Monocu-lar Metric Depth Estimation (MMDE) as a direct map-ping from single RGB images to physical distances, typ-ically by fitting unknown camera intrinsics onto canoni-cal camera spaces. Despite notable progress on specific datasets, existing MMDE networks, including regression models [8, 9], discrete bin classification models [3, 4] and depth foundation models [5, 35, 36], still struggle with scale ambiguity [8, 15] when transferring global scene scales to unseen, zero-shot scenarios with varying camera intrin-sics and environmental factors. Multimodal feature fu-sion [20, 33, 42, 46], large-scale data mixture [31, 38], and camera-depth disentanglement [29, 30] enable train-ing models across multiple modalities or domains simul-taneously, which significantly extends the generalization capabilities for zero-shot MMDE. Nevertheless, when en-countering exceptionally challenging open-world domains, these models commonly require targeted finetuning to re-solve severe out-of-distribution gaps due to their lack of test-time scale alignment. However, downstream finetuning is both computationally prohibitive and highly inflexible for practical deployment. Therefore, our HSN provides a gen-eral plug-and-play TTA framework for various categories of MMDE networks, contingent upon their utilization of an encoder-decoder architecture. 

Test-Time Adaptation for Depth Perception. Deploying a pre-trained MMDE model into unobserved domains of-ten induces massive performance degradation due to dis-tribution shifts in sensor intrinsics, illumination conditions, and environmental variations. To alleviate this without re-triggering expensive offline finetuning, Test-Time Adapta-tion (TTA) focuses on dynamically adjusting model weights or recalibrating output distributions during the inference stage. Although TTA for zero-shot MMDE remains heavily under-explored, the few existing approaches mainly lever-age cross-modal visual clues or domain-specific priors to bridge domain gaps. For instance, MfH [43] adopts a ’generate-and-estimate’ paradigm via diffusion-based hu-man inpainting, while SurgDepth [34] adapts pre-trained decoders exclusively for medical endoscopy using surgical self-supervised signals. However, these methods either in-troduce a heavy generative loop or require repetitive online optimization, incurring severe computational bottlenecks during TTA. Another line of work, such as DR-LiDAR [26], utilizes hardware-dependent dense 3D points from sensors to rescale an affine-invariant disparity map, yet its require-ment for explicit 3D coordinates leads to substantial com-putational and financial overhead, restricting its deployment on low-cost commodity robots. In contrast, we present a cost-effective TTA design by leveraging extremely sparse 1D SPAD inputs coupled with a compact MLP architec-ture, ensuring a versatile solution with highly competitive performance for robust metric depth adaptation in zero-shot MMDE. 

Exploiting Noise in Learning. Noise can manifest in var-ious forms and is generally regarded as a harmful arti-fact that needs to be removed in numerous applications, such as license plate detection [11], EEG analysis [19], point cloud denoising [45], and time series forecasting [40]. This denoising practice dominates the Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD) field, since raw SPAD acquisi-tions frequently suffer from unavoidable environmental in-terference (e.g., clutter from complex background objects and unpredictable ambient light especially outdoors) and intrinsic sensor disturbances like dark count rates (DCR). Consequently, SPAD-based MDE methods [23, 28, 33] pri-oritize raw 3D dense volume denoising as an indispens-able prerequisite. However, existing studies regarding the noise handling of the 1D sparse SPAD representation re-main absent [20, 27], leaving its significantly higher noise completely untouched. Contemporary works like BadCLIP [2] and BAPLE [14] emerge to show how learnable noise can backdoor VLMs, TNT [18] shifts this utility toward TTA for zero-shot im-age classification, proving that such noise-based optimiza-tion within input space can serve as an efficient tool to handle unpredictable distribution shifts. Inspired by this philosophy, we present a distinct insight against conven-tional SPAD processing: rather than a destructive artifact to be filtered out, native SPAD noise constitutes an informa-tive prior that implicitly encodes deterministic environmen-tal characteristics. To this end, we propose the Test-Time Gated Attention framework by harnessing SPAD noise to address zero-shot MMDE, marking the first attempt to ex-ploit noise learning for test-time depth adaptation. 

## 3. HSN: Test-Time Gated Attention 

We propose a Test-Time Gated Attention framework dubbed HSN to refine monocular depth predictions by 

Harnessing SPAD Noise, as illustrated in Fig. 1 and de-tailed in Alg. 1. During inference, the pre-trained encoder-decoder network θ remains frozen to preserve its general structural priors. HSN only optimizes a specific subset of parameters within the Gate Adapter (GA) modules ϕ at test time. This test-time adaptation (TTA) process com-prises three distinct phases: statistical distribution align-ment, physically-constrained optimization, and a robustness audit to ensure reliability. Before dissecting these three phases, we first elaborate on the structural design of the GA modules and the overall workflow that governs their online adaptation.

For in-domain data where the initial geometry is reliable, we set κ = 0 to preserve the original scale. An adaptive constraint modulation strategy is applied to the hyperparameter set P = {λf , γ, ϵ }, dynamically updat-ing the parameters across both unseen and in-domain sce-narios to maintain an optimal equilibrium between aggres-sive correction and structural preservation. For unseen data, we enforce a conservative adaptation trajectory by assign-ing a fixed, high-intensity fidelity weight λf = 1 .5 × 10 6,a stringent reliability threshold γ = 0 .995 , and a restrictive gradient clipping threshold ϵ = 0 .02 . This configuration ensures that the optimization remains heavily anchored to the aligned baseline, preventing structural distortions in un-familiar domains. Conversely, for in-domain data, we introduce a noise-aware scaling to facilitate precision refinement, which is inspired by the data uncertainty concept in illumination en-hancement [12]. Specifically, the fidelity weight λf is adap-tively scaled according to the noise level ω of the SPAD histogram in Eq. 2 using an exponential kernel: 

λf = clamp (C1 · exp( α · ω), λ min , λ max ) (7) where C1 = 4 × 10 5 and α = 2 .5 are empirically set to modulate the exponential sensitivity to ω while balanc-ing numerical stability. This formulation allows the opti-mizer to perform surgical residual corrections in high-SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio) scenarios while remaining conser-vative in the presence of heavy ambient noise. To accom-modate these subtle refinements, the reliability threshold is relaxed to γ = 0 .998 and the gradient clipping threshold is elevated to ϵ = 0 .05 , granting the optimizer sufficient flexibility to polish the final depth map. Furthermore, we perform a pre-optimization evaluation by computing the initial loss Linit using the physical loss function in Sec. 3.3. If Linit < τ exit , indicating the ini-tial state is already physically consistent, an early exit is triggered to directly yield D∗ via Ψ( ·) in Eq. 15, thereby bypassing redundant iterations.

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Datasets 

NYU Depth V2 (with SPAD Simulation): This indoor benchmark is evaluated using the standard Eigen parti-tion [8], which comprises 120K training images and 654 test images of 640 ×480 pixels. Following Nishimura et al . [27], single-pixel SPAD histograms are synthesized from dense RGB-D data by modeling pixel-wise albedo and inverse-square depth decay. The resulting macro-scale signal is then modulated by a stochastic Poisson process alongside simu-lated hardware jitter and background dark counts, where the signal-to-background ratio (SBR) is set to 100 and the his-togram dimension N is set to 1024. During depth inference, the canonical Eigen crop is applied to filter out unaligned boundary artifacts, adhering to conventional evaluation pro-tocols. This dataset serves as a comprehensive benchmark for both in-domain and zero-shot evaluations. 

RGB-SPAD Dataset: This real-world indoor benchmark, released by Nishimura et al . [27], is employed to rigorously evaluate the practical generalization capability of different depth networks. Captured at a resolution of 640 × 480 

pixels, it consists of aligned RGB images and authentic, hardware-captured single-pixel SPAD transient histograms from various indoor environments and target objects, paired with corresponding ground-truth depth maps. Zero-shot generalization on NYU Depth V2: Our seven HSN variants are evaluated against 17 zero-shot depth benchmarks, consisting of one CNN model (HG-Depth [41]), 12 depth foundation models, and four recent TTA counterparts. Additionally, UniDepthV2-N-L [30] (fine-tuned on the target domain) serves as a performance upper bound (Oracle). As reported in Table 2, HSN yields universal, synchronous improvements across all metrics and architectures. Specifically, for depth foundation models, HSN shatters their zero-shot performance ceilings; e.g., it pushes DepthPro [6] to δ1 = 0 .948 and UniDepthV2-L [30] to a remarkable δ1 = 0 .980 , achieving an 11.1% relative error reduction in Abs Rel. On lighter baselines like DAV2-H-B [36], HSN delivers a massive 17.5% absolute gain in 

δ1 and cuts the Abs Rel by 32.2%. Crucially, as a TTA framework, HSN offers superior efficiency over existing counterparts. Unlike MfH [43]’s heavy diffusion computation or DR-LiDAR [26]’s reliance on dense 3D point clouds, HSN maintains a lightweight design and operates under extremely sparse regimes by utilizing only 1D SPAD signals. Despite HSN’s com-pact architecture and data sparsity, our advanced variants (e.g., UniDepthV2-B/L-HSN) competitively match or out-perform these heavy TTA methods, particularly across δ1

and RMSE indicators. Notably, this evaluation reveals that HSN brings significantly more pronounced improvements to zero-shot scenarios than to in-domain settings, firmly validating that HSN primarily excels at bridging cross-domain generaliza-tion gaps rather than merely providing marginal refinements to in-domain setups.

Notably, HSN effectively boosts PhoMoNet [20] in unseen realistic scenarios relative to its in-domain setting, allow-ing it to even outperform the acknowledged strong baseline DAV1-N-L [35] in terms of overall error metrics. Crucially, our HSN framework exhibits substantial improvements on weaker baselines (e.g., AdaBins [3] and the DAV2-H fam-ily [36]), demonstrating its remarkable robustness. These results strongly prove that HSN serves as a generic, plug-and-play TTA framework that effectively mitigates domain shifts on the fly and unlocks the zero-shot potential of di-verse architectures in real-world applications.

Considering both accu-racy and computational efficiency, we choose 2 optimiza-tion steps for our default setting, unless otherwise specified. 5. Conclusion 

In this work, we have established a novel noise-driven paradigm for monocular metric depth estimation by shift-ing the perception of SPAD noise from a pure corruption to a valuable environmental prior. Through the develop-ment of our HSN framework, we demonstrated that the na-tive noise signal can be naturally exploited via GA modules to enforce accurate geometric and scale calibration during inference, thereby bypassing the severe latency overhead or dense 3D data dependencies that typically bottleneck competitive TTA counterparts in resource-constrained plat-forms. The proposed test-time gated attention mechanism performs dynamic attention allocation across hierarchical encoder feature representations in an interpretable manner that aligns with the theories of hierarchical representation robustness. Our extensive experiments on real-world indoor bench-marks featuring synthetic and realistic SPAD histograms validate the efficacy of this framework, showcasing not only state-of-the-art in-domain precision but also remarkable adaptability under zero-shot generalization to unfamiliar environments. Moving forward, this work highlights the un-tapped potential of raw sensor noise in sparse data regimes, opening new possibilities for building highly efficient, multi-modal perception systems across broader applica-tions such as robotics, AR, and autonomous navigation.
