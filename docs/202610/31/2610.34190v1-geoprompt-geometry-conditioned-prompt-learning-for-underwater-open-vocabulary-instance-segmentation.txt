Abstract 

Open-vocabulary instance segmentation (OVIS) provides a promising solution for marine perception by recognizing and segmenting categories beyond predefined label spaces. Ex-isting OVIS methods usually rely on vision-language align-ment, where visual region features are matched with tex-tual category embeddings to recognize both seen and un-seen classes. However, this alignment assumption becomes fragile in underwater environments. Color attenuation, scat-tering, turbidity, and low contrast distort visual appearance, causing region features to deviate from their corresponding semantic concepts. As a result, the similarity between de-graded visual features and textual classifiers becomes unre-liable, which particularly weakens the recognition of unseen underwater categories that cannot be corrected by category-specific supervision. To address these issues, we propose Geo-Prompt, a geometry-conditioned prompt learning framework for underwater open-vocabulary instance segmentation. Our key insight is that geometric structure is more stable than vi-sual appearance in degraded underwater scenes. Specifically, GeoPrompt introduces a geometry-guided visual adaptation module (GVAM) to extract depth-derived structural priors and inject them into multi-scale visual features. It further de-velops a geometry-conditioned prompt learning (GCPL) that generates geometry-aware textual classifiers from structural representations. These geometry-aware classifiers are inte-grated with fixed and image-conditioned textual classifiers to improve open-vocabulary semantic alignment. Experiments on the MARIS-OpenIS benchmark show that GeoPrompt achieves state-of-the-art performance and brings consistent improvements on underwater open-vocabulary instance seg-mentation. 

# Introduction 

Open-vocabulary instance segmentation (OVIS) aims to seg-ment object instances from both seen and unseen categories by aligning visual region representations with textual cate-gory embeddings (Radford et al. 2021; Li et al. 2022; Ding, Wang, and Tu 2022; Liang et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2023a,b; Qin et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2023). Benefiting from vision-language models, OVIS reduces the dependence on closed-set cat-egory annotations and provides a promising paradigm for marine perception, where underwater environments contain diverse, fine-grained, and long-tail object categories. This ability is especially important for underwater scene under-

Despite recent progress, underwater open-vocabulary in-stance segmentation remains highly challenging. Most exist-ing OVIS methods are developed under the assumption that RGB visual appearance can provide discriminative cues for matching textual category embeddings (Cho et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2024; Xie et al. 2024; Shan et al. 2024; Jiao et al. 2024; Niu et al. 2025). However, underwater imaging conditions often violate this assumption. Color attenuation, light scat-tering, turbidity, low contrast, and non-uniform illumination distort object appearance (Akkaynak and Treibitz 2018; Is-lam et al. 2020; Lian et al. 2023, 2024; Chen et al. 2026), causing visual region features to deviate from their corre-sponding semantic concepts. Since open-vocabulary recog-nition depends on comparing visual features with textual classifiers, such appearance drift directly weakens vision-language alignment, especially for unseen categories that rely more heavily on transferable semantic correspondence. To address this challenge, MARIS (Li et al. 2026) intro-duces a marine OVIS benchmark and improves underwater segmentation through geometric enhancement and seman-tic alignment. Nevertheless, existing methods still leave an important problem underexplored: underwater degradation affects not only the visual representation, but also the way visual regions should be semantically aligned with textual classifiers. Prompt-based learning has become an effective strategy for adapting vision-language models to downstream tasks by replacing hand-crafted templates with learnable or input-conditioned textual contexts (Zhou et al. 2022b,a; Khat-tak et al. 2023a,b). In open-vocabulary segmentation, such prompts are used to construct textual classifiers, which di-rectly influence the matching between visual regions and category semantics (Radford et al. 2021; Liang et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2023). Fixed prompts preserve general semantic knowledge but cannot adapt to scene-specific underwater degradation. Image-conditioned prompt learning can gen-erate adaptive prompts from visual features (Zhou et al. 2022a), yet the image features used for prompt generation may also be affected by underwater appearance drift. There-fore, semantic adaptation based only on fixed templates or degraded RGB features remains insufficient for robust under-water open-vocabulary recognition. arXiv:2610.34190v1 [cs.CV] 25 Oct 2026 Figure 1: Visual comparison of appearance-only predictions, pseudo-depth maps, and GeoPrompt predictions. By leveraging depth-derived geometry, GeoPrompt produces more complete and reliable open-vocabulary instance predictions. Geometry provides a natural complementary cue to allevi-ate this problem. Prior RGB-D, monocular depth estimation, and depth-aware segmentation studies have shown that ge-ometric information can complement RGB appearance by providing object boundaries, spatial layout, and shape cues for dense prediction tasks (Hazirbas et al. 2017; Park, Hong, and Lee 2017; Jiang et al. 2018; Chen et al. 2020; Ran-ftl, Bochkovskiy, and Koltun 2021; Yang et al. 2024a,b). Inspired by this observation, we use depth-derived interme-diate features as structural priors for underwater perception. Importantly, our method does not require ground-truth depth annotations or explicit depth supervision. Instead of treating monocular depth as a target prediction, we exploit depth-derived features to provide geometry-aware cues that are complementary to degraded RGB appearance. Based on this insight, we propose GeoPrompt for un-derwater open-vocabulary instance segmentation, which in-troduces geometry-conditioned prompt learning to improve vision-language alignment. GeoPrompt adapts both sides of vision-language alignment. On the visual side, a geometry-guided visual adaptation module extracts depth-derived structural features and injects them into multi-scale visual representations, helping the segmentation decoder capture more stable object structure under underwater degradation. On the semantic side, a geometry-conditioned prompt branch generates geometry-aware textual classifiers from structural representations. These classifiers are integrated with the original fixed classifier and the image-conditioned prompt classifier, allowing the semantic space to adapt not only to image appearance but also to underwater geometry. By in-troducing geometry-guided visual adaptation and geometry-conditioned prompt learning, GeoPrompt improves both vi-sual representation and semantic alignment for seen and un-seen underwater categories. Our contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We reformulate underwater open-vocabulary instance segmentation as a geometry-conditioned vision-language alignment problem, emphasizing that both visual repre-sentations and semantic prompts should be adapted under underwater degradation. 

• We propose GeoPrompt, a unified framework consisting of Geometry-Guided Visual Adaptation Module (GVAM) and Geometry-Conditioned Prompt Learning (GCPL). The former injects depth-derived structural priors into multi-scale visual features, while the latter uses geomet-ric cues to generate degradation-aware semantic prompts. 

• We design a Geometry-Conditioned Vision-Language Alignment strategy that aligns geometry-enhanced vi-sual embeddings with geometry-aware textual classifiers for open-vocabulary mask prediction. Experiments on MARIS show that GeoPrompt achieves state-of-the-art performance and improves generalization to unseen un-derwater categories. 

# Related Work 

Open-Vocabulary Instance Segmentation 

Open-vocabulary instance segmentation (OVIS) aims to rec-ognize and segment object instances from both seen and unseen categories by leveraging the semantic generalization of vision-language models (Radford et al. 2021; Liang et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2023b). Existing approaches often decouple mask generation from category prediction: a segmentation decoder produces instance masks, while visual region em-beddings are matched with textual classifiers derived from category names or prompt templates (Liang et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2023a; Yu et al. 2023). This design enables recognition beyond the predefined training label space. However, most methods are developed on natural-image benchmarks and assume that RGB features provide reliable cues for vision-language alignment. This assumption is particularly fragile in underwater en-vironments. Color attenuation, scattering, turbidity, and low contrast distort visual appearance, causing region features to deviate from their corresponding textual concepts. GVAM extracts depth-derived structural features from a frozen depth estimation model and adapts them to obtain geometry-enhanced visual representations, with the pseudo-depth map shown only as a visualization of the geometry cue. GCPL generates geometry-aware textual classifiers and integrates them with fixed and image-conditioned classifiers for geometry-conditioned vision-language alignment and open-vocabulary instance mask prediction. semantic segmentation studies have addressed distribution shifts through curriculum-style self-training for source-free adaptation (Wang, Liang, and Zhang 2024) and informative data mining for one-shot cross-domain adaptation (Wang et al. 2023). Although these methods do not directly tar-get underwater OVIS, they highlight the importance of ro-bust representations under distribution shifts. Furthermore, geometry-guided self-distillation has been explored for open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding (Wang et al. 2024), demonstrating the potential of structural cues for open-vocabulary representations. MARIS (Li et al. 2026) takes an important step toward underwater OVIS by introducing a marine benchmark and enhancing visual representations through geometric features and semantic alignment. Nevertheless, existing underwater OVIS approaches primarily employ geometry for visual fea-ture enhancement, while geometry-conditioned adaptation of textual classifiers remains underexplored. This motivates GeoPrompt, which uses depth-derived structural representa-tions to guide both visual adaptation and textual classifier generation for more reliable open-vocabulary alignment. 

Geometry-Conditioned Prompt Learning 

Prompt learning adapts vision-language models by replac-ing handcrafted textual templates with learnable or input-conditioned context tokens. CoOp (Zhou et al. 2022b) learns task-specific context vectors, while CoCoOp (Zhou et al. 2022a) introduces image-conditioned prompts to improve generalization to unseen categories. MaPLe (Khattak et al. 2023a) further explores multimodal prompt learning by jointly adapting visual and textual representations. These approaches provide flexible textual classifiers, but their ef-fectiveness depends on the reliability of the features used to generate adaptive prompts. In underwater scenes, image-conditioned prompts may in-herit appearance bias from degraded RGB features. Color distortion, scattering, and background interference can af-fect both visual region embeddings and image-level features used for prompt generation, weakening semantic alignment. Geometry offers a complementary cue, since structural in-formation such as object contours, shapes, and spatial layout can remain informative under appearance degradation. Pre-vious RGB-D segmentation studies (Hazirbas et al. 2017; Chen et al. 2020) have demonstrated the benefits of incorpo-rating geometric cues into visual representations. However, these methods primarily exploit geometry for visual feature enhancement rather than textual classifier adaptation. To address this limitation, GeoPrompt introduces geometry-conditioned prompt learning that generates adap-tive textual classifiers from depth-derived structural fea-tures. By integrating geometry-aware prompts with fixed and image-conditioned classifiers, GeoPrompt adapts both sides of vision-language alignment, reducing dependence on degraded RGB appearance and improving semantic corre-spondence for underwater open-vocabulary recognition. 

# Method 

The overall architecture of GeoPrompt is shown in Figure 2. It introduces three core components: (1) a Geometry-Guided Visual Adaptation Module (GVAM) that extracts depth-derived structural priors and injects them into multi-scale vi-sual features, alleviating underwater appearance drift; (2) a 

Geometry-Conditioned Prompt Learning (GCPL) mod-ule that generates geometry-aware textual classifiers from structural representations, reducing semantic misalignment; (3) a Geometry-Conditioned Vision-Language Alignment 

mechanism that matches geometry-enhanced visual features with adaptive textual classifiers for robust open-vocabulary mask prediction. Through this design, GeoPrompt adapts both sides of vision-language alignment, enabling more re-liable recognition of both seen and unseen underwater cate-gories. 

Geometry-Guided Visual Adaptation 

Underwater images are often degraded by color attenuation, scattering, turbidity, and low contrast, which weaken RGB-based representations for open-vocabulary instance segmen-tation (Lian et al. 2023, 2024; Chen et al. 2026). To comple-ment unreliable appearance cues, geometric structures such as object contours, shapes, spatial layout, and depth-related hierarchy provide useful structural information for dense pre-diction (Hazirbas et al. 2017; Park, Hong, and Lee 2017; Jiang et al. 2018; Chen et al. 2020). We therefore introduce a Geometry-Guided Visual Adaptation Module that extracts depth-derived structural features to enhance multi-scale vi-sual representations.

Geometry-Conditioned Prompt Learning

Fixed prompts such as “a photo of a [CLASS]” preserve general semantic knowledge from pre-training (Radford et al. 2021; Zhou et al. 2022b) but cannot adapt to scene-specific underwater degradation. Image-conditioned prompt learning further generates adap-tive prompts from image features (Zhou et al. 2022a):

# Experiments 

Experimental Setup 

Dataset. We conduct experiments on the MARIS-OpenIS benchmark for underwater open-vocabulary instance seg-mentation. MARIS-OpenIS contains more than 16K un-derwater images with pixel-level instance masks, covering 9 super-classes and 158 fine-grained subclasses. It refines coarse underwater labels such as “fish” and “plants” into more detailed marine categories, making it suitable for eval-uating fine-grained open-vocabulary recognition. Following the official protocol, the dataset is split into 5,712 training images and 10,439 validation images. The training and vali-dation sets contain 84 and 115 categories, respectively, with 41 overlapping categories. We report results on both intersec-tion categories and open-vocabulary categories that appear only in the validation set. 

Evaluation Metrics. We report COCO-style mask AP (Lin et al. 2014), including AP, AP 50 , AP 75 . When category splits are available, we further report AP on seen/intersection and open-vocabulary categories. 

Implementation Details. We use ConvNeXt-Base (Liu et al. 2022) CLIP (Radford et al. 2021) as the vision-language backbone and Depth Anything V2 (Yang et al. 2024b) with ViT-B as the geometry prior extractor. The model is trained for 50K iterations with a total batch size of 16 on 8 GPUs. The base learning rate is set to 1×10 −4. For prompt learning, the number of context tokens is set to 4 and initialized with “a photo of a”. The image-conditioned prompt coefficient is set to λ = 0 .05 , while the geometry-conditioned prompt coefficient is initialized as αg = 0 .05 and constrained by a maximum value of 0.10.

# Conclusion 

In this paper, we proposed GeoPrompt, a geometry-conditioned prompt learning framework for underwater open-vocabulary instance segmentation. GeoPrompt con-sists of two complementary components: GVAM and GCPL. GVAM enhances multi-scale visual features with depth-derived geometric representations, while GCPL generates adaptive textual classifiers from image-level and geometry-level contexts. By jointly adapting visual representations and textual classifiers, GeoPrompt improves geometry-conditioned vision-language alignment under underwater appearance degradation. Importantly, GeoPrompt does not require ground-truth depth annotations, making it practical for RGB-only marine datasets. Experiments on the MARIS benchmark demonstrate the effectiveness of GeoPrompt. GeoPrompt improves OV mAP from 39.77 to 48.20, show-ing stronger generalization to unseen underwater categories. Ablation studies and qualitative results further validate the contribution of the proposed components. Despite these improvements, challenging cases remain. Extremely small objects, heavy occlusion, ambiguous boundaries, or noisy depth-derived geometry features may still lead to incomplete predictions. Future work will explore robust geometry priors and stronger semantic discrimination for complex marine scenarios.
