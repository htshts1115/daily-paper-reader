ABSTRACT
High-resolution video matting requires preserving fine details, such as hair strands
and soft transparency, while maintaining consistency across frames. Memory-
based methods achieve strong matting quality by matching current-frame features
with information stored from earlier frames, but this process becomes increas-
ingly costly as resolution grows. We present MatAnyone-E, an efficient exten-
sion of MatAnyone 2 that focuses computation where it is most needed. Our
approach first performs low-resolution propagation to identify uncertain regions
that require closer inspection and the memory candidates most relevant to them.
It then performs high-resolution matching only for these regions using the se-
lected memory, while reusing low-resolution matching results elsewhere. This
strategy reduces both the number of locations requiring detailed matching and the
amount of memory searched, while preserving the information needed to produce
a complete matte. We further reduce the cost of generating high-resolution mat-
tes with a lightweight decoder designed around measured runtime bottlenecks and
trained to reproduce the original decoder’s representations. Experiments show
that MatAnyone-E maintains matting quality comparable to MatAnyone 2 while
achieving an estimated 7.4× inference speedup and reducing peak GPU memory
usage to 7.6% of the usage of MatAnyone 2 at 4K. With compilation-enabled in-
ference on a single NVIDIA H200 GPU, it reaches 73.07 FPS at 1080p and 28.62
FPS at 4K.

INTRODUCTION
Video matting estimates a continuous alpha matte for each frame, enabling foreground subjects to
be composited onto new backgrounds. High-resolution applications demand accurate recovery of
hair strands, thin structures, and soft transparency, together with temporal consistency to prevent
flickering. Meeting these requirements, however, becomes computationally expensive as resolution
increases, limiting the practical use of otherwise high-quality methods.
Existing video matting methods (Zhang et al., 2025; 2019; Qiao et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2017) ad-
dress this challenge with different trade-offs. Efficiency-oriented approaches, such as RVM (Lin
et al., 2022) and SparseMat (Sun et al., 2023), reduce computation through recurrent temporal mod-
eling or selective high-resolution refinement, but can struggle to preserve fine structures and tem-
poral consistency in challenging scenes. Recent methods such as VideoMaMa (Lim et al., 2026)
and SAM2Matting (Shen et al., 2026) leverage generative or segmentation priors, yet their restricted
processing resolutions limit the detail they can recover from high-resolution inputs. Memory-based
methods, including MatAnyone (Yang et al., 2025) and MatAnyone 2 (Yang et al., 2026), achieve
strong matting quality by retrieving information from earlier frames. However, matching current-
frame features against stored memory and decoding high-resolution mattes incur substantial com-
putational costs as resolution increases. Although selective matte refinement has been explored,
reducing the cost of the underlying temporal memory matching remains a distinct challenge. This
motivates our central question: how can we preserve the quality of memory-based video matting
while avoiding exhaustive high-resolution matching and costly decoding?
The main computational bottleneck lies in comparing every current-frame feature with a large bank
of features stored from earlier frames. Increasing the video resolution enlarges both sets of features,
causing the matching cost to grow rapidly even when the number of stored frames remains fixed.
Yet detailed matching is not equally important everywhere. The model can rely more on informa-
tion carried over from the previous frame at some locations, while others depend more strongly on
newly retrieved information. This suggests focusing matching computation on locations that require
stronger updates from memory. The difficulty is identifying these locations efficiently: the signal
indicating how much each location needs an update is obtained only after the expensive matching
has already been performed.
We address this dilemma with MatAnyone-E, an efficient extension of MatAnyone 2. Our key
idea is to use an inexpensive low-resolution propagation pass to guide subsequent computation.
This initial pass identifies locations that are likely to benefit most from closer inspection and the
memory candidates most relevant to those locations. We then perform matching at the native feature
resolution only between these selected locations and memory candidates, reusing the upsampled
low-resolution matching results elsewhere. Selecting locations alone would still require searching
the entire memory bank; selecting relevant memory as well reduces both sources of matching cost.
The resulting dense matching output is then used for downstream alpha prediction.
Beyond memory matching, producing the high-resolution alpha matte remains a substantial compu-
tational cost. We therefore develop a lightweight decoder guided by measured runtime bottlenecks,
since reducing arithmetic operations alone does not necessarily improve inference speed. We train
this decoder through knowledge distillation from the original decoder to preserve matting quality.
Together, selective memory matching and efficient decoding address the two main sources of high-
resolution inference cost.
Experiments show that MatAnyone-E maintains matting quality comparable to MatAnyone 2 while
substantially improving inference efficiency. At 4K resolution, our complete model achieves an
estimated 7.4× inference speedup and reduces peak GPU memory usage to 7.6% of MatAnyone 2.
Compared with prior methods, MatAnyone-E provides a stronger balance between matting quality
and inference efficiency at high resolutions.

RELATED WORK
Video Matting. Video matting methods address the coupled challenges of alpha-detail recovery,
temporal consistency, and computational efficiency. RVM (Lin et al., 2022) uses recurrent fea-
ture aggregation for real-time human video matting. SparseMat (Sun et al., 2023) exploits spatio-

temporal sparsity for selective high-resolution refinement, while µMatting (Chen et al., 2026) tar-
gets 4K matting with selective refinement and introduces the αMatte4K dataset. These efficiency-
oriented methods (Ke et al., 2022; Qin et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2021) highlight efficient temporal
modeling and selective refinement as important strategies for high-resolution matting. Another
line of work (Li et al., 2024a; Lin et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024b) leverages external guidance and
pretrained priors. FTP-VM (Huang & Lee, 2023) jointly models trimap propagation and matting.
MaGGIe (Huynh et al., 2024) combines transformer attention and sparse convolution for mask-
guided instance matting. Matting Anything 2 (Zhang et al., 2026a) uses separate memory pathways
for masks and trimaps. SAM2Matting (Shen et al., 2026) connects video tracking (Ravi et al., 2025;
Carion et al., 2026) with dedicated matting heads. GVM (Ge et al., 2025) exploits video diffusion
priors (Blattmann et al., 2023), while VideoMaMa (Lim et al., 2026) leverages generative priors to
convert segmentation masks into alpha mattes and train SAM2-Matte.
Closely related to our work, MatAnyone (Yang et al., 2025) introduces region-adaptive memory
fusion for stable target-assigned video matting. MatAnyone 2 (Yang et al., 2026) further improves
matting through learned quality evaluation, large-scale real-world data curation, and reference-frame
training. We build on this memory-based framework and focus on reducing its high-resolution
inference cost. Whereas methods such as SparseMat and µMatting reduce computation through
selective matte refinement, reducing the cost of the underlying temporal memory matching remains
a distinct challenge.
Efficient Video Object Segmentation. Memory-based video object segmentation (VOS) provides
a foundation for temporal information propagation. STCN (Cheng et al., 2021) improves efficiency
through shared frame correspondences and distance-based affinity. XMem (Cheng & Schwing,
2022) combines sensory, working, and long-term memories to control memory growth over long
videos. Cutie (Cheng et al., 2024) augments pixel-level memory reading with object-level queries
for more robust target representations. SAM 2 (Ravi et al., 2025) extends promptable segmentation
to videos through streaming memory attention. These methods highlight the importance of memory
representation and efficient memory access for temporal propagation.
Recent work further reduces the cost of memory-based VOS. EfficientTAM (Xiong et al., 2025)
combines a lightweight plain ViT encoder with spatially compressed memory attention.
Ed-
geTAM (Zhou et al., 2025) compresses frame memories using a 2D Spatial Perceiver with global
and patch-level queries, supported by knowledge distillation. Efficient-SAM2 (Zhang et al., 2026b)
introduces object-aware window routing and sparse memory retrieval to reduce redundant com-
putation. These approaches demonstrate the effectiveness of memory compression and selective
computation for efficient temporal propagation. Our work extends this efficiency perspective to
high-resolution video matting by using uncertainty estimated from inexpensive low-resolution prop-
agation to determine where native-resolution matching is needed, together with query-dependent
memory selection.

METHODOLOGY
Given a video and a first-frame foreground mask, MatAnyone-E predicts an alpha matte Mt for
each frame It. As illustrated in Fig. 2(a), MatAnyone-E follows the main architecture of MatAny-
one 2, but we modify two of the components whose computational cost grows most rapidly with
resolution: memory propagation (Fig. 2(b)) and decoding (Fig. 2(c)). Sparse Memory Propagation
(Sec. 3.2) uses low-resolution propagation to identify uncertain regions that require closer inspec-
tion and the memory candidates most relevant to them. It then performs high-resolution matching
only for these regions using the selected memory, while reusing low-resolution matching results
elsewhere. A lightweight decoder (Sec. 3.3) further reduces high-resolution decoding cost through
profiling-guided design and feature distillation.

Profiling-Guided Decoder Design. As illustrated in Fig. 2(c), both the original and lightweight
decoders progressively upsample features through four stages, increasing the spatial resolution from
1/16 to 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, and finally the full input resolution. While the original decoder uses dense
convolutions throughout, simply replacing them with lower-FLOP depthwise-separable convolu-
tions does not consistently reduce latency across stages. In particular, at higher spatial resolutions,
the reduction in arithmetic cost is increasingly offset by lower arithmetic intensity and increased
memory-access overhead, so fewer FLOPs do not necessarily translate into faster execution.
We therefore adopt different lightweight designs at different stages. For the 1/16 →1/8 and
1/8 →1/4 stages, we use a depthwise 3×3 convolution followed by a pointwise 1×1 convolution.
For the 1/4 →1/2 and 1/2 →1 stages, where reducing memory traffic becomes more important,
we retain dense convolutions but reduce their channel widths. This stage-wise design follows the
measured runtime behavior of each stage rather than applying a single lightweight block throughout
the decoder. Detailed stage-wise profiling and analysis are provided in Appendix D.

5
CONCLUSION
We presented MatAnyone-E, an efficient framework for high-resolution video matting. Our key in-
sight is that dense high-resolution memory matching is largely redundant: only a subset of query
locations requires precise retrieval, and only a subset of memory is relevant to them. By restrict-
ing native-resolution matching to selected query–memory pairs and reusing the upsampled low-
resolution readout elsewhere, Sparse Memory Propagation substantially reduces the cost of tempo-
ral memory retrieval. A lightweight decoder further reduces high-resolution decoding cost through
profiling-guided design and feature distillation. MatAnyone-E preserves the strong matting qual-
ity of MatAnyone 2 while achieving an estimated 7.4× inference speedup and 13.1× lower peak
GPU memory at 4K. We believe selective high-resolution computation offers a practical direction
for scaling memory-based video matting to high resolutions.

For high-resolution inputs, we extend Sparse Memory Propagation (SMP) to multiple refinement
levels. The number of levels is determined by the spatial size of the memory feature grid. Starting
from the native feature resolution H × W, we repeatedly downsample by a factor of two until
12


Preprint
the coarsest grid contains no more than M spatial locations. This produces a sequence of scales
(s0, s1, . . . , sL), where sL = 1 corresponds to the native memory-feature resolution.
Propagation first operates densely at the coarsest level. The resulting memory readout is used to
estimate uncertainty, and only query locations whose uncertainty exceeds the threshold are propa-
gated at the next finer level. For these selected queries, we further restrict matching to a subset of
memory candidates identified from the coarse affinities. Specifically, memory candidates are ranked
according to their maximum affinity to the selected query regions, and only the top ρ fraction is
retained. Sparse propagation is then performed between the selected queries and retained memory
candidates. The refined readouts replace the corresponding regions of the upsampled coarse result,
while the remaining locations directly retain the coarse prediction. This process is repeated until the
native memory-feature resolution is reached.
Algorithm 1 summarizes the complete procedure. When only a single refinement level is required,
the algorithm reduces to the formulation described in the main paper. For all 4K experiments re-
ported in the main paper, we consistently use a 4 →2 →1 propagation schedule with the spatial
budget set to M = 4096.

B.3
RESOLUTION DEPENDENCE OF SPARSE MEMORY PROPAGATION
Sparse Memory Propagation relies on low-resolution uncertainty and affinities to guide query and
memory selection, and its effectiveness therefore depends on the spatial resolution of the underlying
feature grid. At lower input resolutions, the downsampled feature map can become too coarse to
reliably localize uncertain regions or distinguish relevant memory candidates, limiting the benefit of
selective matching.
14


Preprint
This effect is particularly noticeable for query pruning. As shown in Table 6, its efficiency gain be-
comes more pronounced as the input resolution increases: the benefit is relatively modest at 1080p,
becomes clearer at 1440p, and grows substantially at 4K and 8K. Memory pruning shows a similar
resolution-dependent trend. Its additional gain is limited at 1080p and 1440p, but becomes much
more significant at 8K, where the memory dimension is considerably larger.
These results indicate that Sparse Memory Propagation is most beneficial at high spatial resolutions,
while its advantage can be limited when the low-resolution feature grid becomes too small.

EFFICIENCY ACROSS GPU ARCHITECTURES
To evaluate the efficiency of MatAnyone-E across hardware platforms, we benchmark it on five
GPUs spanning consumer, professional, and datacenter classes. Table 9 reports throughput and peak
GPU memory across four resolutions, with and without compilation.
Across all tested GPUs, MatAnyone-E exhibits consistent scaling trends as the input resolution
increases. Compilation further improves throughput and reduces peak memory across devices. At
4K, the compiled model reaches 9.0 FPS on an RTX 3090, 19.1 FPS on an RTX 5090, 9.4 FPS on
an RTX A6000, 13.6 FPS on an A800-SXM4-80GB, and 28.6 FPS on an H200 NVL. These results
show that the efficiency characteristics of MatAnyone-E remain consistent across a wide range of
GPU architectures.
