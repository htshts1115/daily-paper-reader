## Abstract 

Thin, elongated structures are particularly sensitive to con-nectivity errors, as even a few local segmentation mistakes can disrupt otherwise continuous paths. Existing skeleton-based objectives typically supervise fixed centerline loca-tions and do not account for alternative local routes that may restore connectivity. We propose TRACE, a topology-aware, prediction-adaptive objective that uses a target skele-ton to define required local connections. For each connec-tion, TRACE identifies the easiest valid reconnection under the current prediction and jointly supervises its bottleneck with the corresponding skeleton point. The supervision thus adapts to the evolving prediction rather than remaining fixed to the centerline. We further introduce a topology-aware skeleton carrier that preserves both foreground and class-specific topology, enabling reliable definition of these con-nections. Extensive experiments across six 2D and 3D bench-marks show that TRACE improves connectivity and topology while maintaining strong segmentation accuracy with low training overhead. Code will be released upon acceptance. 

## 1. Introduction 

Segmentation of thin, elongated structures is particularly vulnerable to connectivity errors, as even a few misclassified pixels or voxels can break an otherwise continuous struc-ture. This issue arises in retinal vessels [21], cerebral vessels [26, 27], hepatic vessels [2], pavement cracks [15], and road networks [7], where continuity is essential to structural in-tegrity. Such local errors can fragment a single structure into multiple components while producing only minor changes in conventional region-overlap metrics [20]. Reliable thin-structure segmentation therefore requires preserving connec-tivity in addition to accurate region and boundary prediction. ## 2. Related Work 

Centerline and skeleton supervision. Centerline- and skeleton-based objectives promote connectivity by supervis-ing thin structural representations derived from the predic-tion, target, or both. clDice [20] measures reciprocal overlap between the prediction and target using differentiable soft skeletons. Menten et al. [17] developed topology-preserving differentiable skeletonization for gradient-based losses. clCE [1] applies cross-entropy at centerline locations to improve topology while preserving region accuracy. cbDice [19] in-corporates boundary and radius cues to preserve geometry across varying widths. Skeleton Recall Loss (SkelRecall) [13] uses a dilated, class-labeled target skeleton and avoids prediction-side skeletonization. These methods, however, tie structural supervision to centerline or skeleton support and do not explicitly identify the prediction error that blocks a valid reconnection. TRACE addresses this gap with a topology-preserving carrier that defines the local same-class branches to be connected and extends pointwise supervision to the bottleneck of their easiest valid reconnection. 

Global topology supervision. Global topology objectives constrain connected components, loops, and cavities across the segmentation. TopoLoss [11] uses persistent homology to identify predictions associated with topological errors. Betti Matching [22] matches topological features between the prediction and target while accounting for their spatial correspondence. Subsequent extensions support multiclass segmentation [3]. Topograph [16] represents prediction– target component relations as a graph to provide efficient binary and multiclass constraints. Whereas these methods constrain global or component-level topology, TRACE tar-gets a required local reconnection and directly supervises its prediction-dependent bottleneck. 

Pairwise and path supervision. Pairwise- and path-based objectives supervise connectivity through neighboring el-ements, point pairs, or graph paths. MALIS [24] assigns pairwise connectivity errors to the weakest edge along a maximin path in an affinity graph. CAPE [10] penalizes shortest-path costs between selected target-graph nodes on a predicted distance map. SCNP [25] adjusts semantic log-its according to the weakest same-class neighbor. WPRF [28] optimizes widest-path reachability between sampled point pairs on a target-derived support graph using a separate affinity prediction. In contrast, TRACE defines each local reconnection on a class-labeled carrier that preserves the topology of both the foreground union and each semantic class. Removing a carrier point exposes all neighboring same-class branches, which we jointly reconnect via an ex-act search over at most 9 candidate points in 2D or 27 in 3D, using a centered logit margin computed directly from standard multiclass semantic logits. 

## 3. Method 

TRACE turns target-skeleton supervision from fixed-point enforcement into prediction-adaptive local reconnection by identifying and supervising the bottleneck of the easiest valid reconnection under the current prediction. We first formulate local reconnection as a connectivity bottleneck problem that requires a connected set of target-class points to reach all neighboring branches (Sec. 3.1). We then construct TASC, a thin class-labeled carrier that encodes these connectivity requirements while preserving both foreground and class-specific topology (Sec. 3.2). Next, we define a centered logit-margin score that ranks candidate points by their rival-to-target margin relative to the carrier average (Sec. 3.3). Finally, we combine the pointwise and connectivity penalties into a structural objective (Sec. 3.4).

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics 

Datasets. We evaluate TRACE on six thin-structure segmen-tation benchmarks spanning 2D and 3D data, including bi-nary and multiclass tasks. DRIVE [21] is used for retinal ves-sel segmentation and contains 40 RGB fundus images, split into 20/10/10 for training/validation/testing. DeepCrack [15] contains 537 RGB images for surface-crack segmentation (270/30/237), while DeepGlobe Roads [7] contains 6,226 RGB satellite images for road segmentation (4,980/622/624). For DSCA [27], we convert 224 cerebral digital subtraction angiography sequences, annotated for the main arterial trunk and bifurcated vessels, into 2D minimum-intensity projec-tions and use a 144/36/44 split. MSD Hepatic Vessel [2] contains 303 portal-venous-phase 3D CT scans with hepatic vessel and tumor annotations; we treat tumor voxels as back-ground for binary vessel segmentation and use a 243/30/30 split. TopCoW [26] contains 125 3D CT angiography scans with multiclass annotations for 13 Circle of Willis vessel segments, split into 100/12/13 cases. 

Metrics. We evaluate segmentation quality using com-plementary metrics for connectivity, topology, boundary accuracy, and region overlap. We first report clDice [20] to measure centerline consistency and connectivity preser-vation. We also report the absolute Betti number error, 

β = |∆β0| + |∆β1| [22], to quantify topological errors associated with structural connectivity. The 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95) [23] assesses boundary accuracy by measuring spatial disagreement while limiting outlier sensitivity. Finally, Dice [9] measures region overlap and assesses whether improved structural consistency is achieved without sacrificing overall segmentation accuracy. We com-pute each metric independently for each foreground class in every test sample. For multiclass datasets, we average the class-wise values to obtain one score per metric per sample, then average these per-sample scores over the entire test set. 

4.2. Main Comparison Setup 

Baselines. Our primary focus is connectivity preservation; therefore, we conduct extensive comparisons with represen-tative skeleton-based losses, including clDice [20], clDice with topology-preserving skeletonization (Topo-clDice) [17], clCE [1], cbDice [19], and SkelRecall [13]. We also include the recent multiclass extension of Betti Matching [3] as a consistent global-topology reference across binary and mul-ticlass benchmarks, using the projection-based protocol of [14] for 3D volumes. To isolate the effects of the train-ing objective, the main benchmark evaluates representative methods using standard semantic outputs while keeping the prediction head and inference pipeline fixed. We exclude methods that require alternative output formulations, aux-iliary or correction modules, or post-processing from this controlled comparison. 

Training protocol. We use a common nnU-Net [12] train-ing setup for all methods to ensure a controlled comparison. Following Eq. (11), we use Dice loss combined with cross-entropy loss (Dice + CE), the default nnU-Net objective, as Lseg , and replace LTRACE with each compared struc-tural loss while keeping all other training settings fixed. For TRACE and the skeleton-based losses, we select λ

from {0.10 , 0.30 , 0.60 , 1.00 } based on the validation Dice score. Betti Matching is tuned separately because exces-sive topological weighting can degrade segmentation perfor-mance [22]; its weight is selected on the validation set from 

{0.00001 , 0.0001 , 0.001 , 0.01 }.

4.3. Topology Preservation and Training Efficiency 

Topology preservation. We assess whether each method’s target-side skeleton representation preserves topology across deep-supervision scales. We use clDice [20] as a representa-tive differentiable soft-skeletonization method, Topo-clDice [17] as its topology-preserving counterpart, and SkelRecall [13] as a target skeleton-based method. At each active scale, we construct the skeleton from the corresponding training target using each method and record a topology violation if the Betti numbers change for the foreground union or any present semantic class. We report the percentage of target–scale pairs exhibiting at least one violation. 

Training efficiency. We evaluate the training overhead of TRACE against the same representative methods under an identical nnU-Net setup, with Dice + CE as the reference. We report training time per epoch and peak process GPU memory on an NVIDIA H200 (140 GB); runs exceeding the available GPU memory are reported as out of mem-ory (OOM). Topology preservation can still be evaluated in OOM cases, whereas training efficiency cannot be assessed without a completed epoch.

4.5. Ablation Study 

We perform a cumulative ablation to assess the contribu-tion of TRACE’s main components. We compare six con-figurations: the Dice + CE baseline (DCE) used in nnU-Net [12]; conventional target-skeleton recall (Skel) [13]; its scale-native TASC replacement (TASC); TASC with the centered logit-margin score (TASC-R); an unbounded recon-nection variant with ϕ(a) = a (TRACE-U); and the full ob-jective incorporating exact local reconnection and bounded penalty integration (TRACE). We evaluate all configurations on DRIVE. 

## 5. Result and Discussion

## 6. Conclusion 

We propose TRACE, an efficient topology-aware objective that reframes skeleton supervision as prediction-adaptive lo-cal reconnection. TASC provides a class-labeled topological carrier that preserves foreground and class-specific topology while identifying same-class branches that should remain connected. Based on this representation, TRACE identifies the easiest valid reconnection under the current prediction and supervises its critical bottleneck. Across six 2D and 3D benchmarks covering binary and multiclass segmentation, TRACE improves connectivity and topology while maintain-ing region-overlap and boundary accuracy. It also preserves target topology across deep-supervision scales, introduces only modest training overhead, and requires no architectural 8or inference-time modifications.
