A B S T R A C T
The semantic information of objects in remote sensing images is typically invariant to geometric trans-
formations from the dihedral group D4. However, SAM3-based open-vocabulary semantic segmen-
tation (OVSS) methods often exhibit inconsistent responses to different geometric transformations.
To exploit this property and improve the stability of OVSS for remote sensing images, we propose
a feature-adaptive manifold repair method based on dihedral-group geometric transformations. First,
we introduce multi-scale harmonic-guided D4 view selection (MH-D4VS) to select complementary
candidate views from a set of geometrically transformed views. Next, we propose original-view-
anchored adaptive manifold repair (OAMR), which uses the original view as an anchor and reliable
cross-view information to selectively repair locally unreliable visual features. Finally, we develop
pixel decoder test-time adaptation (PD-TTA) for SAM3, which fine-tunes only the parameters of
the GroupNorm layers online during inference, thereby enhancing the model’s ability to adapt to
sample-level distribution shifts. Experimental results show that the proposed method achieves an
average mIoU of 55.6% across eight remote sensing semantic segmentation benchmarks and delivers
consistent performance improvements under different SAM3-based inference frameworks.
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of Vision–Language Models
(VLMs) and Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), Open-
Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation (OVSS) has gradually
emerged as a major paradigm for overcoming the con-
straints of fixed label spaces. Existing OVSS methods can
be broadly categorized into training-based and training-
free approaches. The current mainstream paradigm is still
primarily built upon CLIP [1] and its adaptations for dense
prediction. These methods mainly rely on reconstructing the
internal attention maps or features of CLIP, as exemplified
by MaskCLIP [2], SCLIP [3], ClearCLIP [4], and ResCLIP
[5]. However, the CLIP image encoder is biased toward
relatively large objects, while its text encoder is sensitive to
the ordering of objects, as discussed in [6]. In fine-grained,
multi-object remote sensing scenes, these biases may further
exacerbate insufficient responses to local objects. Conse-
quently, CLIP-based approaches still struggle to fully satisfy
the requirements of remote sensing applications for precise
spatial localization.
To address this limitation, another line of research com-
bines CLIP with VFMs to exploit the finer-grained spa-
tial structure provided by models such as DINO [7, 8]
and SAM [9, 10]. Representative methods include Proxy-
CLIP [11], CorrCLIP [12], and SegEarth-OV [13]. Although
such multi-model collaboration can jointly preserve seman-
tic openness and structural resolution, the feature spaces,
resolutions, and response mechanisms of different models

SAM3 is designed for promptable concept segmentation and

the same ground object generally remain invariant under ge-
ometric transformations from the dihedral group D4. How-

because such transformations alter spatial sampling, local
context, and position-dependent representations. This study
seeks to exploit these response discrepancies to repair and
enhance visual representations, thereby improving segmen-
tation performance.

Building upon MH-D4VS, we further propose Original-

though the candidate views selected by MH-D4VS provide
complementary geometric information, visual features ex-
tracted under different D4 transformations exhibit varying
degrees of response shifts. Consequently, candidate features
at certain spatial locations may contain unstable or even
erroneous semantic activations. Simple multi-view aver-
aging or direct feature fusion may propagate these unre-
liable responses into the final representation, preventing
the model from fully exploiting the complementary advan-
tages of multi-view observations. Based on this observation,
OAMR treats the original view as both the spatial and
representation-state anchor. It evaluates feature consistency
across different views within a unified coordinate system and
establishes local reference structures using stable regions.
Selective repair is performed when the feature from the
original view deviates significantly from the local reference
structure and the candidate views provide representations
that are more consistent across views. In this manner, aux-
iliary views are used to correct locally unreliable responses
while preserving the stable information already contained in
the original-view representation.
After the multi-view geometric information has been
selected and repaired, the semantic responses of the model to
the current test sample may still be affected by cross-domain
distribution shifts. This issue is particularly prominent in re-
mote sensing imagery, where different regions often exhibit
substantial variations in imaging conditions, object scales,
texture distributions, and scene compositions. As a result,
the fixed feature distributions learned by a pretrained model
may not remain consistently reliable across different scenes.
To address this problem, we design Pixel Decoder Test-
Time Adaptation for SAM3 (PD-TTA), a test-time adapta-
tion strategy tailored to the SAM3 dense visual decoder. PD-
TTA uses the predictive uncertainty of the current sample as
a self-supervised signal and performs sample-specific distri-
bution recalibration by imposing an entropy-based constraint
on the decoder representations. This enables the model to
dynamically adjust its semantic responses according to the
current input and improves prediction stability under varying
scene conditions. Adaptation is performed independently
for each test sample without requiring additional annotated
data. During online adaptation, only the parameters of the
GroupNorm layers are fine-tuned, while all other model
parameters remain frozen. PD-TTA therefore improves the
stability of semantic predictions without modifying the main
inference architecture of SAM3.
The main contributions of this work are summarized as
follows:
1. We propose MH-D4VS, which characterizes direc-
tional structures using multi-scale complex harmonics
and chirality. By jointly considering structural pref-
erence, D4 geometric diversity, and transformation-
family balance, MH-D4VS adaptively selects com-
plementary non-identity views, thereby improving the
geometric stability of the feature representations pro-
duced by the pretrained SAM3 model.
2. We propose OAMR, which imposes reliability con-
straints on multi-view visual features through original-
view-anchored local manifold modeling. OAMR con-
structs a local manifold reference using an adaptive
local subspace and evaluates the manifold consistency
of the original and candidate features through refined
cross-view metrics. It repairs locations whose features
deviate significantly from the local manifold and are
supported by reliable candidate views. In addition,
Cross-Scale Guidance is introduced to provide com-
plementary fine-grained spatial evidence.
3. We design PD-TTA for the dense decoding architec-
ture of SAM3. By integrating MH-D4VS, OAMR, and
PD-TTA, we establish a new OVSS inference frame-
work for remote sensing imagery, termed Geometry-
Transformation-Aware Feature-Adaptive Manifold Restora-
tion (GFMR). Experiments on remote sensing datasets
demonstrate that GFMR achieves higher segmenta-
tion accuracy than existing OVSS methods.
2. Related Work
2.1. Training-Based OVSS
Training-based OVSS methods are typically built upon
large-scale pretrained Vision–Language Models (VLMs)
and further adapted to dense prediction tasks using data
with pixel-level or region-level annotations. According to
how visual representations are integrated with class se-
mantics, existing methods can be broadly divided into two
categories: mask-classification-based methods and dense-
feature-adaptation-based methods.
Mask-classification-based methods generally first gener-
ate class-agnostic regions or masks and then perform open-
vocabulary category recognition using visual–language sim-
ilarity. OpenSeg [15], ZegFormer [16], OVSeg [17], and
MasQCLIP [18] follow this paradigm. By decoupling re-
gion generation from open-vocabulary category recognition,
these methods extend the language semantics acquired
during pretraining to previously unseen categories. Another
line of research directly adapts the dense representations
of visual–language models. LSeg [19] constructs an open-
vocabulary prediction space through pixel-level visual–
language alignment. SAN [20], SED [21], and CAT-Seg
[22] further improve the spatial discriminability of visual–
language features using side networks, adaptation mod-
ules, or cost-aggregation mechanisms. SegCLIP [23] and
GroupViT [24] learn region grouping or visual-token aggre-
gation from image–text supervision, providing alternative
approaches to open-vocabulary region modeling.

Overall, training-based methods can explicitly reduce
the representation gap between image-level pretraining and
pixel-level prediction by leveraging task-relevant data, and
therefore generally exhibit strong adaptability to down-
stream tasks. However, their performance depends on addi-
tional training data, optimization procedures, and computa-
tional resources. Consequently, their deployment flexibility
is limited in applications involving rapidly changing cate-
gory spaces or scarce annotations.
2.2. Training-Free OVSS
Training-free OVSS aims to perform open-vocabulary
dense prediction by directly exploiting the visual and se-
mantic knowledge already encoded in pretrained models,
without optimizing model parameters on the target data.
Existing studies have primarily explored three directions:
extracting spatial information from the internal representa-
tions of CLIP, exploiting structural priors provided by Visual
Foundation Models (VFMs), and combining VLMs with
VFMs to jointly preserve open-vocabulary semantics and
fine-grained spatial representations. These three research
directions have ultimately begun to converge toward unified
foundation-model frameworks.
2.2.1. CLIP Internal Representation Adaptation
Methods in this category directly exploit the patch
representations and attention relationships already encoded
within CLIP, enabling a visual encoder originally designed
for image-level recognition to produce spatial responses
that are more suitable for pixel-level prediction. MaskCLIP
[2] directly recovers patch-level visual representations from
CLIP. SCLIP [3] and ClearCLIP [4] improve spatial con-
sistency by modifying token interactions within the self-
attention mechanism. NACLIP [25] further strengthens local
neighborhood relationships to improve localization capabil-
ity. ResCLIP [5], in contrast, exploits the spatial correlations
retained in intermediate layers to enhance high-level pixel-
wise dense representations.
These studies demonstrate that the internal representa-
tions of CLIP retain local information that can be used for
pixel-level prediction. Nevertheless, the CLIP pretraining
objective primarily focuses on global image–text alignment,
while local boundaries, spatial adjacency relationships, and
fine-grained object structures are not directly optimized.
Therefore, relying exclusively on the reconstruction of inter-
nal attention maps or patch representations remains insuf-
ficient to fully bridge the gap between image-level visual–
language pretraining and fine-grained spatial understanding.
2.2.2. Visual Foundation Models for Structure
Discovery
Visual foundation models generally provide more ex-
plicit local correspondences and category-agnostic spatial
structures than visual–language models. The DINO family
[7, 8] learns visual representations with strong local corre-
spondence capabilities through large-scale self-supervised
learning. Building upon such representations, TokenCut [26]
and CutLER [27, 28] further discover salient regions and
instance structures from self-supervised features. The SAM
family [9, 10], meanwhile, exhibits strong category-agnostic
region segmentation capabilities. Based on this capability,
E-SAM [29] constructs a training-free entity segmentation
framework that addresses the over-segmentation and under-
segmentation problems that may occur during automatic
mask generation with SAM. OmniSAM [30] further ex-
plores the ability of SAM to model cross-field-of-view in-
formation in panoramic scenes.
These methods can effectively characterize the regional
organization and object structures within an image. How-
ever, their visual embeddings are generally not directly
aligned with an open-vocabulary textual semantic space.
Consequently, VFMs are better suited to serving as sources
of spatial structure and have difficulty independently per-
forming OVSS.
2.2.3. VLM–VFM Collaborative OVSS
To jointly exploit the open-vocabulary semantic capa-
bilities of VLMs and the fine-grained spatial structures of
VFMs, multi-model collaboration has gradually become an
important direction in OVSS. Some studies enhance the
visual representations of arbitrary categories using external
visual references. For example, ReCo [31], OVDiff [32],
FOSSIL [33], and FreeDA [34] employ retrieval systems,
generative models, or offline visual references to construct
richer visual support information for textual categories.
Another category of methods directly establishes rep-
resentational connections between visual–language models
and self-supervised visual models. Talk2DINO [35] maps
the textual semantics of CLIP to the patch-level visual fea-
tures of DINO-v2, whereas CLIP-DINOiser [36] uses lo-
calization priors provided by self-supervised features to
improve the local representations of CLIP. Because these ap-
proaches require additional mapping modules or lightweight
feature learning, they differ to some extent from a strictly
training-free setting with no parameter updates.
In collaborative frameworks that strictly require no ad-
ditional training, a more common strategy is to use external
VFMs to provide spatial structural guidance. ProxyCLIP
[11] and CorrCLIP [12] improve the spatial responses of
CLIP by exploiting structural relationships derived from
external visual models. CASS [37] further introduces object-
level contextual information. Trident [38] jointly employs
foundation models such as CLIP, DINO, and SAM to en-
hance OVSS ability through their complementary represen-
tations.
Overall, VLM–VFM collaboration can simultaneously
preserve open-vocabulary semantics and spatial structure.
However, such approaches generally depend on multiple
foundation models with different pretraining objectives and
feature spaces, requiring feature mapping, attention guid-
ance, or cross-model information interaction to establish
connections among them.

2.2.4. From Heterogeneous Collaboration to Unified
Foundation Models
In contrast to the aforementioned heterogeneous model
collaboration paradigms, the introduction of SAM3 [14]
provides a new foundation for OVSS frameworks. SAM3 is
designed for promptable concept segmentation and jointly
models concept recognition and dense prediction within
a unified architecture, allowing open-vocabulary semantic
understanding and spatial segmentation to share the same
visual representation system. Based on this framework, Ac-
tiveSAM [39] further improves inference efficiency by dy-
namically selecting active categories according to the in-
put image, thereby reducing redundant computation during
large-scale open-vocabulary inference.
To some extent, SAM3 reduces the cross-model in-
terfaces and representational discrepancies associated with
conventional VLM–VFM collaboration. Nevertheless, a uni-
fied architecture does not necessarily imply that its visual
representations can naturally adapt to the spatial properties
of different data domains. For remote sensing imagery, ar-
bitrary object orientations caused by nadir-view imaging,
substantial scale variations, and densely distributed objects
continue to pose new challenges to spatial representation and
geometric adaptability.
2.3. OVSS for Remote Sensing Images
Extending OVSS to remote sensing imagery is not
merely a matter of transferring a model to a new data
domain. Nadir-view imaging means that objects belonging
to the same category generally lack a fixed orientation.
Variations in spatial resolution and object size also intro-
duce substantial scale differences, while high-resolution
scenes often contain densely distributed small objects, com-
plex boundaries, and large continuous regions. Therefore,
in addition to preserving semantic generalization to open
categories, remote sensing OVSS must explicitly address
challenges such as orientation and scale variations.
Existing studies have explored spatial-detail recovery
and domain adaptation [40, 41] for remote sensing imagery
from different perspectives. SegEarth-OV [13] restores high-
resolution visual–language representations through feature
upsampling. A related annotation-free extension [42] further
introduces AlignEarth, extending open-vocabulary segmen-
tation to the Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) modality.
GSNet [43] employs a dual-branch architecture to com-
bine remote-sensing-specific priors with general visual–
language semantics, whereas SkySense-O [44] enhances
open-world visual representations through remote-sensing-
specific visual–language pretraining. ConInfer [45] further
considers semantic dependencies among different spatial
units in large-scale remote sensing scenes and improves
regional prediction consistency through contextual joint
inference.
The strong category-agnostic spatial modeling capability
of the SAM family has also been increasingly introduced
into open-world remote sensing applications. AerOSeg [46]
and SCORE [47] use SAM features to provide structural
guidance and regional context. RemoteSAM [48] and In-
structSAM [49] utilize SAM-based pipelines to unify seg-
mentation with broader interpretation tasks. Following the
introduction of SAM3, SegEarth-OV3 [50] enhances its
application to training-free remote sensing OVSS through
prediction fusion and class-presence filtering. ProC-SAM3
[51], meanwhile, improves remote sensing OVSS inference
through prompt expansion and reliable-response aggrega-
tion.
Overall, existing remote sensing OVSS methods have
made continuous progress in high-resolution feature extrac-
tion, domain adaptation, cross-model collaboration, contex-
tual modeling, and the utilization of SAM-based foundation
models. Nevertheless, existing SAM3-based remote sensing
OVSS methods primarily focus on inference mechanisms
such as prediction fusion, category filtering, and prompt cal-
ibration, while the multi-view geometric information arising
from rotations and reflections in remote sensing imagery has
yet to be systematically exploited.
On the one hand, the structural information provided by
different geometric views is not equivalent for a given scene,
and simply enumerating all transformations can introduce
redundant observations. On the other hand, even after dif-
ferent views are realigned to a unified coordinate system, the
high-dimensional features at corresponding spatial locations
may still exhibit substantial response discrepancies. Direct
averaging or indiscriminate fusion may therefore propagate
unreliable responses. Consequently, identifying representa-
tive and complementary geometric information from the
multi-view observations generated by a unified foundation
model itself, and reliably exploiting this information to en-
hance the original visual representation, remains an impor-
tant research problem in OVSS for remote sensing imagery.
3. Methodology
3.1. Overall Framework
The overall pipeline of the proposed GFMR framework
for OVSS of remote sensing images is illustrated in Figure 1.
GFMR consists of three interconnected components. First,
MH-D4VS analyzes the multi-scale directional structures of
the input image and selects three candidate views with both
structural representativeness and geometric complementar-
ity from the seven non-identity D4 transformations. Subse-
quently, the features from the selected candidate views are
realigned to the original coordinate system. Taking the orig-
inal view as the anchor, OAMR constructs a local manifold
reference and combines adaptive subspace modeling with
cross-view consistency measurements to identify locally
anomalous features that deviate from the manifold. Selective
repair is performed when the candidate views provide more
reliable representations. Finally, PD-TTA uses the semantic
uncertainty of the current test image to perform sample-
specific adaptation of the affine parameters of the Group-
Norm layers online in the PixelDecoder that participate in
the forward pass.

3.2. Multi-Scale Harmonic-Guided D4 View
Selection
Roads, building boundaries, and other artificial struc-
tures in remote sensing images often exhibit prominent
directional patterns. Different rotated and reflected views
alter the relative relationships between these structures and
their local contexts, thereby producing diverse visual re-
sponses. However, the structural contributions of different
D4 transformations are not equivalent for a given scene.
Directly enumerating all transformations not only introduces
redundant observations but also increases the computational
cost of subsequent multi-view feature extraction. To address
this issue, we propose the Multi-Scale Harmonic-Guided D4
View Selection (MH-D4VS) module as shown in figure 2.
Before visual encoding, MH-D4VS selects three representa-
tive and geometrically complementary candidate views from
the seven non-identity D4 transformations according to the
directional structures of the input image.
MH-D4VS uses the directional structures of the input
image as the basis for view selection. It characterizes the
relative preferences of different geometric views through
image-domain structural responses and further constrains
the geometric complementarity among candidates at the set
level. Specifically, the module first describes the directional
structures of the input image using multi-scale complex har-
monics and introduces chirality to capture the handedness
differences between rotations and reflections. It then derives
the structural response of each transformation through the
analytical action of the D4 group in the harmonic domain.
Finally, the optimal three-view combination is determined
by jointly considering structural quality, geometric diversity,
and transformation-family balance.

3.3. Original-Anchored Adaptive Multi-View
Manifold Repair (OAMR)
Following MH-D4VS, we obtain a multi-view set con-
sisting of the original view 𝑂and three complementary
candidate views 𝑃, 𝑆, and 𝑇. Although these views depict
the same scene, their encoded visual features reside in differ-
ent geometric coordinate systems. Even after spatial align-
ment, the high-dimensional features at corresponding loca-
tions may differ in reliability. Direct cross-view averaging
or indiscriminate fusion may propagate locally anomalous
responses into the final representation and compromise the
stable semantic information already present in the original
view.
To address this issue, we propose Original-Anchored
Adaptive Manifold Repair (OAMR) as shown in figure 3.
OAMR first identifies reliable locations based on cross-view
consistency and constructs local manifold references from
actual view features. It then characterizes the multimodal
structure of high-dimensional features using adaptive local
subspaces and further refines cross-view reliability estimates
through local tangent spaces and neighborhood relation-
ships. Finally, complete feature replacement is performed

3.3.5. Cross-Scale Guidance
Visual features at the target repair level have relatively
low spatial resolution, with each feature location corre-
sponding to a comparatively large image region. Conse-

therefore introduces cross-scale guidance, using a high-

of candidate sources and supplement local decisions at the
target repair level.

entire feature maps.
3.4. Pixel Decoder Test-Time Adaptation
The preceding multi-view selection and manifold repair
enhance the adaptability of SAM3 to geometric transfor-
mations in remote sensing imagery at the input-observation
and visual-representation levels, respectively. The repaired
visual features continue through the original cross-modal
encoding, MaskDecoder, and PixelDecoder pathways of
SAM3 to produce pixel-level predictions. However, the
affine parameters of the GroupNorm layers in the PixelDe-
coder are pretrained on source-domain data, and their nor-
malization behavior may not adequately accommodate vari-
ations in appearance and scene distributions across remote
sensing images. We therefore further propose Pixel Decoder
Test-Time Adaptation (PD-TTA) as shown in figure 4 for
SAM3.
PD-TTA introduces no separate adaptation branch and
preserves the original input and output formats of SAM3.
Instead, it directly designates the affine parameters of the
GroupNorm layers that participate in the PixelDecoder for-
ward pass as the parameters to be adapted at test time. An
unsupervised loss is then constructed from the responses
produced by the original semantic prediction pathway of
SAM3.

At test time, the entropy loss is computed from the
semantic feedback of SAM3 described above. Backpropaga-

𝛾and 𝛽within the PixelDecoder, which are updated using
the Adam optimizer. Throughout optimization, the text en-

frozen. After the predefined number of test-time updates,
the adapted GroupNorm parameters are used directly in
the existing PixelDecoder, and the actual inference pass

coding, MaskDecoder, and semantic prediction pathways of
SAM3. PD-TTA therefore confines adaptation to normaliza-

4. Experiments
We evaluate the proposed method on eight widely used
remote sensing semantic segmentation datasets: OpenEarthMap
[53], LoveDA [54], iSAID [55], UAVid [56], UDD5 [57],
VDD [58], Potsdam and Vaihingen [59]. Mean Intersection-
over-Union (mIoU) is used as the main evaluation metric
across all datasets.
4.1. Datasets
The introduction of the employed eight remote sensing
datasets are shown as follows:
(1) OpenEarthMap consists of high-resolution remote
sensing images acquired from aerial and satellite platforms,
covering 44 countries and regions worldwide. The imagery
has a spatial resolution of approximately 0.25–0.50 m, pri-
marily contains three visible RGB bands, and provides an-
notations for eight land-cover classes.
(2) LoveDA contains high-resolution remote sensing
images obtained through Google Earth, with a spatial resolu-
tion of approximately 0.30 m and three visible RGB bands. It
covers both urban and rural scenes and includes seven land-
cover classes.
(3) iSAID is built upon the high-resolution aerial im-
agery of DOTA-v1.0. Its images originate from multiple
sources, including Google Earth, GF-2, and JL-1, with spa-
tial resolutions varying by source. The dataset primarily
contains RGB imagery, together with some panchromatic
imagery, and provides annotations for 15 typical object
categories.
(4) UAVid consists of high-resolution urban video im-
agery captured from oblique viewpoints using unmanned
aerial vehicles (UAVs), primarily in RGB format. Because
the imagery is acquired through low-altitude oblique imag-
ing, its ground spatial resolution varies with the imaging
distance and viewing angle. The dataset includes eight cate-
gories of urban land cover and objects.
(5) UDD5 contains three-channel RGB imagery ac-
quired by UAVs across multiple urban and campus scenes at
flight altitudes of approximately 60–100 m. It comprises five
classes: vegetation, buildings, roads, vehicles, and others.
(6) VDD contains high-resolution RGB imagery cap-
tured using a DJI Mavic Air II UAV across urban, indus-
trial, rural, and natural scenes. Its ground spatial resolution
varies with viewing angle and flight conditions. The dataset
includes seven land-cover classes and covers diverse viewing
angles and illumination conditions.
(7) Potsdam and (8) Vaihingen are both part of the ISPRS
2D Semantic Labeling Benchmark and consist of very-high-
resolution orthophotos acquired from aerial platforms. Their
spatial resolutions are approximately 0.05 m and 0.099 m,
respectively. Potsdam provides four-band imagery compris-
ing RGB and near-infrared (NIR) bands, whereas Vaihingen
primarily contains NIR, red, and green (NIR–R–G) bands.
Both datasets include six urban land-cover classes.
Together, these datasets span satellite, aerial, and UAV
imaging platforms and exhibit substantial differences in spa-
tial resolution, object scale, and viewpoint. They therefore
enable a comprehensive evaluation of OVSS performance
across diverse remote sensing scenarios.
4.2. Implementation Details
We use the officially released SAM3 model with its
Perception Encoder-Large+ (PE-L+) visual backbone and
follow the text-prompting and prediction-fusion settings of
SegEarth-OV3 [50] by default. GFMR requires no dataset-
level training on the target datasets. Except for the Group-
Norm affine parameters updated online by PD-TTA during
prediction for each individual image, the visual encoder, text
encoder, PixelDecoder convolutional layers, instance head,
semantic head, and all other module parameters of SAM3
remain frozen. The original mask-generation and result-
fusion procedures are preserved.
MH-D4VS and OAMR use the same inference config-
uration across all datasets, without parameter adjustments
for individual test sets. MH-D4VS performs candidate geo-
metric view selection before visual encoding, while OAMR
operates on the aligned multi-view visual features and pre-
serves the spatial reference of the original view and the
subsequent prediction pipeline. None of them requires gradi-
ent computation or model parameter updates; together, they
form the training-free visual adaptation core of GFMR.
The PD-TTA settings are fixed across all experiments
as 𝜂= 1 × 10−3, 𝑇= 1, 𝑆max = 72, and 𝛼cls = 1.0. A
new Adam optimizer is initialized for each image, and the
SAM3’s default GroupNorm parameters are restored after
the actual prediction pass. Consequently, neither adapted
parameters nor optimizer momentum is carried over between
images.

5. Conclusion
This work proposes GFMR to address the underutiliza-
tion of geometric information, inconsistent local representa-
tions across views, and distribution shifts between samples
in SAM3-based OVSS of remote sensing images. Without
modifying the core architecture or original prediction path-
way of SAM3, GFMR integrates geometry-aware view se-
lection, original-view-anchored manifold repair, and online
PixelDecoder adaptation at test time into a unified inference
pipeline. Specifically, MH-D4VS uses multi-scale complex
harmonics, chirality, and set-level geometric constraints to
select three representative and complementary D4 candidate
views. Anchored to the original view, OAMR uses adaptive
local subspaces, refined cross-view metrics, and cross-scale
guidance to repair locally anomalous representations using
only candidate features with reliable support. PD-TTA uses
local–global semantic entropy computed from the outputs
of the frozen semantic head as a self-supervised signal to
update the affine parameters of two GroupNorm layers in the
PixelDecoder online during image segmentation. MH-D4VS
and OAMR require no gradient updates, while PD-TTA
requires neither target-dataset-level training nor additional
annotations.
Experiments on eight remote sensing benchmarks demon-
strate that GFMR achieves an average mIoU of 55.6%, with
consistent gains across satellite, aerial, and UAV imagery.
Transferring MH-D4VS and OAMR to another SAM3-
based inference framework, ProC-SAM3, also yields sub-
stantial segmentation improvements, demonstrating the com-
patibility of the proposed modules with different frame-
works. Ablation studies further confirm the complementar-
ity of the three components: the training-free MH-D4VS and
OAMR modules account for the primary performance gains,
while PD-TTA provides consistent additional improvements
at the sample level. There remains considerable room to
improve the effectiveness of test-time adaptation (TTA)
for SAM3 inference. Future work will further investigate
the integration of TTA strategies into the SAM3 inference
process.
