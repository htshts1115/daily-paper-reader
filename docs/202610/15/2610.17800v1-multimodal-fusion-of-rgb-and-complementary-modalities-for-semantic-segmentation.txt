Abstract 

Multi-modal semantic segmentation adds an auxiliary sensing stream to RGB images (RGB-X), such as thermal, LiDAR, depth, event, polarization, or light field. This can improve robustness in poor lighting and under motion blur. But different sensors often produce features that do not match well, and fusion can become unstable. To alleviate these issues, we propose a bidirectional polarity-aware cross-modality fusion (BPCF) module that effectively captures complementary cues while enhancing feature alignment. We evaluate the framework on six modality pairings: RGB-Thermal, RGB-LiDAR, RGB-Depth, RGB-Event, RGB-Polarization, and RGB-Light Field. Our model achieve state-of-the-art (SOTA) results on eight public datasets, including MFNet, KITTI-360, DELIVER, DDD17, DSEC, MCubeS, ZJU, and UrbanLF. The codes and trained models will be released upon acceptance.  

> Keywords: Semantic Segmentation, Multi-modal Fusion, Polarity-aware Cross-attention, Cross-modal Feature Alignment

# 1 Introduction 

In autonomous driving and robotic perception, RGB-only semantic segmentation often suffers from performance degradation under low illumination, fast motion, and adverse weather conditions [1, 2]. Fortunately, non-RGB modalities provide complementary cues that can mitigate these limitations. For instance, incorporating thermal, LiDAR, depth, event, light field, and polarization modality data can enhance robustness against low-light conditions, motion blur, and adverse weather conditions [1, 3]. Nevertheless, achieving reliable multi-modal fusion that scales to many sensors and consistently leverages each modality’s strengths remains an open challenge. The problem can be considered from two perspectives. The first is the efficient utilization of comple-mentary information. In multi-modal scenarios such as RGB–Thermal or RGB–Event, the same object may exhibit reversed attention responses across modalities. Consequently, cross-modal interactions in RGB-X semantic segmentation are not purely positively correlated. Depending on the sensor physics and scene conditions, the same semantic structure can elicit aligned responses across modalities or reversed responses. Importantly, these negative correlations are not necessarily noise. Our key observation is that these contrastive responses can be indicative: they can reveal complementary cues that are hard to cap-ture while the fusion mechanism assumes only non-negative similarity. Recognizing that such negative correlations can also provide valuable cues, we propose a bidirectional polarity-aware linear cross-attention (BPLCA) mechanism. BPLCA decomposes features into positive and negative components and performs cross-modal attention across both branches. A second challenge in RGB-X semantic segmenta-tion is cross-modal feature alignment. Heterogeneous modalities (e.g., RGB, thermal, LiDAR, depth, events, and polarization) differ substantially in their statis-tics and appearance, making naive fusion prone to inconsistent representations or over-reliance on a single modality [2, 4, 5]. To address this, we introduce a Dual Feature Consistency Constraint (DFCC) that explicitly encourages cross-modal features to become semantically coherent, thereby narrowing the modality gap and stabi-lizing fusion. DFCC aligns RGB and auxiliary features by first reducing distribution mismatch, and then apply-ing scene-adaptive affine modulation whose parameters are predicted from fused features. During fusion, it fur-ther regularizes cross-modal semantics by constructing spatial attention maps at each stage and penalizing their discrepancies, encouraging consistent attention and representations while retaining complementary cues. With BPLCA and DFCC integrated into our frame-work (together with the proposed designs above), we achieve consistent improvements and outperform recent SOTA methods on eight multi-modal datasets (Fig. 1). The main contributions of this work are as follows: • We propose a multi-modal semantic segmentation framework that supports flexible modality combi-nations. It enables effective representation learning across diverse RGB-X configurations, including RGB-Thermal, RGB-LiDAR, RGB-Depth, RGB-Event, RGB-Polarization, and RGB-Light Field. • We develop a bidirectional polarity-aware cross-modality fusion (BPCF) module, which integrates bidirectional polarity-aware linear cross-attention (BPLCA) with a dual feature consistency constraint (DFCC) to effectively fuse heterogeneous modalities. • We introduce a stage-wise constraint loss that progressively enforces consistent cross-modal feature alignment. • We conduct extensive experiments on eight multi-modal datasets covering six auxiliary modalities. The results show that our method consistently outperforms SOTA approaches across all datasets. 

# 2 Related Work 

## 2.1 Semantic segmentation 

Semantic segmentation has progressed from CNN-based architectures such as FCN [6], U-Net [7], and SegNet [8], to encoder–decoder designs like DeepLabv3+ [9]. More recently, Transformer architectures have become dominant, with models including ViT [10], Swin Transformer [11], SegFormer [12], SDPT [13], serving as fundamental baselines for RGB-only dense prediction. Despite this progress, performance degrades under motion blur, low light, or rain/snow. This motivates multi-modal RGB+X architectures for robust scene understanding, where X denotes auxiliary modalities such as thermal, LiDAR, depth, event cameras, polarization, and light field. 

## 2.2 Multi-modal semantic segmentation 

Prior work recognizes the complementary value of non-RGB modalities for semantic segmentation. It explores fusion methods from pairwise RGB–X integration to modality-agnostic frameworks that unify multiple sensor types. For RGB+event combination, [14–19] integrate events over time and use event-count or activity-rate cues, then use dual-branch backbones together with bidirectional cross-attention, to synchronize features across modalities and scales. Several studies also evaluate fusion with auxiliary modalities beyond events. Their results indicate that non-RGB modalities provide powerful complementary cues for scene understanding [20–22]. KTBNet [23] proposes a parameter-efficient symmetric framework that balances the contributions between RGB and an additional modality such as thermal, thereby preventing the dominance of a single branch. Moreover, better-known works on RGB–P segmentation has shown only limited performance with these dual branch architecture [2, 24]. Recently, PolaFormer [25] introduces a learnable mechanism with power-based scaling to explicitly model both positive and negative query-key interactions. PolaFormer targets single-modality linear self-attention, so its polarity design mainly repairs attention expressiveness by recovering missing negative interactions. In contrast, our BPLCA/PLCA targets cross-modality fusion, so its polarity design mainly structures inter-modal evidence by separating shared cues from complementary cues to improve fusion. Nevertheless, the above designs lack modality-agnostic generalization, as they are tied to fixed modality combinations. CMX [4] employs cross-modal feature rectification and fusion modules for long-range context exchange across modalities. Its successor CMNeXt [2] extends scalability through a self-query hub and a parallel pooling mixer, maintaining compactness while adapting to diverse sensor types. Similarly, Zheng et al. [26] propose a modality-agnostic pipeline that dynamically selects the most informative modalities, while their follow-up [18] introduces a modality-agnostic Feature Fusion (MFF) module that synthesizes heterogeneous sensor streams into a unified representation. GeminiFusion [27] further refines cross-modal interactions using pixel-wise intra- and inter-modal attention, and OmniVec2 [28] explores a large-scale shared representation space for multi-modal and multi-task learning. Although these works have advanced the field, challenges still remain in fully exploiting complementary information across modalities and in achieving consistent feature alignment. In this work, we propose a bidirectional polarity-aware cross-modality fusion (BPCF) module to alleviate these issues. 

# 3 Method 

As shown in Fig. 2, our model adopts an encoder–decoder architecture with stage-wise fusion modules. Each modality is first encoded by a dedicated Transformer backbone [12]. Following prior work [2, 4], we incorporate ScoreNet to flexibly integrate RGB with one or more auxiliary modalities. For multiple auxiliary inputs, ScoreNet selects the most informative feature at each spatial location. The stage-wise BPCF fusion modules then combine the hierarchical feature representations from the two modality branches. Finally, the fused multi-scale features are aggregated by an MLP-based segmentation head to produce final predictions.

# 4 Experiments 

## 4.1 Experimental Setup 

4.1.1 Datasets 

We evaluate our approach on eight widely used multi-modal datasets: MFNet [30], KITTI-360 [31], DDD17 [32], DSEC [33], DELIVER [2], UrbanLF [34], MCubeS [24], and ZJU [35]. An overview of the datasets is provided in Tab. 1; additional dataset details and common configuration parameters are provided in Sec. 4.1.1 and Sec. 4.1.2. An overview of the datasets used in our experiments is provided in Tab. 1. The details are introduced below: 

RGB-T MFNet [30] is a driving-scene dataset with 1,569 aligned RGB-thermal pairs across 8 semantic classes. It provides 784/392/392 images for training, validation, and testing, respectively, covering both daytime and nighttime conditions. 

RGB-L KITTI-360 [31] is a suburban driving dataset, providing 49,004 training and 12,276 validation images with a resolution of 1408 × 376. Following the Cityscapes dataset [36], it defines 19 semantic classes. 

RGB-X DELIVER [2] is a large-scale synthetic benchmark built in the CARLA simulator, supporting multi-modal segmentation across RGB, depth, event, and LiDAR streams. It contains over 47k annotated frames from six camera views under diverse conditions, including challenging weather (fog, rain, night) and five sensor degradation types (e.g., motion blur, exposure imbalance, LiDAR jitter). The dataset defines 25 semantic classes covering urban elements such as vehicles, roads, pedestrians, and vegetation. 

RGB-E DDD17 [32] includes over 12 hours of driving data collected with a DAVIS346B sensor, providing event streams and grayscale images at 200 × 346 resolution. We generate pseudo-labels using EV-SegNet [37], resulting in 5 semantic classes and splits of 15,950/3,890 samples. 

RGB-E DSEC [33] contains over 10k RGB-event frames with 11 categories, captured from stereo cameras in urban and rural environments. We follow [16] for preprocessing and dataset splits (8082/2809 for training/testing). 

RGB-P MCubeS [24] consists of 500 samples with aligned RGB, NIR, and polarization cues (DoLP, AoLP), annotated with 20 material categories. The dataset is split into 302/96/102 for training, validation, and testing. 

RGB-P ZJU [35] is a polarization semantic segmentation dataset collected in outdoor scenes. Each sample contains four polarized RGB images at 0 ◦, 45 ◦, 90 ◦, and 135 ◦, from which AoLP and DoLP are derived using Stokes parameters. It provides 344 training and 50 validation samples across 8 semantic classes, with image resolution of 1224 × 1024. 

RGB-LF UrbanLF [34] is a light-field dataset for urban-scene segmentation, including both real (580/80/164) and synthetic (172/28/50) subsets, each with 14 semantic classes. Central-view annotations are used for supervision.

4.1.3 Evaluation Metrics 

We evaluate model performance using a set of standard metrics for semantic segmentation, including per-class Intersection over Union (IoU), mean IoU (mIoU), mean IoU excluding the background class. These metrics jointly provide a comprehensive assessment of segmentation quality across categories and pixels, while also accounting for class imbalance. In addition, we report computational efficiency in terms of FLOPs, number of parameters, and inference speed (FPS) to evaluate the practicality of our models for real-world deployment. Detailed metric definitions are provided below. 

Intersection over Union (IoU): 

IoU quantifies the overlap between the predicted and ground truth segmentation masks for each class. It is defined as: IoU c = |Ac ∩ Bc||Ac ∪ Bc| , (25) where Ac and Bc denote the predicted and ground truth pixels for class c, respectively. A higher IoU indicates better boundary delineation and region matching. 

Mean IoU (mIoU): 

The primary evaluation metric is mIoU, which averages IoU scores across all classes, ensuring a balanced evaluation regardless of class frequency. Formally, mIoU = 1

## 4.6 Analysis of ScoreNet Dynamic Modality Selection 

To further investigate whether ScoreNet performs meaningful dynamic modality selection, we visualize its decision heatmaps at different encoder stages. ScoreNet assigns an informativeness score to each auxiliary modality at every spatial patch and selects the modality with the highest score to construct a unified auxiliary representation before stage-wise RGB-auxiliary fusion. Therefore, the routing decision is spatially adaptive rather than image-wise or modality-wise fixed. As shown in Fig.8, the ScoreNet responses exhibit clear stage-dependent and modality-dependent patterns. In shallow stages, the score maps contain more fine-grained and high-frequency responses, indicating that early routing mainly focuses on local texture, boundary, and low-level appearance variations. In contrast, the deeper stages show smoother and more semantically organized activation regions, where high-score responses are aligned with object-level structures such as vehicles, road boundaries, and background regions. This suggests that ScoreNet progressively shifts from low-level local selection to high-level semantic modality routing. Moreover, the decision maps of different auxiliary modalities are not identical. For example, AoLP and DoLP exhibit complementary high-response regions across the scene, indicating that ScoreNet does not collapse to a static preference for a single modality. # 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we presented a multi-modal semantic segmentation framework that flexibly integrates RGB with diverse auxiliary modalities, including thermal, LiDAR, depth, event, light field, and polarization data. Our approach features a selective fusion mechanism that dynamically activates the most informative auxiliary modality at each spatial location, a bidirectional polarity-aware linear cross-attention (BPLCA) combined with a dual feature consistency constraint (DFCC) for feature-aligned fusion, and a stage-wise supervision loss that progressively enforces cross-modal consistency. Extensive experiments on eight public 15 benchmarks, including MFNet, KITTI-360, DELIVER, DDD17, DSEC, MCubeS, ZJU, and UrbanLF, demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms SOTA approaches.
