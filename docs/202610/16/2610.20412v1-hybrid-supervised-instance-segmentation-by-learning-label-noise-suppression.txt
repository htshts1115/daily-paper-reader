To reach top accuracy, current fully supervised instance segmentation methods severely rely on large-scale pixel-wise labeled datasets. They are usually expensive and time-consuming to obtain. Though weakly or semi-supervised methods utilize cheap bounding box labeled, image-level labeled or unlabeled samples to save the labeling cost, their performance is largely sacrificed. To save labeling cost without losing much performance, in this paper, we present a pipeline that can utilize economical bounding box labels and accurate pixel-wise labels in a hybrid way. Specifically, we design two ancillary models to learn label noise suppression and obtain accurate pseudo pixel-wise labels from bounding box labels for training. One is designed to suppress mislabeling between foreground and background, and the other is designed to suppress noise from mislabeling of instances. Moreover, we exploit category-aware spatial attention module, category constraint module, instance constraint module, and self-learning training approach to improve the accuracy of pseudo labels. Experiments on the PASCAL VOC 2012 and the Cityscapes datasets show that our method can achieve competitive performance with much less labeling cost.

1. Introduction 

Instance segmentation is a fundamental task in computer vision and artificial intelligence. It plays a crucial role in autonomous driving [16], robotics [19], medical image diagnosis [55] and etc. 

Recently, fully supervised instance segmentation methods [23,24,45,11,38] have been intensively studied and they profit from a large amount of training data to reach top performance. However, their accuracy heavily relies on the quantity and quality of pixel-wise labeled training data, which is labor-intensive and expensive to create [18,42,14]. To be precise, drawing a segmenta-tion on an object needs 79 s [6] while obtaining a bounding box needs only 7 s [48], which is about 11 :3 more expensive. To reduce labeling cost, some weakly supervised methods [64,21,1,66,13,30,39,26,4] and semi-supervised methods [7] have been proposed. Instead of using pixel-wise labeled samples, they utilize economical image-level labeled [64,21,1,66,13], bounding box labeled [30,39,26,4] or unlabeled samples [7] for learning. Among them, weakly supervised methods [26,4] using bounding box supervision achieve the best performance. Nevertheless, they still have a large performance gap compared with fully supervised methods [24,38], which is unacceptable in practical usage. We attribute this to strong and notorious label noise in bounding box labels, and existing works [26,4] cannot handle it well. Specifically, we identify two types of noise in bounding box labels for instance segmentation, i.e ., background noise and instance noise, as illus-trated in Fig. 1. Background noise mainly comes from the uncertain foreground and background separation within each bounding box. Instance noise is caused by the overlap of multiple bounding boxes, resulting in confusion when assigning pixels in the overlapping area to different instances. Therefore, suppressing these two types of label noise in bounding box labels remains the key problem for achieving decent performance with low labeling cost. To solve this problem, in this paper, we propose a novel hybrid supervised pipeline that combines full supervision and weak supervision. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the proposed pipeline consists of three models, including a semantic ancillary model, an instance ancillary model, and a primary instance segmentation model. The semantic ancillary model is designed to suppress background noise by learning semantic segmentation. And the instance ancillary model is designed to further suppress instance noise by learning instance segmentation. Then, any instance segmentation model can be trained with bounding box labels in a weakly super-vised way. Though the whole idea is straightforward, there are two challenging problems for us to achieve decent performance. First, how to fully utilize rich information in bounding box labels to cor-rect pseudo labels? Second, how to avoid overfitting when the number of pixel-wise labels is limited? To address the first challenge, we propose a category-aware spatial attention module, a category constraint module, and an instance constraint module. The category-aware spatial attention module directly fuses information of bounding box labels and intermediate semantic segmentation results ( i.e ., information of category, location, and shape of objects) into the deep features of CNN, helping ancillary models to generate more accurate pseudo labels. The category constraint module and instance constraint module utilize the label consistency prior ( i.e ., the segmentation mask should be inside the bounding box) to refine the segmenta-tion results. For the second challenge, we exploit the self-learning training approach. During training, it utilizes pseudo labels generated from bounding box labeled images by ancillary models to optimize themselves. To avoid introducing noise, we ignore loss from the noisy area, e.g ., an area where two bounding boxes overlap. The self-learning training approach can not only avoid overfitting with limited pixel-wise labels but also improve the label noise suppression performance. Experiments on the PASCAL VOC 2012 [18] and Citycapes [14] show that our hybrid supervised method can largely boost the per-formance compared with corresponding fully supervised baseline, and outperform some former state-of-the-art fully supervised instance segmentation methods, even with only 1% pixel-wise labels. We also extend our method to semantic segmentation task on the PASCAL VOC 2012 [18] and achieve better performance than a fully supervised setting with more than 75% reduction to the labeling cost. The main contributions of this work can be summarized as follow: 

 We present a pipeline that combines the advantages of the low labeling cost from bounding box labels and high accuracy from pixel-wise labels. It can fully utilize both types of labels in a hybrid way and well balance the performance and labeling cost. 

 We design a semantic ancillary model and an instance ancillary model, which can effectively suppress background noise and instance noise in bounding box labels. 

 We exploit category-aware spatial attention module, category constraint module, instance constraint module, and self-learning training approach in ancillary models to fully utilize bounding box labels and avoid overfitting with limited pixel-wise labels. 

2. Related Work 

In this section, we first review semantic segmentation methods and instance segmentation methods, then we introduce semi-supervised and weakly supervised instance segmentation methods. 

Semantic segmentation. As a dense-prediction task, semantic segmentation assigns each pixel in an image to a specific category.

Fully convolutional networks (FCNs) [44] are the milestone in semantic segmentation. Later, a series of fully supervised methods [58,37] improve the accuracy of semantic segmentation by using contextual information [20,28,65], exploiting the multi-scale infor-mation [12,61,56,62], enhancing the fusion of features from differ-ent levels [60,59], or learning boundary information of targets [53]. Our ancillary models are based on one of these works, i.e ., DeepLabv3+ [12], which is reliable and easy to train. 

Instance segmentation. Mainstream instance segmentation methods [24,11,9,10,38] first detect the objects with bounding boxes and then describe their shapes in binary masks. Another branch of instance segmentation methods [45,35,46,47] rely on dense-prediction, which first assigns a semantic category to each pixel, and then separates pixels into different instances. Currently, the state-of-the-art results are usually achieved by two-stage methods [24,11], which are based on reliable two-stage detectors like Faster R-CNN [49]. With the development of one-stage detec-tors [41,54], instance segmentation methods based on one-stage detector can achieve competitive results both in speed and perfor-mance [9,38,10]. To verify the effectiveness of our method, we choose a two-stage based method ( i.e ., Mask R-CNN [24]) and a one-stage based method ( i.e ., CenterMask [38]) as the primary instance segmentation model in our pipeline. 

Semi-supervised instance segmentation. Though fully super-vised instance segmentation methods [24,11,9,10,38,45] achieve reasonable performance, the labeling cost is unbearable. To address this problem, Bellver et al. [7] present a semi-supervised method for instance segmentation. It predicts pseudo labels on unlabeled images to augment the dataset. However, the pseudo-labels are often inaccurate for outlier examples, the noise and false positives in pseudo labels limit their performance. Li et al. [39] uti-lize both pixel-wise labels and bounding box labels for semi-supervised instance segmentation. It relies on [2,50] to obtain pseudo pixel-wise labels from bounding box labeled images, which impedes them to achieve similar performance with full supervi-sion. A series of recent researches [27,36,63] aim to predict object masks for categories whose shapes are unknown in the dataset. They assume costly pixel-wise labels are available for a subset of categories and other categories have only bounding box labels. Although they make good progress in this task, the significant dif-ference in shape between different categories limits their performance. 

Weakly supervised instance segmentation. To save labeling cost, a few weakly supervised methods have been proposed, and they usually use image-level labels [64,21,1,66,13] or bounding boxes [30,26,39,4]. For image-level supervision, Zhou et al. [64] make use of class peak responses to locate objects and extract instance masks with some off-the-shelf methods [2]. Ge et al. [21] refine image-level labels to pixel-wise labels with a cascaded pipeline that is trained alternatively with a curriculum learning strategy. Ahn et al. [1] exploit the inter-pixel relations on class attention maps for training and discover instances by estimating rough locations of objects and detecting boundaries between dif-ferent instances. Zhu et al. [66] use a novel instance extent filling approach to learn the full extent of pixel-level instance response. Cholakkal et al. [13] construct an object category density map to provide both the global object count and the spatial distribution of object instances. Though they largely reduce the labeling cost by using image-level labels and achieve promising results, their performance in instance segmentation is still much lower than fully supervised methods. As for bounding box supervision, Khoreva et al. [30] and Li et al. [39] utilize GrabCut [50] and MCG [2] to propose instance masks from bounding box for learning. Hsu et al. [26] train the segmenta-tion model with bounding box labels in an end-to-end way with multiple instance learning strategy. Though their results are better than the image-level supervised method, they can only partially suppress the background noise and cannot deal with the instance noise. These limit their performance. To balance the performance and labeling cost, we utilize bounding boxes for saving the labeling cost while maintaining a small portion of pixel-wise labels for learning label noise suppression. 

3. Methodology 

In this section, we first introduce our motivation and formula-tion, then we describe our pipeline and details of the proposed category-aware spatial attention module, category constraint mod-ule, instance constraint module, and self-learning training approach. Finally, we introduce the loss function for learning. 

3.1. Motivation and Formulation 

Instance segmentation is a task that aims to jointly detect and separate each distinct foreground object of interest from the back-ground. Our goal is to save the labeling cost while keeping the per-formance for instance segmentation. As shown in Table 1, both image-level label and bounding box label can largely save the labeling cost, but image-level label lacks instance-level informa-tion which is crucial for achieving satisfactory performance, there-fore bounding box label is a better choice for our goal. Obviously, compared with ground truth instance-level pixel-wise label Y, the bounding box label B is not accurate enough and contains two types of label noise, i.e ., background noise and instance noise as shown in Fig. 1, which inevitably leads to inferior performance of weakly supervised methods [30,39,26,4]. In the ground truth instance-level pixel-wise label Y, each pixel x in image X is assigned to the correct category and instance. But for bounding box label B, some background pixels may be labeled with foreground category, and pixels belonging to one of the instances may be assigned to the other incorrectly, i.e .,

In the second step, we aim to suppress the instance noise in bounding box labels, i.e ., assigning foreground pixels to correct instances. We view it as an instance segmentation task and design an instance ancillary model based on DeepLabv3+ [12] to predict pseudo labels in high resolution. But the spatial-invariant property of fully convolutional networks makes it unable to identify differ-ent instances in different spatial locations. To solve this, we change the decoder of DeepLabv3+ [12] into two branches and utilize spatial-embedding loss introduced by Neven et al. [45] for training. The spatial-embedding loss [45] assigns each pixel a spatial coordi-nate to avoid the spatial-invariant problem, and helps the model to learn to identify different instances by predicting position-relative offset vectors that point to its corresponding instance center. Fur-thermore, the instance segmentation results are non-overlapping, so the instance noise introduced by overlapping instance proposals is avoided. As shown in Fig. 4 (b), the instance ancillary model has two decoder branches, i.e ., confidence branch and instance branch. The first deconvolutional layer is followed by a batch normaliza-tion layer and a ReLU layer. The second deconvolutional layer is followed by a sigmoid layer or tanh layer for predicting confidence maps or offset maps respectively. It is well known that object boundaries are usually hard for instance ancillary model to estimate accu-rately, and they easily introduce label noise. To make instance ancillary model pay more attention to object boundaries, we add an extra 3  3 convolutional layer to explicitly obtain the edges information from semantic segmentation results. In this way, the CASA module can fully extract the information of both bounding box labels and segmentation results, and fuse them into the corre-sponding feature map of ancillary models using element-wise mul-tiplication. As shown in Fig. 5 (a) and (b), the CASA module enhances the feature of foreground objects and helps ancillary models to largely avoid incomplete segmentation. The input of CASA blocks in the semantic ancillary model is a 3-dimensional tensor representing the bounding box label called label maps, where if a pixel belongs to a bounding box with a specific category, its corresponding category channel is set to 1 and the background channel is set to 0. Notice that a pixel can belong to multiple bounding boxes. As for instance ancillary model, the intermediate segmentation results are also concatenated to the input of CASA blocks. 

3.4. Category Constraint Module & Instance Constraint Module 

Considering generated pseudo pixel-wise label should be con-sistent with the bounding box label, i.e ., the segmentation mask should be inside the bounding box, we propose a category con-straint module and instance constraint module to further improve the accuracy of pseudo labels. As shown in Fig. 4 (c), the category constraint module can con-strain category solution space for each pixel, e.g ., a pixel inside a bounding box without overlapping could only belong to the back-ground or category of the bounding box. It avoids misclassification inside the bounding box and largely removes false positives out-side the bounding box as illustrated in Fig. 5 (c). Meanwhile, the original clustering procedure [45] of instance ancillary model may lead to inaccurate segmentation results when objects are too close to each other, i.e ., two objects can be clustered as one. To solve this, as shown in Fig. 4 (d), instance constraint module can filter out the area of confidence map outside the bounding box for each object sequentially before the clustering procedure, so as to reduce instance noise. Fig. 5 (d) shows it helps the ancillary model to better generate pseudo labels for different instances. 

3.5. Self-Learning 

Samples with clear pixel-wise labels are important for ancillary models to learn label noise suppression, and the number of sam-ples should be small to keep total labeling cost low. However, insufficient training samples may result in the overfitting problem. To solve this, we utilize pseudo labels generated from bounding box labeled images by ancillary models to optimize themselves during training. It works similar to iterative label refinement mechanism [30,39]. To avoid introducing noise from pseudo labels, we adopt a strategy of ignoring noisy area, i.e ., we ignore the losses from the pixels of uncertain categories (whose max confidence is less than 0.5 in pseudo labels) and pixels of uncertain instances (whose locations are in overlapping bounding box area). As shown in Fig. 5 (e) and (f), the self-learning training approach not only solves the overfitting problem but also improves the quality of pseudo labels.

4.2. Implementation Details 

Here, we introduce the implementation details of our semantic ancillary model, instance ancillary model, and primary instance segmentation models on the both PASCAL VOC 2012 [18] and Cityscapes [14]. Notice that bounding box labels can be obtained from pixel-wise labels by finding extreme points. 

Training of semantic ancillary model. In current implementa-tion, the backbone network is based on Xception-65 [12] and ADAM optimizer [31] is utilized. It is pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset [51]. We use ADAM optimizer [31] and polynomial learn-ing rate decay ð1  iter max iter Þ0:9. The initial learning rate and batch size are set to 7 e  4 and 8. The cropping size is set to 512  512 on the PASCAL VOC 2012 [18] and 640  640 on the Cityscapes [14]. The cross entropy loss and lovász loss [8] are combined as its loss func-tion. We train the model for 60 k iterations with four GeForce GTX 1080 Ti GPUs. 

Training of instance ancillary model. In the current imple-mentation, we also use the same backbone network, optimizer, and polynomial learning rate decay as those in the semantic ancil-lary model. The spatial-embedding loss [45] is used as the loss function. We use offset value between ½ 1; 1 to represent

We first pre-train our model for 40 k iterations with a batch size of 8 on 448  448 crops that are centered around an object to avoid spending too much computation time on background patches without any instances. Then, we finetune the model for another 20 k iterations on 864  864 crops with a batch size of 2, and fix the batch normalization statistics during this stage. We follow [45] to use Lov asz-hinge loss [8] instead of cross entropy in Eq. (11) to maximize the intersection-over-union for each instance mask. The instance ancillary model is optimized with two GeForce GTX 1080 Ti GPUs. 

Training of primary instance segmentation model. Our pro-posed method can generally adopt any instance segmentation model as primary instance segmentation model. Here, we choose two widely used and recent networks, i.e ., Mask R-CNN [24] and CenterMask [38]. For the training setting, batch size, learning rate, weight decay, momentum, and the number of the iterations are set to 8, 1 e  2; 1e  4, 0.9 and 24 k, respectively. The ADAM optimizer [31] is utilized. In our implementation, Mask R-CNN [24] adopts ResNet-101 [25] as backbone network on the PASCAL VOC 2012 [18]. For the Cityscapes [14], we follow [24] and use ResNet-50 [25] as the back-bone network. ResNet-101 and ResNet-50 [25] are pre-trained on ImageNet dataset [51]. For CenterMask [38], we choose ResNeXt-152 [57] as backbone network for all datasets. ResNeXt-152 [57] is pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset [51]. The initial learning rate is 1 e  2 and drops to 1 e  3 at the 16 k-th iterations on the PASCAL VOC [18] and at the 18 k-th iterations on the Cityscapes [14]. After 24 k iterations, we finetune the model for another 2 k iterations with pixel-wise labeled images. For data augmentation, we use the same settings as those in Mask R-CNN [24]. Both Mask R-CNN [24] and CenterMask [38] are optimized on eight GeForce GTX 1080 Ti GPUs.

4.5. Extension to Semantic Segmentation 

Semantic segmentation is a similar task. The proposed method can generally apply to it by removing the instance ancillary model and replacing the primary instance segmentation model with the primary semantic segmentation model. Particularly, we use the DeepLabv3+ [12] as the primary semantic segmentation model. Generally, we can first train the semantic ancillary model and predict pseudo labels, then optimize the primary DeepLabv3+ [12] with these pseudo labels. We notice the semantic ancillary model can be seen as a DeepLabv3+ [12] with category-aware spa-tial attention module and category constraint module, so we let the primary DeepLabv3+ [12] share the parameters with semantic ancillary model and optimize them jointly in an end-to-end way.

During the training of each epoch, we first train the semantic ancillary model and DeepLabv3+ [12] with ground truth pixel-wise labels, then we train the primary DeepLabv3+ [12] with pseudo pixel-wise labels produced by the semantic ancillary model. Notice that we ignore losses from pixels of uncertain cate-gories (whose max confidence values are less than 0.5 in pseudo labels). Since the primary DeepLabv3+ [12] shares the parameters with the semantic ancillary model, the semantic ancillary model is also updated. And in the next epoch, the updated semantic ancil-lary model can predict more accurate pseudo pixel-wise labels to further improve the primary DeepLabv3+ [12]. In this way, the semantic ancillary model is able to predict more and more accurate pseudo labels and the primary DeepLabv3+ [12] can achieve com-petitive results after training. Following [44,12], we evaluate the performance using mIoU 

metric on the PASCAL VOC 2012 [18]. As shown in Table 8, when the labeling cost is the same, our method performs better than Ibrahim et al. [29]. Notice that our model can be trained in an end-to-end way, while Ibrahim et al. [29] cannot. As shown in Table 8 and Fig. 8, we can see that our method even outperforms the fully supervised setting both quantitatively and qualitatively with only 10% pixel-wise labels, which means the proposed method can achieve 2.2 better mIoU with more than 75% labeling cost reduction. The reason may be that the fully supervised method is sensitive to label noise coming from its augmented training set [22]. 

5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we present a pipeline of hybrid supervision that can fully utilize pixel-wise labels and bounding box labels, it well balances performance and labeling cost for instance segmentation. Specifically, we design a semantic ancillary model and an instance ancillary model to suppress the background noise and instance noise in bounding box labels. Moreover, we exploit category-aware spatial attention module, category constraint module, instance constraint module to fully utilize bounding box labels, so as to produce high-quality pseudo instance-level pixel-wise labels. And we propose a self-learning training approach to avoid the overfitting problem. Experimental results on the PASCAL VOC 2012 [18] and the Cityscapes [14] show that our method not only outperforms weakly and semi-supervised methods [30,39,26,4,7] by a large margin, but also achieves better performance than some former state-of-the-art fully supervised methods [23,15,34,5,43,3,35] with much less labeling cost.Our pipeline is able to learn the accurate shapes of objects from original PASCAL VOC 2012 [18], suppress the noise, and even generate pseudo labels better than noisy ground truth in [22], as shown in Fig. 9. We also extend our method to the semantic segmentation task, and achieve better performance than full supervision on the PAS-CAL VOC 2012 [18] with more than 75% labeling cost reduction. Our work mainly focuses on designing ancillary models to pro-duce accurate pseudo pixel-wise labels for instance/semantic seg-mentation tasks and has not designed the primary segmentation model. In the future, it is worth investigating how to design a pri-mary segmentation model that is robust to noise in labels, and fur-ther extend our work to other pixel-wise prediction tasks e.g ., panoptic segmentation [33,32].
