Abstract 

Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation aims to classify pixels without being restricted to a fixed set of categories. Although Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) demonstrates remarkable potential for open-vocabulary recognition, its segmentation capability is impaired by noisy inter-class patch interactions. To this end, this work proposes a unified training-free object-centric framework, named CorrCLIPv2, from three complementary aspects of patch correlation, feature representation, and output regularization. Specifically, for patch correlation, we leverage class-agnostic mask genera-tors to explicitly disentangle inter-class interactions. Moreover, we utilize spatially-coherent features from self-supervised models to compute attention scores to mitigate mask inaccuracies. Regard-ing feature representation, we devise a refinement mechanism to simultaneously strengthen spatial details and semantic representation. Within the output space, we unify predictions via voting in each mask to enhance spatial consistency. To mitigate contextual bias and enhance prediction accu-racy of objects, we further extend the framework by incorporating a cross-image object retrieval paradigm, which incurs minimal computational overhead. We build a memory bank of CorrCLIP-enhanced features from unlabeled images and implement a regime of multi-CLIP ensemble. By aggregating semantically consistent object features across diverse contexts retrieved from the mem-ory bank, CorrCLIPv2 mitigates context-induced interference and obtains more robust predictions. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach establishes a new state-of-the-art on 10 stan-dard benchmarks. Remarkably, it even surpasses fully supervised open-vocabulary methods across 22 multi-domain datasets. Code is available at: https://github.com/zdk258/CorrCLIP. 

Keywords: Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation; Vision foundation model; Correlation reconstruction; Unlabeled images

Critically, these methods do not explicitly identify which types of patch correlations degrade segmentation perfor-mance, thereby limiting their potential for further improvement. To analyze this issue in depth, we conduct a breakdown analysis. For the segmentation task, ideal patch correlations in CLIP should maintain feature discriminability across classes and aggre-gate semantic information for semantic alignment. We categorize correlations into intra-class and inter-class types. Intuitively, intra-class correla-tions inherently satisfy both objectives. However, the impact of inter-class correlations presents a complex ambiguity. On one hand, aggregating information from distinct categories may intro-duce noise that compromises discriminability. On the other hand, it may provide useful contex-tual cues that facilitate alignment. To resolve this ambiguity, we conduct a controlled investigation starting from a restricted setting of pure intra-class correlations. The empirical results are shown in Fig. 1, suggesting a substantial improvement in CLIP’s segmentation performance, which confirms 2the efficacy of intra-class aggregation. Addition-ally, intra-object correlations achieve results com-petitive with intra-class correlations. In contrast, we observe consistent degradation as inter-class correlations are reintroduced. Notably, even the most semantically similar inter-class patches are detrimental. These results suggest that, in the con-text of dense prediction, inter-class correlations primarily act as noise rather than providing useful contextual information. Motivated by the aforementioned observation, we contend that explicitly regulating the interac-tion scope is imperative. To this end, we propose Scope Reconstruction that utilizes object masks generated by class-agnostic mask generators (Kir-illov et al. 2023; Ravi et al. 2024; Qi et al. 2023) to strictly confine patch interactions within objects, thereby minimizing inter-class correla-tions. Furthermore, to avoid potential multi-class ambiguities in masks, we introduce Value Recon-struction to refine similarity measures via coher-ent features of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) VFMs (Oquab et al. 2023; Sim´ eoni et al. 2025; Heinrich et al. 2025), further suppressing the weight of irrelevant correlations. Beyond optimiz-ing patch correlations, we present Feature Refine-ment, which introduces a spatial branch to recover spatial details from lower layers and a semantic branch utilizing mask class tokens to consolidate regional semantics. Finally, in the output space, we introduce Map Correction to unify predictions via voting in each mask, ensuring spatial con-sistency. These mechanisms effectively establish a robust object-centric framework, which recon-structs patch correlations, refines feature represen-tations, and unifies prediction outputs within each object instance. While this framework improves object classification accuracy, inherent contextual bias may still cause object representations to devi-ate from their intrinsic semantics, leading to mis-classification. This issue stems from the necessity of global contextual interaction in the preceding ViT layers of CLIP. Unlike the final layer where we can strictly confine the scope, early layers must aggregate information globally to form seman-tic concepts. Consequently, an object’s embedding may be susceptible to interference from its sur-rounding context (e.g., a “cat” in a dark cage may be embedded closer to “fence”). In such cases, relying solely on information from a single input image is insufficient to correct this semantic drift. To this end, we extend our framework to a cross-image object retrieval paradigm, Cor-rCLIPv2, which incurs only marginal inference overhead. The core insight is that the feature rep-resentation of a single object instance may fluctu-ate due to contextual bias. In contrast, a semantic prototype distilled from multiple instances of the same class across diverse environments is more robust and reliable. Following this insight, we first construct an offline memory bank populated with CorrCLIP-enhanced features from a large collec-tion of unlabeled data. A key challenge here is accurately linking the query object to semanti-cally consistent support objects. To address this challenge, we introduce VFM-Indexed Semantic Retrieval (VISR). Unlike CLIP, SSL-based VFMs produce visually discriminative representations at the fine-grained object level, where intra-class fea-tures maintain high affinity, whereas inter-class features are well-separated. By utilizing VFM features as a stable anchor to retrieve a visual support set and aggregating their corresponding CorrCLIP features, VISR effectively marginal-izes out the context-induced variance, stabilizing predictions under complex backgrounds. Moreover, we exploit the decoupled design of our retrieval mechanism to enable a scal-able approach for open-ended model integration, proposing a Decoupled Multi-CLIP Ensemble strategy. Unlike traditional ensembles that incur linear computational costs with each added model, our framework decouples the costly feature encod-ing of different CLIP variants, which is performed offline, from the online inference stage. Conse-quently, we can effectively fuse complementary knowledge dimensions from multiple pre-trained models with negligible marginal inference over-head, improving robustness without compromis-ing efficiency. Our contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We conduct a breakdown analysis revealing that inter-class correlations predominantly manifest as destructive noise rather than constructive context in CLIP-based segmentation.

• We index a large-scale memory bank using SSL-based VFMs to retrieve semantically consistent objects, and develop a decoupled multi-CLIP ensemble strategy that integrates diverse pre-trained models efficiently.

It sets a new state-of-the-art on 10 standard benchmarks as shown in Fig. 2 and outperforms fully supervised open-vocabulary counterparts on the multi-domain MESS bench-mark comprising 22 datasets. This work extends our preliminary study, CorrCLIP (Zhang et al. 2025), which has been accepted as an oral paper at ICCV’25. We evolve CorrCLIP into a cross-image paradigm with mini-mal inference overhead. The major improvements include: (1) a retrieval-augmented framework that leverages unlabeled images to rectify contextual bias; (2) the integration of SSL-based VFMs to construct a robust index, enabling the effective retrieval of semantically consistent support fea-tures; (3) a decoupled ensemble strategy designed to efficiently leverage diverse pre-trained CLIP models; (4) a substantial expansion of experi-ments on two challenging large-vocabulary bench-marks (ADE20K-847 (Zhou et al. 2017), Pascal Context-459 (Mottaghi et al. 2014)) and the MESS benchmark (Blumenstiel et al. 2023) with 22 multi-domain datasets, ensuring a more rigor-ous assessment. 

# 2 Related Work 

## 2.1 Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation 

Diverging from traditional semantic segmenta-tion (Chen et al. 2017; Cheng et al. 2022; Tang et al. 2025) methods that operate within a closed set of predefined labels, OVSS aims to seg-ment images based on an open set of textual descriptions. The rapid evolution of this field is largely attributed to the emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Generally, existing OVSS methods can be categorized into training-based and training-free approaches. Training-based approaches require additional fine-tuning using various forms of supervision, including mask annotations (Cho et al. 2024; Xu et al. 2023b; Liang et al. 2023; Li et al. 2025a), image-level labels (Wysocza´ nska et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2025b), or image-text pairs (Barsellotti et al. 2025; Jose et al. 2025). While these methods often yield supe-rior performance on specific benchmarks, they tend to compromise the inherent generalization ability of VLMs as demonstrated in (Sun et al. 2024). Conversely, training-free methods circum-vent the need for further training, thereby preserv-ing the original zero-shot capabilities of VLMs. Within the training-free landscape, a promi-nent research direction involves changing the attention mechanisms of CLIP. Several works (Zhou et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2024; Li et al. 2023; Lan et al. 2024a; Hajimiri et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025b) modify the final layer’s attention, transitioning from a query-key interaction to a self-self mechanism to encourage patches to focus more on themselves. Others (Liu et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2025; Bai et al. 2025; Sun et al. 2025) observe that lower layers often exhibit higher spa-tial coherence, thereby utilizing these layers to refine the noisy attention maps of the final layer. Despite these innovations, performance remains bottlenecked by CLIP’s incoherent features stem-ming from its fundamental training objective. To address this, recent studies (Lan et al. 2024b; Kim et al. 2025a; Sun et al. 2025; Shi et al. 2025; Barsellotti et al. 2024; Kang and Cho 2024) have integrated auxiliary foundation models to bol-ster CLIP’s segmentation capabilities. However, existing methods fail to explicitly regulate patch correlations. In this work, we introduce correlation reconstruction to explicitly reduce inter-class cor-relations, thereby significantly improving CLIP’s segmentation performance. 

## 2.2 Vision-Language Pre-training 

Vision-language pre-training (VLP) has emerged as a dominant paradigm for learning joint cross-modal representations. By harnessing weakly 4supervised learning on vast collections of image-text pairs, VLP enables models to capture intrin-sic semantic associations between visual con-tent and linguistic descriptions. While pioneer-ing studies demonstrated the potential of this approach, their capabilities were inevitably lim-ited by the scarcity of training data. A paradigm shift occurred with the advent of large-scale web-crawled datasets, enabling recent frameworks (Radford et al. 2021; Jia et al. 2021) to learn sig-nificantly more robust and transferable represen-tations. Among these, CLIP (Radford et al. 2021) has established itself as the de facto standard. By leveraging contrastive learning to align visual and textual feature spaces, CLIP achieves remark-able zero-shot generalization. Building upon this foundation, a wave of subsequent research (Cherti et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2024; Bolya et al. 2025; Fang et al. 2023) has further advanced the field by refining data curation strategies and optimizing training protocols. While CLIP lays the founda-tion for open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, its global image-text alignment objective is not inherently compatible with the pixel-level granu-larity required for segmentation, thereby requiring specific adaptations to bridge this gap. 

## 2.3 Vision Foundation Models 

The primary objective of VFMs is to learn uni-versal visual representations from large-scale data. Many VFMs build on self-supervised learning, which leverages data without human annotations. Early methods such as MAE (He et al. 2022) rely on reconstructive objectives that capture struc-tural cues, while DINO (Caron et al. 2021) intro-duces self-distillation to obtain stronger semantic features. This line of work is significantly advanced by DINOv2 (Oquab et al. 2023), whose large-scale curated training and improved optimiza-tion produce representations that capture intrin-sic, high-level semantics. The recently developed DINOv3 (Sim´ eoni et al. 2025) further strengthens this paradigm through enhanced data scaling and training stability, yielding consistently stronger generalization across recognition and dense pre-diction tasks. Building on these advances, RADIO (Heinrich et al. 2025) distills knowledge from strong teacher models such as DINOv2 and CLIP, allowing it to inherit rich semantic representa-tions and local details. Benefiting from the global semantic coherence and local fine-grained details of SSL-based VFMs, we employ their features to suppress irrelevant correlations and construct indices to retrieve semantically consistent support features to stabilize predictions. 

## 2.4 Class-Agnostic Mask Generator 

Semantic segmentation jointly learns category recognition and pixel-level delineation, requiring models to decide both what and where. This reliance on fixed label spaces limits generaliza-tion to open-world scenarios. Class-agnostic mask generators, by contrast, focus solely on mask pre-diction without semantic classification, making them easier to train and inherently more robust to unseen categories. Large-scale vision models have recently advanced this paradigm. The Segment Anything Model (SAM) (Kirillov et al. 2023; Ravi et al. 2024) learns from a massive image–mask corpus and employs a promptable architecture to produce high-quality, zero-shot masks, establish-ing a general foundation for open-world segmen-tation. EntitySeg (Qi et al. 2023, 2022) similarly targets the segmentation of generic entities rather than predefined classes, using strong visual priors to achieve class-agnostic object extraction. Based on these advanced open-world mask generators, we construct a training-free object-centric OVSS framework and further extend it to a cross-image object retrieval paradigm. 

## 2.5 Memory Bank for OVSS 

Memory bank has recently emerged as a promis-ing paradigm to enhance OVSS. However, exist-ing methods predominantly construct memory banks heavily anchored to textual guidance. For instance, FreeDA (Barsellotti et al. 2024) relies on predefined categories from COCO Captions (Chen et al. 2015), ReME (Xuan et al. 2025) utilizes Large Language Models to generate textual labels, and PGSeg (Zhang et al. 2023a) depends on mas-sive image-text pair datasets. A critical limitation of these approaches is that they are inherently bounded by a fixed set of text vocabularies dur-ing construction. This contradicts the objective of OVSS. Text-anchored memory banks force con-tinuous visual features into predefined semantic bins, inevitably overfitting to the taxonomy and granularity of the construction dataset. In con-trast, our method decouples the memory bank 5construction from language and naturally accom-modates the open-ended nature of OVSS. This language-free design elegantly circumvents vocab-ulary overfitting, unleashing the true potential of memory banks for infinite-category scenarios.

Consequently, the feature representa-tion of a local object is inevitably entangled with its surrounding context. We hypothesize that while indi-vidual object representations are susceptible to specific contextual bias, the semantic prototype distilled from multiple instances of the same cate-gory is robust. Theoretically, aggregating features from diverse contexts can marginalize out context-induced variance, thereby stabilizing the semantic representation and prediction. We construct an ideal feature bank using ground-truth class masks. Specifically, we extract Cor-rCLIP features for all objects in a dataset and group them by category. During inference, for each target object, we randomly sample a support set from the corresponding category bank and aver-age them with the target feature. As illustrated in Fig. 4, this oracle aggregation yields a substantial performance improvement over the single-image baseline. This result empirically confirms that accessing a semantically consistent support set can effectively mitigate feature context-induced inter-ference. Motivated by this, CorrCLIPv2 aims to approximate this oracle behavior in an unsuper-vised, open-vocabulary setting via retrieval.

## 4.1 Datasets and Evaluation Metrics 

We conduct comprehensive evaluations on ten standard benchmarks and a massive multi-domain benchmark. 

Standard Benchmarks. We utilize the val-idation sets of five datasets, which constitute ten specific benchmarks. Pascal VOC (Ever-ingham et al. 2010) (1,449 images) is evalu-ated on V20 (20 classes) and V21 (w/ back-ground). Pascal Context (Mottaghi et al. 2014) (5,104 images) supports three benchmarks: PC59 (59 classes), PC60 (w/ background), and fine-grained PC459 (459 classes). COCO (Caesar et al. 2018) (5,000 images) is divided into COCO-Stuff (171 classes) and COCO-Object (81 classes). 

ADE20K (Zhou et al. 2017) (2,000 images) is evaluated on the standard A150 (150 classes) and the large-vocabulary A847 (847 classes). 

Cityscapes (Cordts et al. 2016) (500 images) focuses on urban scenes with 19 classes. 

MESS Benchmark. To verify open-vocabulary robustness across distinct distributions, we employ the Multi-Domain Evaluation of Semantic Segmentation (MESS) benchmark (Blumenstiel et al. 2023). It com-prises 22 datasets spanning five diverse domains: General, Earth Monitoring, Medical Sciences, Engineering, and Agriculture & Biology. 

Memory Bank Source. We construct our offline feature memory bank using the COCO training set (Caesar et al. 2018), which contains approximately 118k images. Crucially, we utilize only the raw images to extract visual features, without accessing any ground-truth annotations or semantic labels. 11 Following standard protocols, we report the mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) for all experiments. 

## 4.2 Implementation Details 

We instantiate our framework with two vari-ants based on model capacity: CorrCLIPv2-B and CorrCLIPv2-L, which utilize the CLIP-ViT-B/16 and CLIP-ViT-L/14 architectures, respectively. Unless otherwise specified, we employ the weights from MetaCLIP (Xu et al. 2024) as the primary backbone. For the Decoupled Multi-CLIP Ensem-ble, we additionally leverage the DFN (Fang et al. 2023) as the auxiliary backbone to incorporate diverse pre-training priors. For the class-agnostic mask generator, we employ EntitySeg (Qi et al. 2023) implemented with the Mask2Former (Cheng et al. 2022) architecture and a Hornet-L (Rao et al. 2022) backbone. For VFM, we utilize C-RADIOv3 (Heinrich et al. 2025) with a ViT-L/16 backbone. The VFM-Indexed Semantic Retrieval is implemented using the FAISS-GPU library (John-son et al. 2019). To balance efficiency and accuracy on large-scale databases, we utilize the IVFPQ index. During inference, we adopt a sliding win-dow strategy to handle high-resolution inputs while preserving local details. The temperature coefficient τ for the similarity calculation is set to 0.25. The balancing hyperparameter θ is set to 0.5.

On out-of-distribution datasets with large vocabu-laries (A150 and A847), CorrCLIPv2 even out-performs fully supervised methods like SAN and CLIPSelf. Considering the trade-off between accuracy and computational overhead, we adopt a scale factor of 2 as the default setting.

4.5.2 Impact of Different Vision Foundation Models

4.5.4 Impact of Different Mask Generators

In contrast, SAM, which relies on grid-point prompting, inevitably generates redun-dant and overlapping masks, introducing ambigu-ity. These results sug-gest that CorrCLIPv2 effectively capitalizes on high-quality proposals, and its performance will naturally scale with future advancements in class-agnostic segmentation.

We investigate how the quality of the seman-tic features stored in the memory bank affects the final performance. ## 4.6 Limitations and Future Work 

In open-vocabulary settings, the presence of numerous irrelevant categories inevitably intro-duces noise and semantic ambiguity. We conduct an oracle prompt experiment where we restrict text queries exclusively to categories actually present in the image. As shown in Table 12, the significant performance gap between this ideal scenario and standard inference highlights the model’s susceptibility to semantic distractors from a massive open-ended vocabulary. Therefore, a critical avenue for future work is the develop-ment of robust image-level category pre-filtering strategies. Specifically, prior to pixel-level seg-mentation, we can employ auxiliary methods to determine which categories are likely present in the scene. This could be achieved by leverag-ing lightweight multi-label classification networks, calculating global image-text similarity scores, or prompting large vision-language models to gener-ate a set of candidate objects. By implementing such a recognize-then-segment paradigm, we can effectively prune irrelevant text queries, mitigate semantic distractors, and further narrow the gap toward oracle performance. 

# 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we reveal that inter-class patch cor-relations in CLIP primarily manifest as noise, hin-dering segmentation performance. To address this, we propose a unified, training-free object-centric framework, named CorrCLIPv2. By explicitly dis-entangling inter-class interactions, strengthening feature representations, and unifying intra-mask predictions, our approach significantly enhances CLIP’s segmentation capabilities. Furthermore, to mitigate contextual bias, we evolve our framework into a cross-image paradigm. We introduce VFM-indexed semantic retrieval to accurately access semantically consistent support features from a large-scale memory bank of unlabeled data. This retrieval mechanism, combined with our decou-pled multi-CLIP ensemble strategy, significantly enhances prediction accuracy with minimal infer-ence overhead. Extensive experiments demon-strate that CorrCLIPv2 sets a new state-of-the-art across ten standard benchmarks and notably out-performs fully supervised open-vocabulary coun-terparts on the MESS benchmark. We believe this work provides a scalable, robust foundation for open-vocabulary semantic segmentation.
