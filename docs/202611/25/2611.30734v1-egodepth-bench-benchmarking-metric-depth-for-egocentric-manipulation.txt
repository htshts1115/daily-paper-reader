# EgoDepth-Bench: Benchmarking Metric Depth for Egocentric Manipulation 

Yuan Gao *, Dongchen Wei †, Xinyang Lin, Shuhua Cao, Xueliang Guo, Zhuohan Li, Yihao Wang 

GenRobot 

https://egodepth-bench.github.io 

Abstract — We introduce EgoDepth-Bench, comprising 941,233 synchronized samples collected across 37 distinct physical environments. Each sample combines six calibrated RGB views with metric depth in the native fisheye view, reconstructed from five active-infrared stereo modules. A calibrated ball-bar confirms millimeter-level geometric accuracy, with a median error of 1.27 mm on a known sphere spacing. Environment-held-out splits enable evaluation of generalization to unseen environments. Across monocular, stereo, and multi-view models, the benchmark measures both depth accuracy and prediction coverage, including on hands and objects. Fine-tuning MapAnything on EgoDepth-Bench reduces metric AbsRel from 0.708 to 0.091 and scale-aligned AbsRel from 0.327 to 0.072 on unseen environments. These results demonstrate that EgoDepth-Bench provides effective supervision and enables rigorous evaluation of metric and relative depth estimation in egocentric manipulation. The project page provides the evaluation code and interactive RGB-depth sequences. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Egocentric demonstrations provide a rich source of train-ing data for robot manipulation [1]. Recovering the metric geometry of hands, manipulated objects, and the surrounding workspace can further support learning from these demon-strations [2], [3]. However, depth estimation in egocentric manipulation is particularly challenging: head motion is cou-pled with independent hand and object motion, occlusions change continuously, and fisheye cameras with a wide field of view introduce strong viewing-angle variation. Evaluating depth in this setting therefore requires metric ground truth on dynamic surfaces, together with measurements that explicitly characterize the interacting hands and objects. Existing datasets address only subsets of these require-ments. HOI4D captures real egocentric manipulation using perspective RGB-D cameras [4], while WideDepth evaluates fisheye depth using imagery rendered from static laser-scanned scenes [5]. Aria Digital Twin (ADT) provides real egocentric interaction and native-fisheye depth rendered from registered scene and object models, but is limited to two physical environments [6]. Thus, existing resources do not jointly provide native-fisheye metric depth for real dynamic manipulation, broad coverage across physical environments, held-out-location evaluation, and interaction-specific depth analysis. We introduce EgoDepth-Bench, a metric depth dataset and benchmark of real egocentric manipulation. A head-mounted camera rig records 941,233 synchronized samples

Metric Z-depth for the target fisheye camera is re-constructed from five active-infrared stereo modules us-ing learned stereo estimation followed by calibrated multi-view fusion in the target camera’s native fisheye image coordinates. Training on EgoDepth-Bench substantially improves both metric and relative depth estimation at held-out sites. With rig geometry fixed, fine-tuning MapAnything [7] reduces test AbsRel from 0.708 to 0.091, with substantial gains remaining after per-frame scale alignment. Our contributions are: 1) A real egocentric manipulation dataset spanning 37 distinct physical environments, with six synchronized RGB views, native-fisheye metric Z-depth, and inde-pendent ball-bar validation of reconstructed geometry. 2) A depth benchmark that jointly evaluates accuracy and prediction coverage, including dedicated measurements on hands and manipulated objects and diagnostics over viewing geometry and motion. 3) A systematic evaluation of public monocular, stereo, and multi-view models, together with controlled stud-ies of input views, rig geometry, and cross-environment generalization and adaptation. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Depth Benchmarks 

NYUv2, KITTI Depth, and DIODE evaluate depth in indoor RGB-D scenes, driving, and indoor–outdoor imagery, respectively [8]–[10]. Bonn RGB-D Dynamic includes real moving scenes and object manipulation, while Dynamic Replica provides synthetic stereo sequences with moving people and animals [11], [12]. Camera geometry and capture viewpoint motivate more targeted benchmarks. ScanNet++ registers fisheye pho-tographs to laser-scanned geometry [13]. WideDepth ren-ders its indoor fisheye benchmark from scans and provides separate real outdoor training data with LiDAR depth [5]. AerialMetric combines targeted evaluation of UAV imaging conditions with model adaptation [14]. EgoDepth-Bench examines real head-worn manipulation through local depth diagnostics and adaptation across physical sites. 

B. Egocentric Interaction Geometry 

HOI4D uses head-mounted RGB-D sensors [4]. ADT records one RGB and two monochrome views and renders aligned fisheye depth from registered scene and object mod-els [6]. HOT3D and SHOW3D provide hand and object poses and meshes for interaction geometry [15], [16]. The Ego-1K research release offers 12 rectified RGB views and evaluates stereo consistency [17]. EgoLive recon-structs depth from rectified RGB stereo pairs and validates it against physical targets [18]; Xperience-10M provides stereo-derived depth for egocentric interaction [19]. These resources already support depth evaluation: EgoM2P evaluates video depth on HOI4D with sequence-level scale and shift alignment [20], and UniK3D reports zero-shot results on ADT [21]. EgoDepth-Bench reconstructs native fisheye ground-truth depth from dedicated active-IR pairs, retaining six RGB views as model inputs and separat-ing training and evaluation across physical sites (Table I). 

C. Depth Models and Geometric Inputs 

Depth Anything v2 and Marigold predict relative depth [22], [23]; Metric3D v2 and UniDepth v2 estimate metric depth [24], [25]. Depth Any Camera and UniK3D support a broader range of camera projections [21], [26]. Foun-dationStereo uses rectified image pairs [27], while VGGT and MapAnything recover geometry from multiple images; MapAnything also accepts supplied calibration [7], [28]. Our evaluation groups models by these input and scale assumptions, separating metric accuracy from accuracy after alignment to ground truth. III. T HE EGO DEPTH -B ENCH DATASET 

EgoDepth-Bench pairs six synchronized RGB views of head-worn interaction with metric ground-truth Z-depth pixel-aligned with camera 2’s fisheye RGB image, supporting monocular, stereo, and multi-view comparisons on the same observations. 

A. Data Collection and Coverage 

Five participants performed freely chosen activities such as folding clothes, tidying tables, cleaning, and mopping, without prescribed head or hand movements. Their 1,155 recordings span 37 physical sites and 8.72 hours. Table II summarizes the environments and data partitions. The head-mounted rig combines six global-shutter RGB cameras with five Gemini 336L active-infrared (IR) stereo modules whose views overlap in the workspace (Fig. 2). RGB is recorded at 1600 × 1296 . Camera 2 has a calibrated RGB field of view of approximately 163 ◦ horizontally and 

136 ◦ vertically, exceeding 200 ◦ diagonally. Each IR module records 848 × 480 stereo pairs with a nominal 95 mm baseline. A shared hardware trigger synchronizes RGB and IR acquisition at 30 Hz. The dataset contains 941,233 synchronized timestamps, each with six RGB images and one camera-2 ground-truth depth map, totaling 5,647,398 RGB images. RGB images and depth maps are provided at 512 × 416 and approximately 30 Hz, accompanied by calibration at this resolution and original timestamps.

EgoDepth-Bench benchmarks metric depth during real egocentric manipulation using six synchronized RGB views and native fisheye ground truth, with ball-bar validation of recovered dimensions. Shared observations and separate training and test sites support model comparisons and adap-tation. Hand and object diagnostics show that low local error can coexist with incomplete prediction coverage. The current protocol scores individual frames.
