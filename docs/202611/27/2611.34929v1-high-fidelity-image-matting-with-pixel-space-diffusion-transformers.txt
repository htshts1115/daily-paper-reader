# Abstract 

Diffusion models have shown strong potential for image matting by transferring generative priors learned from large-scale pretraining. However, existing approaches largely rely on latent diffusion models, whose compressed representations and VAE-based reconstruction introduce a mismatch with the high-frequency precision required for matting. We present PixelMatte, a framework that adapts pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers for high-fidelity image matting. We show that strong image-conditioned matting capabilities can be acquired even from a pure text-to-image model without paired image-to-image pretraining. Our approach reuses the backbone’s native patch embedding to jointly process the input image, trimap, and noisy alpha matte as spatially aligned tokens, enabling direct pixel-space prediction without additional condition encoders or adapters. With pixel-accurate synthetic super-vision and real-image fine-tuning, PixelMatte predicts al-pha mattes in a single forward pass. Two lightweight cal-ibration modules, LayerShift and High-Pass Refiner, fur-ther improve regional opacity accuracy and boundary fi-delity. Experiments across four benchmarks and two dis-tinct pixel diffusion backbones demonstrate substantial im-provements over existing methods, reducing SAD and gradi-ent errors by 24–44% relative to RenderMatte on P3M-500-NP and AM-2K, while achieving competitive performance on AIM-500 and RenderMatte-2K. These results demon-strate the effectiveness of pixel-space generative priors for precision-sensitive image matting. Project page: https: //white-yc.github.io/pixelmatte/ .

# 1. Introduction 

Image matting estimates the per-pixel alpha matte of an arbitrary foreground object from the observed image [17]. Unlike traditional image segmentation, which assigns dis-1arXiv:2611.34929v1 [cs.CV] 25 Nov 2026 crete labels, image matting is an inherently ill-posed inverse problem due to the ambiguity in recovering alpha values from a composite image [9, 33, 41]. Trimap-guided mat-ting addresses this ambiguity by introducing an auxiliary trimap that partitions the image into definite foreground, definite background, and an unknown region requiring ex-act alpha estimation. The trimaps may be produced us-ing segmentation models [12, 18, 50], providing semantic and spatial guidance for image matting models to focus on recovering fine-grained details within the unknown re-gion. The main challenge lies in intricate structures such as hair, fur, foliage, and transparent boundaries, where accu-rate alpha estimation requires pixel-level precision. Tradi-tionally, image matting has been formulated as a supervised regression problem [5, 8, 21, 25, 27, 29, 31, 39, 44, 46]. More recently, generative approaches have emerged that adapt pretrained diffusion models for alpha matte predic-tion [6, 9, 33, 41, 45, 47]. These methods leverage rich generative priors acquired through large-scale pretraining, which can help mitigate the effects of imperfect alpha an-notations and enable generalization to diverse visual con-tent beyond the limited distributions of existing matting datasets. However, most existing generative approaches rely on latent diffusion models [13, 34], creating an inherent mis-match with the pixel-level precision required for image mat-ting. Matting is a high-frequency task, yet the network of a latent model operates on a spatially compressed rep-resentation in which hair-thin structures and opacity tran-sitions are not represented explicitly [4, 48, 49]. It pre-dicts a compressed code and is supervised at the pixel level only through a frozen decoder trained for natural im-ages [20, 42, 43, 51]. Fine details are therefore recovered in-directly, relying on the strength of the generative prior rather than on direct modeling of high-frequency alpha structure. Recent advances in pixel-space diffusion models have made high-quality image generation directly in pixel space increasingly practical, enabled by improved prediction ob-jectives, architectures, and training strategies [2, 19, 23, 24, 32, 48]. In contrast to latent diffusion models, these ap-proaches bypass VAE-based compression and reconstruc-tion, preserving direct access to high-frequency image in-formation that is only implicitly encoded in latent represen-tations. This enables diffusion transformers to learn and optimize fine-grained visual structures under direct pixel-space supervision, making them particularly promising for image matting, where precise recovery of high-frequency details is essential. In this paper, we present PixelMatte , a simple yet ef-fective framework for high-fidelity matting built upon pre-trained pixel-space diffusion transformers. We demonstrate that even a pure text-to-image model without paired image-to-image pretraining can acquire strong image-conditioned matting capabilities through supervised fine-tuning. Specif-ically, we directly embed the input image, trimap, and noisy alpha matte using the backbone’s native patch embedding, enabling spatially aligned conditioning without additional encoders or adapters. We adapt the pretrained models us-ing the pixel-accurate synthetic training data of Render-Matte [33], followed by mixed training with real images. We further introduce two lightweight calibration modules, 

LayerShift and High-Pass Refiner , to improve opacity ac-curacy and boundary fidelity, respectively. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce PixelMatte , a framework that adapts pre-trained pixel-space diffusion transformers to image mat-ting by reusing the backbone’s native patch embedding for spatially aligned image and trimap conditioning. It needs no VAE, condition encoder or adapter, predicts the matte in a single step, and works even for a pure text-to-image backbone (PRXPixel [32]) without paired image-to-image pretraining. • We propose two lightweight calibration modules: Layer-Shift , learned per-layer shifts that adjust regional opacity levels inside the frozen backbone, and the High-Pass Re-finer , a small high-pass-constrained residual network that sharpens boundary transitions. • With two pixel diffusion backbones, PixelMatte achieves state-of-the-art results on P3M-500-NP and AM-2K, re-ducing the SAD of RenderMatte [33] from 6.2 to 4.3 and from 6.5 to 4.4, and competitive results on AIM-500 and RenderMatte-2K. 

# 2. Related Work 

Trimap-guided matting. Deep Image Matting [44] es-tablished a learning-based framework for trimap-guided al-pha estimation and introduced the Composition-1k bench-mark. Subsequent methods improved alpha regression through learned feature indexing [29], guided contextual attention [21], attention-based representations [31], and pretrained vision transformers with detail-capture mod-ules [46]. Real-image datasets, including P3M-10k [14], AM-2K [16], and AIM-500 [15], have further advanced the evaluation of matting in diverse natural scenes. These approaches primarily learn direct alpha regression from matting-specific supervision, with high-frequency details recovered through task-specific architectures and image fea-tures. In contrast, PixelMatte explores transferring pre-trained pixel-space generative priors to fine-grained alpha prediction. 

Generative priors for image matting. The success of pretrained diffusion models has motivated their adaptation to dense visual prediction tasks [7, 11, 30, 35, 43]. DiffMatte [9] instead introduces it-erative pixel-level diffusion refinement using a lightweight decoder, and SDMatte [10] adapts Stable Diffusion for visual-prompt-guided interactive matting. More recently, RenderMatte [33] fine-tunes FLUX.1 Kontext [13], an image-editing model pretrained on paired image-to-image data, for trimap-guided matting, combining pixel-accurate synthetic supervision with alpha-edge losses and group-relative alignment. Pixel-space diffusion transformers. Recent advances in pixel-space diffusion have demonstrated high-quality image generation without pretrained VAEs [3, 40]. JiT [19] re-visits direct pixel prediction with large-patch transformers, while PixelDiT [48] combines patch-level generation with pixel-level detail modeling. PRXPixel [32] and HiDream-O1-Image [2] extend pixel-space diffusion to large-scale image generation. Beyond generation, PixRestore [38] develops a pixel-space diffusion transformer trained from scratch for image restoration, incorporating feature-based conditioning for detail recovery. PixelControl [22] ex-plores pixel-space controllable generation with additional structure-aware condition injection to improve fine-grained spatial fidelity. In contrast to these task-specific architec-tures, PixelMatte directly repurposes pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers for image-conditioned alpha estima-tion. By reusing their native patch embedding for spa-tially aligned image and trimap conditioning, our frame-work avoids additional conditioning encoders or adapters while enabling direct pixel-space matting. 

# 3. Method 

Latent diffusion matting introduces a representation mis-match between compressed latent features and the high-frequency precision required for image matting (Fig. 2a). In contrast, PixelMatte adapts pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers to predict and supervise alpha mattes directly in pixel space, without VAE-based reconstruction (Fig. 2b). We reuse the backbone’s native patch embedding to jointly process the input image, trimap, and noisy alpha matte as spatially aligned tokens (Sec. 3.1). The model is fine-tuned in two stages and performs alpha estimation in a single for-ward pass (Sec. 3.2). Finally, LayerShift (Sec. 3.3) and the High-Pass Refiner (Sec. 3.4) further calibrate regional opac-ity levels and boundary transitions, respectively. 

3.1. Pixel-Space Matting with Aligned Conditioning 

Given an image I and a trimap T ∈ { 0, 0.5, 1}H×W whose unknown region is U = {p : T (p) = 0 .5}, the goal is to predict the alpha matte α ∈ [0 , 1] H×W . We denote by 

x0 the matte 2α − 1 replicated to three channels and by 3c = ( I, ¯T ) the condition, where ¯T is the trimap 2T − 1

replicated to three channels. We adopt two pretrained pixel-space rectified-flow transformers [26, 28], PRXPixel [32] (PRX) and HiDream-O1-Image [2]. Both operate directly on raw pixels using a linear patch embedding E, with patch sizes P = 16 and P = 32 , respectively, and rotary posi-tional embeddings [37]. The network fθ predicts the clean target from its noisy counterpart: 

xσ = (1 − σ) x0 + σ k ϵ, ˆx0 = fθ (xσ , σ, c ), (1) where ϵ ∼ N (0, I), σ ∈ (0 , 1] is the noise level and k is the native noise scale of the backbone. 

Conditioning through the patch embedding. A latent matting model obtains its image condition from the VAE, whose code of the image is appended to the noisy latent. To adapt pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers for image-conditioned matting, we reuse their native patch em-bedding to encode the input image and trimap alongside the noisy target matte. Unlike latent approaches that encode image conditions through a VAE [33], our formulation re-quires no separate condition encoder or adapter. Specifi-cally, we concatenate the three patch-token sequences: 

S =  E(xσ ) ; E(I) + s ; E( ¯T ) + s , (2) For PRX, s is a learned vector initialized to zero and shared across all condition tokens; HiDream uses no such vector (s = 0 ) and instead distinguishes the three segments along the temporal axis of its rotary positional encoding. The seg-ments share the same spatial grid, so corresponding tokens at position (i, j ) receive identical spatial rotary coordinates. This alignment enables joint self-attention to directly asso-ciate target tokens with image and trimap tokens at corre-sponding locations. The pretrained HiDream backbone can already exploit this spatial correspondence before matting fine-tuning, as demonstrated in our reference reconstruction experiment. With a fixed text prompt, the target tokens are projected directly into pixel patches, enabling alpha predic-tion and supervision without an intermediate VAE decoder.

# 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Implementation details. We evaluate PixelMatte with two pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers, PRX-7B (PRXPixel [32]) and HiDream-O1-Image 8B [2]. Both fol-low the fine-tuning procedure of Sec. 3.2; PRX operates at 1024 2, while HiDream is adapted to 2048 2 for its final training stages and inference. PixelMatte (PRX-7B) and PixelMatte (HiDream-8B) denote the complete models with LayerShift and the High-Pass Refiner. Inference is a sin-gle forward pass without test-time augmentation, and we 5report results averaged over two independently evaluated noise seeds. On a single H200 GPU, PRX and HiDream take 0.46 s at 1024 2 and 0.64 s at 2048 2, respectively, com-pared with 0.91 s for RenderMatte at 1024 2.

Datasets, metrics and baselines. We evaluate on P3M-500-NP [14], AM-2K [16], AIM-500 [15] and RenderMatte-2K (RM-2K) [33], using their released trimaps. We report SAD, MSE ( ×10 3), Grad and Conn. In Tab. 1, Conn follows the implementation released with RenderMatte for consistency with the published results; the remaining tables use the definition of DIM [44]. We com-pare against representative supervised and diffusion-based matting methods using the published results collected by RenderMatte [33]. 

4.2. Comparison Experiments 

Quantitative comparison. Table 1 compares PixelMatte with existing matting methods on the four benchmarks. On P3M-500-NP and AM-2K, which are in-domain for our real-data stage, PixelMatte (HiDream-8B) reduces SAD and Grad by 30–44% relative to RenderMatte, and Pixel-Matte (PRX-7B), which runs at the same 1024 2 resolution as RenderMatte, by 24–38%. These gains are already evi-dent in the one-step predictions before calibration, and the HiDream model trained only in the synthetic stage already achieves a lower Grad than RenderMatte on all three real-image benchmarks. On AIM-500, both backbones reduce Grad by 24–31%, indicating improved accuracy in fine spa-tial transitions. On the synthetic RM-2K benchmark, Pixel-Matte performs comparably to RenderMatte. These results show that pixel-space diffusion is an effective alternative to latent generative matting, particularly on fine structures in real images. 

Qualitative comparison. Figure 4 presents visual com-parisons on the four benchmarks, with zoomed regions se-lected independently of the methods. PixelMatte recovers fine alpha structures such as hair strands and animal fur, which complements its lower gradient error.

4.4. Analysis and Discussion 

Effects of calibration. Table 4 decomposes SAD by the ground-truth alpha value and the distance to the contour. LayerShift provides modest improvements across all pixel subsets, consistent with its role in regional opacity calibra-tion. In contrast, the High-Pass Refiner achieves its largest gains near hard boundaries: on saturated pixels within 3 px of the contour, the SAD contribution decreases from 0.54 after LayerShift to 0.23 after refinement. Figure 5 further distinguishes the two behaviors: LayerShift adjusts opac-ity over extended regions, whereas the High-Pass Refiner introduces localized corrections along object contours. 

Application to a latent backbone. We further apply the same refiner architecture to RenderMatte, a latent diffusion matting model; each refiner is trained on the predictions of its own backbone using only RenderMatte’s synthetic com-posite training set (Tab. 5). Both models benefit from re-finement on all four benchmarks. On RM-2K, the refiner reduces the SAD of HiDream from 3.74 to 3.37 and that of RenderMatte from 3.33 to 3.28, which shows that learned boundary calibration applies to different generative matting backbones.

Limitations. Although direct pixel-space prediction re-moves the compressed latent representation and its decoder, it does not remove the limitations of generative pretraining or of matting supervision. PixelMatte inherits visual pri-ors learned primarily for image synthesis, which may not fully capture the opacity characteristics of challenging ma-terials: we observe residual low-amplitude alpha in satu-rated regions on RM-2K and noise-seed sensitivity for some transparent objects on AIM-500. Our calibration modules, which are trained separately for each backbone, improve re-gional opacity and boundary transitions but do not resolve every such error. 

# 5. Conclusion 

We presented PixelMatte, demonstrating that pretrained pixel-space diffusion models can be effectively adapted to high-fidelity image matting through simple spatially aligned conditioning and task-specific supervision. By directly pre-dicting alpha mattes in pixel space, our approach avoids VAE-based reconstruction while retaining the visual pri-ors acquired during generative pretraining. Lightweight 8opacity and boundary calibration modules further improve prediction fidelity, yielding strong results across two dis-tinct diffusion backbones and multiple matting benchmarks. Our findings suggest that pixel-space generative foundation models offer a promising alternative to latent diffusion for precision-sensitive dense prediction tasks, extending their potential beyond image synthesis.
