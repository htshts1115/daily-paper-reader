Abstract
Generalized panorama depth estimation has witnessed
great advances in recent years. These methods typically
achieve robust zero-shot generalization performance us-
ing large pretrained models, but their high computational
cost makes real-time inference on embedded platforms chal-
lenging. In contrast, previous lightweight panorama mod-
els have mostly been developed for single-domain evalu-
ations and cannot generalize well due to domain shifts.
To reduce the gap between these two types of mod-
els, we introduce EG360, an efficient and generalizable
panorama depth estimation model.
Specifically, build-
ing on a lightweight depth model, we design an Adaptive
Panorama-Perspective Distillation (APPD) strategy guided
by scene geometry, along with a Spherical Cross-Level Fea-
ture Fusion (SCFF) module for global semantic perception.
As a result, with only 6.593M parameters after reparam-
eterization, EG360 achieves strong zero-shot performance
on real-world benchmarks and runs at 35 FPS on a Jetson
Orin NX 16GB in MAXN mode, whereas a previous ViT-
small model runs at less than 2 FPS. Our code will also be
made public for future research.

timation is an important task that infers the 3D geometry of
the corresponding scene from a 360 degree image, which
can be useful for other applications such as virtual reality
[3, 44] and autonomous driving [30, 38].
In the domain of monocular panorama depth estimation,
some generalizable methods [23, 46] that can perform ro-
bust zero-shot predictions have emerged. Though they can

lation and allowing it to capture semantic information effi-
ciently are necessary. Concretely, we propose an Adaptive
Panorama-Perspective Distillation (APPD) strategy to ex-
ploit panorama and perspective teachers jointly. Whereas
Depth Anywhere [45] and PanDA [6] rely on perspective
and panoramic teachers, respectively, we combine their
complementary supervision. Table 2 shows that DAP [23]
performs better on Stanford2D3D, which is indoor, while
MoGe2 [47] outperforms on Metropolis, which is outdoor.
Their relative usefulness may also vary across views: a
perspective crop dominated by a wall may provide limited
cues for estimating absolute distance, whereas a panorama
teacher can use the surrounding contexts. Motivated by this,
APPD uses the proposed normalized plane-fitting residuals
to dynamically adaptively weigh the two teacher losses, as-
signing more weight to the panorama teacher for more pla-
nar views. Additionally, to aggregate global contexts while
accounting for spherical geometry, we introduce Spheri-
cal Cross-Level Feature Fusion (SCFF). SCFF uses cross-
level attention [40] between the two coarsest encoder fea-
ture maps, with the finer map querying the coarser one. At-
tention combines feature similarity with a spherical direc-
tion bias that reflects the spatial proximity on the sphere.
Combining these two proposed techniques, we present
EG360, a lightweight monocular panorama depth estima-
tion model that can perform robust zero-shot predictions.
Training on abundant labeled data [36, 48, 59] together with
unlabeled data [9, 61], EG360, comprising 6.593M param-
eters and costing 23.492 GFLOPs after structural reparame-
terization [12], can achieve an average AbsRel of 0.168 and
run on Orin NX at 35 FPS. It not only runs faster than pre-
vious lightweight models but also narrows the gap between
lightweight and foundation models.
In summary, our contributions are as follows:
• We propose Adaptive Panorama–Perspective Distillation
(APPD), which uses normalized plane-fitting residuals to
weigh panorama and perspective teacher supervision for
unlabeled data adaptively.
• We introduce Spherical Cross-Level Feature Fusion
(SCFF), which combines cross-level attention with a
spherical direction bias to aggregate global context be-
tween the two coarsest feature maps.
• We develop EG360, a lightweight model with 6.593M pa-
rameters and 23.492 GFLOPs, and demonstrate its zero-
shot performance on real-world benchmarks.
2. Related Works
2.1. In-Domain Panorama Depth Estimation
Depth estimation for panorama images presents unique
challenges due to the equirectangular projection (ERP) and
resulting distortions. Some works directly work on the ERP
image and propose different techniques to tackle the dis-
tortion. OmniDepth [63] uses both rectangular and square
CNN filters. ACDNet [62] combines convolution kernels
with different dilations with adaptive channel-wise fusions.
PanoFormer [33] and EGFormer [54] propose panorama-
specific transformer blocks to adapt widely used trans-
former [40] blocks to the panorama domain. Some other
works use additional distortion-free formats for 360 images
to mitigate the negative effects of distortions. BiFuse [42],
BiFuse++ [43], and UniFuse [17] use cubemap (CP) im-
ages and fuse the features from CP and ERP images adap-
tively for better performance than using ERP or CP alone.
HRDFuse [1] enhances depth prediction by collaboratively
learning from ERP and tangent projections. In addition,
S2Net [21] moves to the spherical surface generated from
the ERP image with the HEALPix sampling [14]. Never-
theless, these methods are typically trained and evaluated
within the same domain, limiting their generalizability.
2.2. Zero-Shot Panorama Depth Estimation
Different from in-domain methods, zero-shot methods aim
to perform robust predictions on unseen data.
Depth
AnyWhere [45] distills the knowledge from large per-
spective models [52] to small panorama models via unla-
beled panorama data, while PanDA [6] distills from large
panorama models.
DA360 [18] finetunes Depth Any-
thing V2 [53] and equips the DPT [32] decoder with ERP
padding. DA2 [20] generates high-quality panoramic depth
data from perspective data via perspective-to-panorama dif-
fusion [37].
DAP [23] introduces a three-stage curation
pipeline to generate reliable pseudo labels for unlabeled im-
ages. DepthMaster [46] and PaGeR [5] utilize a Depth Any-
thing V3 [22] structure to process cubemap images and pro-
duce ERP depth images. However, these zero-shot meth-
ods with large backbones tend to be computationally ex-
pensive, making them unsuitable for deployment on mobile
platforms. Thus, we aim to develop a lightweight model by
distilling complementary knowledge from panoramic and
perspective teachers adaptively and enabling efficient spher-
ical global context modeling.
2.3. Zero-Shot Perspective Depth Estimation
Recent advances in monocular depth estimation from per-
spective images have benefited from improved architectures
and increasingly diverse training data.
MiDaS [31] en-
ables joint learning from heterogeneous datasets using a ro-
bust, scale-shift-invariant loss. Depth Anything [52] and
Depth Anything V2 [53] leverage large-scale unlabeled im-
ages to improve generalization. Beyond relative depth es-
timation, MoGe2 [47] recovers metric-scale 3D point maps
from single images using a decoupled design that separates
relative geometry and metric-scale learning. Despite their
strong generalization capabilities, their computation costs
make them unfriendly to mobile platforms. To improve de-

3.4. Spherical Cross-Level Feature Fusion
Previous works have shown that perceiving the global con-
text is important for depth estimation [27, 57]. Also, our
ZipDepth baseline already implements several modules to
enhance global perception.
However, due to the distor-
tion and left-right discontinuity in ERP images, its global
modeling is insufficient, so we design a Spherical Cross-
Level Feature Fusion (SCFF) module to further enhance its
contextual modeling capabilities. As shown in Fig. 3, it
consists of a spherical cross-level attention module and a
panorama local feed-forward network.
Cross-level attention
Let C3 and C4 denote the outputs
of the third and fourth encoder stages. We flatten their spa-
tial dimensions into token sequences X3 and X4, respec-
tively. Queries, keys, and values are computed as Q =
LN(X3)WQ, K = LN(X4)WK, and V = LN(X4)WV .
Here, LN denotes layer normalization [4], and WQ, WK,
and WV are learnable projection matrices. Bias terms are
omitted for brevity. In this way, each fine-scale query can
therefore aggregate information from the coarse feature.

Implementation Details
We initialize the base student
from the released ZipDepth checkpoint and train each of
our configurations independently for 100,000 iterations at
512 × 1024 ERP resolution using the PyTorch [29] frame-
work on an RTX 2080 Ti. We use AdamW [24] with weight
decay 10−3, gradient-norm clipping at 1.000, and AMP
FP16. We also use a OneCycle [35] learning schedule with
a peak learning rate of 10−4, a 1% warm-up fraction, and
linear annealing. Unless stated otherwise, in each iteration
we use a batch size of 8 for labeled data and 8 for unlabeled
data when using distillation. We use the checkpoint from
the last step to evaluate for fairness. Augmentation includes
horizontal flips, color jitter, random yaw and pitch rotations,
and polar RGB masking, following DA360 [18].
Comparison settings
For UniFuse, BiFuse++, and ACD-
Net, we retrain them using ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18
encoders. For DA360-S, the ViT-Small version, we finetune
its released checkpoint. These four models are retrained
on our labeled data for fairness. For DAP, DepthMaster,
and PaGeR, we directly use released weights. Efficiency is
measured on Jetson Orin NX 16GB (MAXN, PyTorch ea-
ger, AMP FP16, batch size one, native input resolutions).
Latency averages 300 forward passes after warm-up, ex-
cluding I/O and CPU preprocessing. Attention uses native
PyTorch Scaled Dot-Product Attention (SDPA) with exter-
nal xFormers [19] and flashAttn [10, 11] disabled for fair-
ness and pytorch-native. Our model uses reparameterization

4.2. Comparison with Existing Methods
Table 3 compares depth accuracy and inference cost, with
latency measured on a Jetson Orin NX 16GB in MAXN
mode. Compared with UniFuse, EG360 reduces AbsRel by
26.245%, 39.432%, and 20.297% on Matterport3D, Stan-

δ1 on all three datasets. With only 6.593M parameters and
23.492 GFLOPs, EG360 is also more efficient than previ-
ous lightweight models. Compared to DA360-S, EG360
achieves similar results on Matterport3D and Metropolis
while running 18 times faster. DAP and PaGeR can achieve
better results, but they require 2543.697 and 26200.188
GFLOPs of computation. As for DepthMaster, it achieves
the lowest indoor AbsRel, but its high Metropolis error may
be related to the absence of outdoor panoramas in training.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we presented EG360, an efficient and general-
izable panorama depth estimation model built on top of our
Adaptive Panorama–Perspective Distillation technique and
Spherical Cross-Level Feature Fusion module. Experiments
demonstrate that our model can achieve strong generaliza-
tion ability while enabling real-time inference on a Jetson
Orin NX 16GB in MAXN mode.
Limitations and future work.
Although EG360 can
achieve competitive results, we only used limited unla-
beled data, and scaling the unlabeled data may further en-
hance performance [41, 51]. Additionally, as a single-frame
model, it suffers from temporal incoherence.
Extending


EG360 to panoramic video depth estimation [8, 36] via
cross-frame fusions may reduce depth-flickering effects.
