# TacHOI: Contact-Aware Object Reconstruction and Tracking for Egocentric Videos 

Jackson Codd, Haoyu Li, Xianyi Cheng 

Abstract — Egocentric videos offer a scalable source of human demonstrations for robot learning. However, recovering metric object geometry and motion from monocular video is hindered by scale–depth ambiguity and hand-induced occlusion, while image-derived contact and shape priors alone cannot uniquely determine metric scale. We introduce TacHOI, a training-free framework that integrates tactile contact with monocular vision for joint hand–object reconstruction and tracking, without requiring object templates or depth sensors. Starting from pretrained visual estimates, TacHOI uses tactile contact to constrain object depth relative to the estimated hand, while spatially separated contacts on a metrically calibrated hand provide a physical ruler for object extent. It incorporates these constraints into a joint optimization of hand poses, object trajectories, and anisotropic object scale alongside visual alignment. Across 100 ARCTIC and HO3D sequences with synthesized tactile observations, TacHOI achieves the lowest median ADD-S* among the evaluated baselines, reducing error from 15.6 to 3.6 cm on ARCTIC and achieving 2.8 cm on HO3D. Additional experiments on OpenTouch demonstrate its applicability to measured tactile observations. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Humans effortlessly manipulate objects in daily life, from pouring a drink to assembling a tool. The growing adoption of smart glasses [1] offers new opportunities to capture these everyday interactions at scale, turning daily activities into a rich source of demonstrations for robots to learn manipulation skills [2]. However, a key challenge of learning manipulation skills from egocentric videos is the ambiguity in estimating precise hand-object trajectories, especially for monocular observation. Hand-induced occlusion and self-occlusion further complicate accurate reconstruction and tracking [13]. A fundamental challenge in monocular reconstruction is scale ambiguity: an object scaled by a factor of k and placed 

k times farther from the camera produces the same image projection. Even accurate masks and 2D tracks therefore cannot uniquely determine its metric scale and depth. When demonstrations are retargeted to a robot, scale errors can lead to misplaced grasps and incorrect motion trajectories. Exist-ing methods rely on additional information, including known instance-level 3D meshes [4], depth measurements [21], multiple camera views [6], object scans [7], or learned shape and grasp priors [8]. These requirements constrain their ap-plicability to in-the-wild human demonstrations. Meanwhile, growing efforts to capture such demonstrations with tactile gloves [9]–[11] offer a complementary source of geometric constraints. A. Model-Free Object Pose Estimation 

Most object-pose estimators assume a CAD model at test time. Model-free methods instead construct object rep-resentations from reference observations or learned geo-metric priors. FoundationPose [4] at its core is a CAD-based method, but it supports model-free pose estimation from reference images. Any6D [5] reconstructs an object representation from a single RGB-D observation and jointly estimates metric size and 6-DoF pose. Although effective on unseen objects, these approaches still rely on reference observations or depth and generally do not exploit physical constraints from hand-object interaction. Recent methods instead reconstruct object motion directly from monocular interaction videos. Do-as-I-Do [12], MagicHOI [13], Follow My HOLD [14], Grasp in Gaussians [15], AGILE [16], and CHOIR [17] combine hand-object interaction cues with learned geometric, generative, or contact priors to improve reconstruction and tracking. These methods demonstrate the value of interaction context, but their contact constraints are inferred from images and therefore remain sensitive to occlusion and errors in monocular depth, scale, and hand pose. TacHOI instead uses measured pressure to provide direct evidence of when and where contact occurs. 

B. Object Reconstruction 

Classical geometry-based methods such as GSLAM [18] recover scene structure through multi-view consistency and camera motion estimation. Reconstruction of handheld ob-jects is more challenging because both the camera and the object may move. Hands also cause severe occlusion, and monocular geometry remains ambiguous in scale. Sequence-level neural reconstruction methods address these challenges by jointly modeling hands and objects. HOLD [3] uses compositional implicit fields for category-agnostic recon-struction, while G-HOP [8] and MagicHOI [13] introduce learned generative or novel-view priors to recover geometry under limited observations. Such priors enable plausible completion of occluded regions but do not necessarily re-cover the metric configuration of the specific observed object. Feed-forward models provide a faster alternative. SAM 3D Objects [19] reconstructs complete objects from masked images, while ForeHOI [20] incorporates video evidence and hand-object-specific completion. These models provide useful geometric priors, but image plausibility and silhouette consistency alone do not determine metric scale or depth. Additionally, pretrained models are limited by the scope of their training data. For naturally ambiguous objects, like bananas, SAM 3D cannot reconstruct with the correct aspect ratios. TacHOI complements pretrained reconstruction with measured contact, introducing a new information source to ground metric pose estimation and reconstruction. 

C. Visuotactile Hand–Object Estimation 

Contact has previously been used as a geometric prior without direct tactile sensing. Contact Potential Fields (CPF) [22] optimize predicted hand-object contacts toward physically plausible configurations, but the contact itself is inferred visually and is therefore unreliable under severe occlusion. Physical tactile sensing can provide stronger con-straints. Tac2Pose [23] estimates object pose from optical tactile observations, while NeuralFeels [24] combines RGB-D, tactile sensing, and robot proprioception to jointly esti-mate object shape and pose. The ViTaM family [25], [26] similarly fuses vision with distributed tactile sensing for reconstruction during deformable or forceful interactions. Mack et al. [27] further show that low-resolution binary con-tact improves RGB-D object-pose estimation within a factor-graph formulation. These approaches demonstrate the value of tactile information but typically assume known object geometry, depth sensing, calibrated robot kinematics, spe-cialized tactile hardware, or learned object-specific models. TacHOI targets a complementary setting: monocular recon-struction of an unknown-category object manipulated by an instrumented human hand. Rather than reconstructing local surface geometry from tactile images, TacHOI converts low-resolution pressure measurements into contact constraints between the estimated hand and object, providing metric information for object depth, scale, and pose without a CAD template, depth sensor, or calibrated robot proprioception. A. Overview and Initialization 

TacHOI jointly refines hand pose, object pose, and anisotropic object scale using complementary visual and tactile cues. Silhouettes and sparse tracks align the object with the image, tactile contact anchors it to the hand, and the metrically calibrated hand constrains object extent through caliper and contact-spread measurements. The OpenTouch [9] dataset provides a 16 × 16 pressure map. For benchmarks without tactile measurements, we synthesize maps from ground-truth hand and object poses by assigning palmar-facing MANO vertices to their nearest tax-els and measuring their distance to the object mesh. Pressure activates within 3 mm of contact and increases linearly to 1 as the distance approaches zero, approximating sensor com-pliance while preserving pressure magnitude as a continuous per-taxel weight. A YOLO-based detector [39] localizes each hand, and HaMeR [33] initializes its MANO [34] parameters and 2-D joints. Metric hand scale is obtained from Rokoko measurements on OpenTouch [9] and ground-truth MANO bone lengths on ARCTIC [37] and HO3D [38]. For the object, Qwen3-VL [28] and SAM 3 [29] produce a modal mask, which Diffusion-VAS [30] completes to an amodal mask Mt. SAM 3D Objects [19] reconstructs a canonical mesh V0 = {vj }Jj=1 , Do-As-I-Do [12] initializes its camera-space pose and trajectory, and CoTracker3 [31] supplies sparse 2-D tracks for when the object is off-screen. We optimize Eq. (4) with Adam [40] for 600 iterations using an initial learning rate of 3 × 10 −3 and cosine anneal-ing [41]. Each iteration samples 12 frames for the shared hand-object terms and up to eight additional frames from those on which the hand is detected. We freeze α for the first 33% of iterations so that pose converges before scale can absorb pose error. We render soft silhouettes with PyTorch3D [35], and contact-candidate vertices follow DexGraspNet [36]. Across 54 ARCTIC fits, joint refinement required a median of 50 s per clip, with a range of 42 –111 s. We evaluate whether tactile contact can serve as an effec-tive metric constraint for object pose and reconstruction. We assess camera-space tracking accuracy, analyze the contribu-tions of individual loss groups, evaluate object geometry and scale, and demonstrate the complete pipeline using measured tactile data on in-the-wild OpenTouch clips. 

A. Experimental Setup 

Datasets and tactile observations. ARCTIC [37] con-tributes 55 egocentric bimanual clips spanning 11 articulated objects and seven subjects. It provides object and MANO ground truth but no sensor depth. HO3D [38] contributes 45 single-hand clips covering nine YCB objects and provides object, hand, and RGB-D ground truth. We evaluate five clips per object and subsample each clip to at most 150 frames. Neither benchmark records touch, so we synthesize the map from ground truth object and hand pose. OpenTouch [9] provides 30 in-the-wild clips with a measured 16 ×16 pressure glove and motion-capture hand skeleton, but no object-pose, object-geometry, or depth ground truth. 

Baselines and coverage. We compare TacHOI against FoundationPose [4], BundleSDF [21], and ForeHOI [20]. FoundationPose is provided the ground-truth CAD model. On HO3D, FoundationPose, Do As I Do, and BundleSDF use sensor depth, and on ARCTIC they use ViPE-estimated monocular depth. These comparisons are therefore informa-tive but not strictly input-matched. We exclude HOLD [3] and Magic HOI [13] because their per-sequence runtime makes evaluation at our benchmark scale impractical. Vi-TaM [25] is not directly comparable because it relies on a learned distributed-tactile framework and targets dynamic interaction reconstruction rather than metric object pose and scale. 

Metrics. ADD-S ∗ is the directed mean nearest-surface dis-tance from the posed prediction to ground truth. The asterisk distinguishes this non-corresponding-mesh variant, which includes both reconstruction and pose error. We also report centroid error, along-ray error, and rotation error. Shape metrics use similarity ICP followed by bidirectional Chamfer distance (CD) and F-score at 5 and 10 mm. Similarity align-ment removes translation, rotation, and scale and therefore evaluates shape rather than metric reconstruction. Scale error is reported analogously as the magnitude of the signed median. We compute a statistic per clip and report the median across all clips per dataset.

We presented TacHOI, a framework that jointly refines hand poses, object motion, and metric scale by combining monocular visual estimates with tactile contact.
