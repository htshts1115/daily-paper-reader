Fuse to Recognize the Unseen: Class-Relative Infrared–Visible Fusion for
Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation

Abstract
Visible–infrared image fusion leverages complementary
thermal cues to enhance visual perception, particularly un-
der challenging illumination.
However, such perceptual
complementarity does not readily translate into semantic
gains in open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS),
where CLIP-based models are predominantly pretrained on
RGB imagery.
Directly introducing IR information may
instead disrupt RGB–language alignment, turning promis-
ing thermal cues into semantic interference that undermines
unseen-class recognition. Our pixel-level RGB–IR seman-
tic analysis reveals that class-relevant thermal cues can
correct certain RGB misclassifications while preserving re-
liable RGB predictions, motivating RGB-anchored, class-
relative thermal cue selection. Based on this insight, we
propose EmergeFuse, an infrared–visible fusion framework
for OVSS that selectively converts complementary thermal
evidence into unseen-class semantic corrections. Specifi-
cally, class-relative cue selection evaluates RGB–IR seman-
tic responses and calibrated thermal evidence relative to
RGB predictions, generating class-relative correction re-
quests through competitive selection. Text-contrast image
rectification then translates these selected requests into se-
mantic rectification directions through text contrasts, guid-
ing evidence-gated feature updates to realize complemen-
tary thermal evidence for unseen-class recognition.
Ex-
periments on three benchmarks demonstrate gains of up to
4.755% in unseen-class mIoU and 15.730% in strict com-
plementary conversion rate over existing fusion methods,
while maintaining competitive fusion quality.

By incorporating thermal information, fusion can bene-

To investigate this issue, we apply the same frozen OVSS

identified.

class-relative thermal cue selection to exploit complemen-
tary IR evidence while preserving reliable RGB predictions.

Guided by these observations, we propose EmergeFuse,
which selectively exploits complementary thermal evidence
while preserving reliable RGB semantics, as illustrated
in Fig. 1(b).
Class-relative cue selection (CRCS) takes
the highest-scoring RGB class at each pixel as a refer-
ence and evaluates RGB–IR semantic responses and ther-
mal evidence to identify class-relevant thermal cues that
can compensate for RGB misclassifications while suppress-
ing potentially misleading cues.
The selected thermal
cues are then passed to text-contrast image rectification
(TCIR), which derives semantic correction directions from
candidate–reference text contrasts to guide their selective
integration into multi-scale fusion features, enabling the
fused image to express complementary thermal evidence as
language-aligned category cues that enhance unseen-class
segmentation.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We reveal a class-dependent challenge in transferring
thermal complementarity to CLIP-based OVSS. Our
RGB–IR pixel-level analysis uncovers concentrated class
confusion in RGB predictions for unseen categories and
substantial IR-only recovery of RGB errors, highlighting
the need for class-relative thermal cue selection that bal-
ances semantic correction and preservation.
• We propose EmergeFuse, a class-relative semantic rec-
tification framework that selects complementary thermal
evidence to generate targeted rectification requests, then
realizes these requests as language-aligned visual cues
in fused images for unseen-class recognition without re-
training.
• Experiments on three benchmarks demonstrate gains of
up to 4.755% and 15.730% in unseen-class mIoU and
strict complementary conversion rate, respectively, while
maintaining competitive fusion quality.
2. Related Work
2.1. Task and Language-Guided Image Fusion
A key challenge in infrared–visible image fusion is to move
beyond image-quality-oriented reconstruction and adapt the
fusion process to downstream perception requirements. To
this end, task-aware methods connect fusion with down-
stream tasks through task supervision and semantic fea-
ture interaction [3, 22, 23, 35, 39, 53, 54].
TDFusion
[2] learns fusion objectives from downstream losses via
meta-learning, CLDyN [43] adapts fusion through closed-
loop semantic compensation, and UniFusion [17] promotes
cross-task fusion through modality-consistent representa-
tions. DRFusion [28] incorporates task-specific objectives
for adaptive image fusion.
Beyond task-driven supervision, language-guided meth-
ods exploit textual semantics to provide more flexible con-
trol over the fusion process [15, 18, 29, 32, 52]. Text-IF [44]
and GText-IF [45] leverage textual guidance to adapt fusion
to different image degradations, while OmniFuse [50] ex-
tends language-driven control to semantic object enhance-
ment under composite degradations. These advances en-
able fusion to accommodate diverse task objectives and se-
mantic requirements. However, such adaptability does not
necessarily translate into the ability to recognize categories
unseen during task-specific training.
2.2. Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation
Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) aims to
assign pixel-level labels from text-defined categories, in-
cluding classes unseen during task-specific training [11, 16,
26, 27, 46, 48]. This generalization capability largely re-

lies on CLIP’s pretrained vision–language alignment. To
improve dense prediction, CAT-Seg [5] models image–text
matching costs, while SED [40, 61] combines hierarchical
encoding, gradual cost decoding, and category rejection.
Training-free approaches, including SCLIP [34], Proxy-

matching and contextual interactions. Despite their archi-
tectural differences, these methods largely build upon visual
representations pretrained on RGB imagery, posing chal-

taining reliable vision–language alignment.
To extend open-vocabulary recognition beyond visible
imagery, multimodal OVSS methods explore incorporating
complementary information from additional sensing modal-
ities [1, 4, 9, 33, 37].
OpenRSS [51] introduces ther-
mal prompts into SAM, while Open-RGBT [47] combines
text and visual prompts for object localization and box-
guided mask generation on fused RGB–IR images. These
approaches extend the visual evidence available for open-
vocabulary recognition, but incorporating thermal informa-
tion does not necessarily ensure its effective semantic uti-
lization.
In contrast, EmergeFuse evaluates thermal evi-
dence relative to RGB-predicted classes and selectively re-
alizes complementary semantic corrections in fused image.

Evaluation Metrics. We report seen, unseen, and overall
mIoU, together with the harmonic mean (hIoU) of seen and
unseen mIoU. To evaluate semantic preservation and com-
plementary conversion, we measure semantic preservation
rate (SPR) and strict complementary conversion rate (SCC)
for both seen and unseen classes. SCC measures the recov-
ery rate on a fixed set of pixels misclassified by RGB but
correctly recognized by IR. Fusion quality is assessed using
VIF, Qabf, SSIM, and SF. Detailed metric definitions are
provided in Supplementary Materials.
4.2. Open-Vocabulary Performance and Semantic
Conversion
Table 1 compares OVSS performance on FMB, MFNet,
and SemanticRT. As few existing methods directly ad-
dress open-vocabulary-aware infrared–visible fusion, we
primarily compare against representative fusion methods.
Open-RGBT [47] is excluded from the quantitative com-

EmergeFuse achieves the highest hIoU. Its unseen mIoU
reaches 33.772%, 37.849%, and 30.194%, outperforming
the strongest conventional fusion baselines by 3.308, 4.755,

5. Conclusion
Effective RGB–IR fusion for OVSS requires selectively
translating complementary thermal evidence into language-
aligned visual semantics. EmergeFuse achieves this through
RGB-anchored guidance and image-level rectification, im-
proving unseen-class recognition while preserving reli-
able RGB predictions.
Experiments on three bench-
marks demonstrate improved unseen-class segmentation
with competitive fusion quality.
