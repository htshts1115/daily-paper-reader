ABSTRACT
Training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) has made strong
progress by adapting CLIP-like representations to dense prediction, but its re-
maining errors are often local, structured, and tied to the active label space. A
successful correction must be visually plausible, legal under the target vocabulary,
and executable in the segmentation score field. We propose SEER, a training-free
test-time repair layer for Semantic Evidence and Executable Region propagation.
SEER casts post-prediction OVSS repair as evidence-conditioned label propagation.
The OVSS score space proposes compact candidate labels, a frozen VLM verifies
closed-set regional evidence, and an execution gate certifies whether the evidence
is legally and semantically actionable. The accepted label is then propagated by
an OVSS-side policy: coherent high-confidence regions allow broad propagation,
while object- or boundary-risk cases use conservative score-grounded propagation.
Across eight standard OVSS benchmarks, SEER reaches 58.0 average mIoU with-
out updating OVSS weights. Matched-input controls isolate the repair mechanism,
while resource measurements report the cost of the complete frozen system.

INTRODUCTION
Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) assigns dense labels from an open or user-specified
vocabulary. Starting from early open-vocabulary scene parsing Zhao et al. (2017), the field has been

accelerated by CLIP Radford et al. (2021) and OpenCLIP Cherti et al. (2023). Training-free methods
further improve dense prediction by modifying CLIP attention, calibrating patch-text similarity, and
injecting visual priors. MaskCLIP Zhou et al. (2022) exposes dense CLIP predictions, SCLIP Wang
et al. (2024) adapts self-attention for segmentation, ProxyCLIP Lan et al. (2024b) introduces proxy
features, Trident Shi et al. (2025) restructures feature aggregation, and CorrCLIP Zhang et al. (2025)
corrects patch correlation. As these systems become stronger, the bottleneck shifts from global
recognition to local residues such as boundary spillovers, object-part confusions, and stuff/object
ambiguities.
Residual repair requires more than crop classification. OVSS predictions live under an active label
space, where a visually meaningful region can be non-target when its label is absent, while a scene or
stuff region becomes executable once the corresponding label enters the vocabulary. A repair action
therefore has three coupled requirements. The label must be visually supported, legal under the active
vocabulary, and propagated to the right pixels. Regional evidence can suggest a semantic direction,
while pixel support must still be recovered from the dense prediction.
General-purpose vision-language models (VLMs) provide a natural source of regional semantic
evidence. Qwen-VL Qwen Team (2026), LLaVA-OneVision-1.5 An et al. (2025), and GPT-4o Hurst
et al. (2024) can compare candidate descriptions and answer localized visual questions. Direct pixel
decisions remain unreliable because regional semantics, label-space validity, and pixel execution
become entangled in one response. A VLM may name the highlighted region correctly without
knowing whether the label is executable in the current OVSS score field or how far it should propagate
inside the proposal. We therefore restrict the VLM to regional evidence and retain pixel authority in
the segmentation model.
This paper studies how regional VLM evidence can be made executable in OVSS label propagation.
Our answer is SEER, short for Semantic Evidence and Executable Region propagation. SEER uses
prediction-side signals from the baseline mask and score tensor to select auditable proposals, asks a
VLM to verify a compact score-derived candidate set with uncertain as an abstention option, and
accepts evidence only if it passes executable gates. After certification, the OVSS score field executes
the decision through a propagation policy. Coherent high-confidence regions can use broad label
propagation, while object-like or boundary-sensitive regions use conservative support derived from
the underlying OVSS score field for pixel-level propagation.
This framing keeps the method tied to OVSS. Labels are proposed by open-vocabulary scores, action
validity is checked against the active label space, and pixel changes are produced by score-space
propagation. Broad propagation is used after closed-set evidence verification, while conservative
propagation remains the fallback when boundaries or selector noise make broad support risky. Across
eight standard benchmarks, SEER reaches 58.0 average mIoU with the ViT-L/14 base. Fixed-
prediction controls hold the score tensor, proposals, candidates, parser, and audit budget constant
while varying the repair signal or executor. We also report complete-system latency and memory,
lighter verifiers, proposal-source transfer, sensitivity tests, and residual failures.
This paper makes three contributions.
• We formulate OVSS post-prediction repair as executable region propagation, separating regional
semantic evidence, label-space validity, and pixel support.
• SEER is a training-free evidence-to-execution layer that uses frozen VLM verification while
leaving pixel propagation to the OVSS score field.
• Systematic diagnostics evaluate compact candidates, executable gates, multi-view evidence,
evidence-conditioned propagation, and complete-system cost.

METHOD
Overview. SEER repairs a fixed OVSS prediction in four steps (Fig. 2). It (1) selects a small
proposal subset, (2) builds legal candidate labels from the frozen score tensor, (3) asks a frozen VLM
to select one candidate or abstain, and (4) lets the OVSS scores decide which pixels may change.
High-confidence coherent regions may use the full proposal; object-like or boundary-sensitive cases

and never predicts or edits a mask.

Implementation details. We follow the CorrCLIP evaluation configuration, while the repair input
itself is a fixed CLIP score field S and its induced prediction Y 0. Region domains come from
a fixed SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2024) proposal bank and are never treated as semantic predictions.
The default verifier is the locally deployable Qwen3.5-9B (Qwen Team, 2026), chosen for strong
regional recognition, open weights, and deterministic decoding; it is not fine-tuned on any evaluation
benchmark. Its public documentation does not provide a sample-level pretraining manifest, so we do
not assume zero overlap with segmentation or grounding data and instead test replacement by three
other VLM families and CLIP. The VLM receives proposal views and legal label names. Ground
truth, target masks, and pixel-write supervision are excluded.
For mechanism studies, we fix Y 0, S, the proposal bank, candidates, parser, gate, propagation
policy, audit inputs, and evaluation split; only the tested repair signal or executor changes. This
fixed-prediction protocol uses MetaCLIP FullCC ViT-B/16 QuickGELU, frozen DINO ViT-B/8,
benchmark-native mappings, and aspect-ratio-preserving resolution (2048, 336) for VOC/PC and
(2048, 448) for City/ADE. Its initial scores are a separate locally frozen diagnostic state, not a
reproduction of the published CorrCLIP row in Tab. 1. The constants were selected once from
aggregate diagnostics on the evaluated natural-image validation domains, then frozen across all eight
benchmarks, two backbones, four OVSS bases, and every verifier; we do not claim domain-blind
development or medical/remote-sensing validation. Appendix C lists the complete contract and
sensitivity tests.

4.3
FIXED-PREDICTION REPAIR ANALYSIS
Fixed-prediction repair.
The fixed-prediction diagnostic in Fig. 1 reports the controlled setting
used in the mechanism analysis. The prediction map and score tensor are fixed, and only the evidence-
to-execution layer is applied. Gains across eight benchmarks rule out changes in backbone, resolution,
or training recipe, supporting the premise that residual OVSS errors are repairable once regional
evidence becomes executable.
Plug-in transfer across OVSS bases.
We next ask whether the repair layer is tied to one score
geometry. The same SEER pipeline is applied to ProxyCLIP, Trident, ResCLIP, and CorrCLIP:
proposals, candidates, VLM auditing, filters, propagation rules, coefficients, and thresholds stay fixed
across bases and benchmarks. Only the OVSS prediction and score cache change. Every reported
base–benchmark pair improves, with mean gains from 4.53 to 5.93 mIoU; full results and confidence
intervals appear in Appendix I.

CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS
SEER frames OVSS repair as executable region propagation after a fixed prediction has been pro-
duced. It couples closed-set VLM evidence with OVSS score-space execution: the verifier identifies
actionable regional labels, and the segmenter decides their pixel support. This evidence-to-execution
design supports high-recall propagation on coherent regions while using conservative support near
uncertain boundaries. Across eight benchmarks, cross-base plug-in tests, and protocol-matched con-
trols, the experiments show consistent repair gains with controlled proposal-level intervention cost.
The controls attribute the gain to the coupling of closed-set regional evidence and score-controlled
execution; proposals, crop classification, and smoothing each remain below the full system. The
complete system is intended for offline or selective repair. It adds 9.3% cached or 19.3% cold latency
and uses 18.7GB peak verifier memory in the measured configuration. Proposal misses, absent
candidates, verifier errors, and weak score support remain important limitations, especially for thin or
mixed regions. Future work will study lighter auditing, stronger proposal coverage, and more robust
boundary execution.

SUPPLEMENTARY OVERVIEW
The main paper reports the compact experimental chain supporting the central claim that SEER

material gives the extended diagnostics behind that chain and uses the same terminology as the main
text. Coherent region propagation applies a certified label to the full proposal support. Boundary-
conservative propagation restricts the support to score- and margin-supported pixels. Adaptive
propagation chooses between these regimes based on evidence form and predicted risk.

In the priority score, αi = |Mi|/|Ω|, qi is the dominant-label fraction, δi is the candidate-minus-
base score gap, λi is the low-margin fraction, ηi is the mean entropy signal when available, and
ri is the fixed risk-label fraction. For the view builder, d = max(box width, box height). The
full view resizes the highlighted image to width 640, the local crop expands the proposal box by
max(32px, 0.3d) before fitting it inside 448 × 448, and the tight crop expands by max(6px, 0.3d)
before fitting it inside 336 × 336. The object-like routing set includes people/person, vehicles,
animals, common furniture, and small handled objects when those names appear in the active dataset
vocabulary. The structural risk-ratio set is fixed to common boundary-sensitive labels including
background, wall, ceiling, floor, ground, road, building, tree, grass, sky, water, mountain, sidewalk,
platform, rock, snow, wood, fence, door, window, curtain, light, sign, track, and shelves. These lists
only affect routing and priority; they do not add candidate labels.
For a proposal Mi, the priority score combines four monotone signals: (i) the purity of the dominant
baseline prediction within the proposal, (ii) proposal area normalized by image area, (iii) competition
between the strongest non-baseline score and the baseline score, and (iv) the fraction of pixels
predicted as risk-sensitive classes. This priority serves as a budget controller. Its coarse weights
encode a fixed preference for impact, purity, score competition, uncertainty, and risk avoidance; they
are not fitted per benchmark. It only orders proposals before VLM verification, and Appendix F
shows that ranking alone is insufficient without closed-set evidence and executable propagation.
For score-grounded propagation, Eq. 14 uses three fixed constants. τs is a small tolerance that allows
the target score to match the current baseline score, τm defines low-margin pixels, and τl is the
wider tolerance allowed only inside low-margin support. We set (τs, τm, τl) = (0.02, 0.20, 0.25)
once and keep the values unchanged across benchmarks. These rules form a deterministic execution
contract: semantic evidence proposes a legal direction, while score support and risk predicates
decide where the direction may write. Boundary-conservative propagation additionally requires low
margin and is triggered when score support is weak after semantic certification. The default adaptive
policy selects coherent propagation for certified low-risk regions and falls back to score-grounded or
boundary-conservative propagation for object-like, boundary-like, large-risk, or high-risk proposals.

Limitations.
SEER is a plug-in repair layer over a fixed OVSS prediction, so the complete-system
comparison reports the accuracy-cost trade-off of adding frozen proposal-level regional auditing to
an OVSS predictor, while matched-input diagnostics isolate repair. Its priority, gate, and routing rules
form a deterministic execution contract, fixed across datasets, bases, and auditors to enforce legal
labels, high-confidence evidence, and score-compatible support. SEER remains shaped by proposal
coverage, candidate recall, regional verifier errors, and latent score support; improving thin structures,
mixed proposals, crowded boundaries, and candidate-set misses in boundary-sensitive images is an
important direction for future work.
