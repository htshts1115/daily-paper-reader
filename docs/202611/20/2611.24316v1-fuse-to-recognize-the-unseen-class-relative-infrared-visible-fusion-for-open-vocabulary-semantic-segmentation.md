## 摘要
可见光-红外图像融合利用互补的热线索增强视觉感知，尤其在具有挑战性的光照条件下。然而，这种感知互补性并不能轻易转化为开放词汇语义分割（OVSS）中的语义增益，因为基于 CLIP 的模型主要是在 RGB 图像上预训练的。直接引入红外信息反而可能破坏 RGB-语言对齐，使有前景的热线索变成语义干扰，从而削弱对未见类别的识别。我们的像素级 RGB-IR 语义分析表明，与类别相关的热线索可以纠正某些 RGB 误分类，同时保留可靠的 RGB 预测，这启发了以 RGB 为锚、类别相对的热线索选择。基于这一洞察，我们提出 EmergeFuse，一种用于 OVSS 的红外-可见光融合框架，它选择性地将互补热证据转化为未见类别的语义校正。具体而言，类别相对线索选择评估相对于 RGB 预测的 RGB-IR 语义响应和校准热证据，通过竞争性选择生成类别相对校正请求。文本对比图像校正随后通过文本对比将这些选定的请求转化为语义校正方向，引导证据门控的特征更新，以实现用于未见类别识别的互补热证据。在三个基准上的实验表明，与现有融合方法相比，未见类别 mIoU 最高提升 4.755%，严格互补转换率最高提升 15.730%，同时保持有竞争力的融合质量。

## Abstract
Visible-infrared image fusion leverages complementary thermal cues to enhance visual perception, particularly under challenging illumination. However, such perceptual complementarity does not readily translate into semantic gains in open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS), where CLIP-based models are predominantly pretrained on RGB imagery. Directly introducing IR information may instead disrupt RGB-language alignment, turning promising thermal cues into semantic interference that undermines unseen-class recognition. Our pixel-level RGB-IR semantic analysis reveals that class-relevant thermal cues can correct certain RGB misclassifications while preserving reliable RGB predictions, motivating RGB-anchored, class-relative thermal cue selection. Based on this insight, we propose EmergeFuse, an infrared-visible fusion framework for OVSS that selectively converts complementary thermal evidence into unseen-class semantic corrections. Specifically, class-relative cue selection evaluates RGB-IR semantic responses and calibrated thermal evidence relative to RGB predictions, generating class-relative correction requests through competitive selection. Text-contrast image rectification then translates these selected requests into semantic rectification directions through text contrasts, guiding evidence-gated feature updates to realize complementary thermal evidence for unseen-class recognition. Experiments on three benchmarks demonstrate gains of up to 4.755% in unseen-class mIoU and 15.730% in strict complementary conversion rate over existing fusion methods, while maintaining competitive fusion quality.
