# LIFT: Local Metric Alignment and Foundation-guided Propagation for Few-shot Depth Completion 

Xiang Li and Xiaojin Gong ∗

Abstract — Few-shot depth completion aims to recover dense metric depth from sparse measurements using only a small number of target-domain annotations, offering a favorable balance between annotation cost and model adaptation. We pro-pose LIFT, a framework that harnesses both depth and feature priors from a frozen pretrained monocular depth estimation (MDE) foundation model for few-shot depth completion. Specif-ically, LIFT first estimates local scale and shift parameters on a coarse grid and interpolates them into smooth, dense parameter fields, providing a spatially adaptive metric depth initialization. It then progressively refines this initialization through multi-scale depth propagation guided by foundation features. Finally, a lightweight residual decoder combines multi-level foundation features with propagation correction to refine the metric depth. Extensive experiments on NYUv2 and KITTI-DC demonstrate that our proposed method achieves state-of-the-art few-shot performance. The source code will soon be released. 

I. INTRODUCTION Depth completion aims to recover dense metric depth from sparse depth measurements with the aid of an RGB image. Compared with other 3D vision paradigms, such as Monoc-ular Depth Estimation (MDE) [1] and stereo vision [2], it can provide more reliable 3D geometry by leveraging direct depth observations, particularly in visually challenging scenarios. This advantage has made depth completion a fundamental task in autonomous driving [3] and robotics [4], where accurate metric geometry remains essential for down-stream perception and decision-making. With the advent of foundation models, particularly large-scale MDE foundation models [5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], depth completion has increasingly shifted from designing task-specific models from scratch toward leveraging pow-erful pretrained priors. Existing foundation model-driven approaches can be broadly categorized into training-based and test-time adaptation methods. Training-based meth-ods [11], [12], [13] typically incorporate pretrained MDE models into dedicated architectures and train them on large-scale synthetic datasets, such as Hypersim [14] and Virtual KITTI [15], [16], before transferring to real-world bench-marks. While effective, they require substantial training data and computational resources. In contrast, test-time adaptation methods [17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22] adapt pretrained MDE models to sparse depth observations during inference

However, their reliance on iterative optimization for each test sample introduces con-siderable inference overhead, limiting their practicality for real-time robotic applications. These limitations motivate a more efficient few-shot paradigm that leverages pretrained MDE priors for label-efficient depth completion, as explored by UniDC [23] and OASIS-DC [24]. Following this direction, we investigate how to better exploit pretrained foundation priors under limited supervision for accurate depth completion. Regard-less of whether these priors are leveraged through few-shot adaptation or training-based approaches, two key challenges remain: how to align dense relative depth with sparse metric measurements, and how to fully exploit the rich semantic and geometric priors encoded in monocular foundation models .For the first challenge, various alignment strategies have been developed based on global alignment [17], [12], [25], Poisson-based optimization [26], [24], and pixel-wise local fitting [13]. However, global alignment cannot capture spa-tially varying discrepancies, while Poisson-based optimiza-tion does not explicitly model local scale and shift varia-tions. Pixel-wise local fitting, in contrast, may suffer from unstable parameter estimation under sparse observations. To address these limitations, we propose a grid-based local met-ric alignment strategy, which estimates local transformation parameters on a coarse regular grid using nearby sparse measurements and interpolates them to the full resolution. This design captures spatially varying metric discrepancies while providing stable and efficient alignment. For the second challenge, the aligned depth prior alone may not fully exploit the rich information encoded in pre-trained foundation models. To this end, we introduce multi-scale foundation feature-guided depth propagation, which leverages multi-level features from a frozen MDE encoder to guide the propagation of reliable depth information within semantically and geometrically consistent regions. A lightweight residual decoder further refines the propa-gated depth to correct remaining fine-grained discrepancies. Together, these components effectively exploit the comple-mentary depth and feature priors of pretrained foundation models for accurate metric depth recovery under few-shot supervision. A. Monocular Depth Foundation Models 

Recent vision foundation models have significantly ad-vanced MDE through large-scale pretraining and powerful visual representations. Existing monocular depth foundation models can be broadly categorized into discriminative and generative paradigms. Discriminative models, such as the Depth Anything series [7], [8], [27], perform efficient dense regression, while generative models, such as Marigold [9] and DepthFM [10], leverage diffusion priors to recover finer geometric details. Despite their strong generalization, most foundation models predict scale-ambiguous relative depth, motivating metric MDE methods such as ZoeDepth [28] and Metric3D [6], [29]. However, recovering absolute scale from a single image remains challenging, often resulting in scale errors and geometric distortions in metric MDE. 

B. Foundation-driven Depth Completion 

Foundation-driven depth completion leverages pretrained MDE priors together with sparse sensor measurements for accurate depth reconstruction. Existing methods can be categorized into training-time and test-time paradigms. Training-time methods exploit pretrained MDE models as geometric priors and learn to align or fuse them with sparse measurements. Representative approaches, including PromptDA [11], Any2Full [25], Marigold-SSD [12], Pri-orDA [13], and LDCM [26], rely on substantial training with synthetic or real-world data. Several of these meth-ods demonstrate zero-shot generalization to unseen real-world domains, although domain gaps remain a challenge. In contrast, test-time methods, such as Marigold-DC [17], SteeredMarigold [18], Zero-DC [19], TestPromptDC [20], LRDA [21], and CAPA [22], adapt pretrained models during inference using scene-specific sparse observations, avoiding extensive training at the expense of additional inference overhead. To balance training cost and inference efficiency, few-shot methods, including UniDC [23], OASIS-DC [24], and our approach, adapt foundation-model priors with only a small number of target-domain RGB-depth pairs. 

C. Metric-Relative Depth Alignment 

Most MDE foundation models predict scale-ambiguous relative depth, making metric-relative depth alignment afundamental step in foundation-driven depth completion. Existing methods either estimate a single global affine trans-formation from sparse observations, as in Marigold-DC [17], Marigold-SSD [12], and Any2Full [25], or enforce local ge-ometric consistency through Poisson-based optimization, as in LDCM [26] and OASIS-DC [24]. While global alignment cannot capture spatially varying calibration errors, Poisson-based methods require additional optimization and primarily enforce gradient consistency. To enable spatially adaptive alignment, PriorDA [13] performs pixel-wise KNN-based lo-cal least-squares fitting, but independently estimated param-eters can fluctuate across neighboring pixels due to varying sparse neighborhoods. In contrast, we formulate alignment as a continuous parameter field, estimating local transformation parameters on a coarse spatial grid and densely reconstruct-ing them via bilinear interpolation, thereby preserving spatial adaptivity while improving consistency. 

D. Spatial Propagation 

Spatial propagation has been widely used in depth com-pletion to refine dense predictions through affinity-guided neighborhood aggregation. Existing methods mainly differ in how propagation affinities are obtained. CSPN [30], CSPN++ [31], NLSPN [32], and DySPN [33] learn prop-agation affinities using dedicated networks, with subsequent works further introducing adaptive neighborhoods or dy-namic propagation. UniDC [23] and OASIS-DC [24] further exploit hyperbolic representations to construct geometry-aware propagation affinities. Building on this line of work, our method computes local affinities from projected features of a frozen MDE encoder and uses them for depth aggrega-tion within a coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline. Building upon these foundation priors, we propose LIFT, a depth completion framework composed of three components: a Grid-based Local Alignment (GLA) module for spatially adaptive metric alignment, a Multi-scale Depth Propagation (MDP) module guided by MDE features to improve semantic consistency, and a lightweight residual decoder for recovering fine-grained details. A. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics 

We evaluate on NYUv2 [38] and KITTI-DC [39], repre-senting indoor and outdoor depth completion, respectively. Following UniDC [23] and OASIS-DC [24], we adopt the few-shot settings of 1, 10, and 100 annotated training frames, together with a 1-sequence setting. All training subsets are sampled from the official training split. For the 1-, 10-, and 100-shot settings, five subsets are independently sampled with different random seeds, and the reported results are averaged across the five runs. For the 1-sequence setting, we follow UniDC [23] and report the average performance of models trained on each of the ten training sequences. For NYUv2, images are resized to 240 × 320 and center-cropped to 228 × 304. Sparse inputs are generated by uni-formly sampling 500 valid depth points per image, with random resampling during training and fixed sampling during evaluation. Performance is evaluated on the standard 654 test images over depths from 0.1 to 10 m. For KITTI-DC, inputs are bottom-center cropped to 352 × 1216, while all valid projected LiDAR measurements are retained as sparse inputs. Evaluation is conducted on the official 1,000-image cropped validation set over depths from 0.1 to 80 m. We report Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE), both measured in meters. B. Implementation Details 

Our framework is implemented in PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU. We adopt the frozen Depth Anything V2 ViT-B/14 model [8] as the monocular depth foundation model to provide relative depth predictions and intermediate features. In the grid-based local alignment module, we use a grid stride of four pixels and select the k nearest sparse measurements, with k = 10 for NYUv2 and k = 20 for KITTI-DC. For multi-scale depth propagation, we use features from layers 12, 9, 6, and 3 of the frozen MDE encoder to guide four successive coarse-to-fine stages, respectively. The projected features are bilinearly re-sized to the corresponding propagation resolutions: (Ht ,Wt ),

(⌊H/4⌋, ⌊W /4⌋), (⌊H/2⌋, ⌊W /2⌋), and (H,W ), where Ht ×

Wt denotes the native encoder token-grid resolution and 

H ×W denotes the target depth resolution. For the training objective, we set λg to 0 .1 and λr to 5.0, 2.0, 0.2, and 0.2 for the 1-, 10-, 100-shot, and 1-sequence settings, respectively. The trainable modules are optimized using AdamW with a learning rate of 10 −4, a weight decay of 10 −3, and a batch size of 1. For 1-, 10-, and 100-shot training, we use 40, 500, and 1,500 iterations on NYUv2, and 40, 500, and 2,000 on KITTI-DC, respectively. LIFT has 0.763M trainable parameters and runs in 0.144 s per image on KITTI-DC using an RTX 3090 GPU. The frozen MDE model, GLA, and propagation with residual refinement take 0.069, 0.036, and 0.039 s, respectively. 

C. Comparison to State-of-the-Art Methods 

We compare LIFT with representative depth completion methods, including conventional propagation-based meth-ods [30], [32], [33], [36], [37], recent prompting-based methods [35], and state-of-the-art few-shot approaches [23], [24]. As shown in Table I, LIFT consistently achieves the best results on both indoor and outdoor datasets across all supervision settings. In particular, LIFT attains RMSE/MAE of 0 .1449 /0.0619 on NYUv2 and 1 .3706 /0.3750 on KITTI-DC in the challenging 1-shot setting, substantially outper-forming previous state-of-the-art methods. The consistent improvements across 1-, 10-, and 100-shot settings demon-strate the ability of the proposed framework to leverage pretrained depth priors under limited supervision. LIFT also achieves the best results under 1-sequence training, further demonstrating its robustness to both limited annotations and restricted scene diversity. Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 provide qualitative examples under 1-shot and 100-shot supervision. On KITTI-DC, the predic-tions preserve clear depth transitions between the road and surrounding structures, while maintaining complete object shapes for objects such as riders and cars and recovering thin structures such as street lamps. On NYUv2, the pre-dictions preserve the layout of furniture and capture depth transitions around doors and walls. The consistent overall geometry across the two supervision levels demonstrates the strong initialization provided by metric alignment, while the subsequent refinement modules further improve local struc-tures and fine-grained details, even with extremely limited annotations.

In this paper, we have presented LIFT, a few-shot depth completion framework that effectively leverages the priors of a pretrained monocular depth foundation model. LIFT first employs grid-based local alignment to convert relative depth predictions into spatially adaptive metric depth, and then progressively refines the aligned depth through multi-scale feature-guided propagation and a lightweight resid-ual decoder. This design enables the framework to exploit both the global depth prior and local metric observations while requiring only a small number of annotated training samples. Extensive experiments on KITTI-DC and NYUv2 demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed components and show that LIFT achieves competitive depth completion performance under extremely limited supervision.
